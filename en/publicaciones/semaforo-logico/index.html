🚦 Traffic Light with Logic Gates

A traffic light for a two-street intersection built with 4000-series chips: an NE555 provides the clock, a binary counter counts the seconds and a few gates decide which light is on at each moment. The complete project, from the schematic to the breadboard.

From the table to the equations

Q4 decides who has the right of way. It is 0 in states 0 to 7 and 1 in states 8 to 15: exactly half the cycle. That is why the two red lights need no calculation at all.

\( \text{Red}_A = Q_4 \) \( \text{Red}_B = \overline{Q_4} \)

F marks the end of each half. States 6, 7, 14 and 15 are the only ones with Q3 and Q2 both at 1. That is where yellow goes.

\( F = Q_3 \cdot Q_2 \)
\( \text{Yellow}_A = \overline{Q_4} \cdot F \) \( \text{Green}_A = \overline{Q_4} \cdot \overline{F} \)
\( \text{Yellow}_B = Q_4 \cdot F \) \( \text{Green}_B = Q_4 \cdot \overline{F} \)

Q1 does not appear in any equation: each phase lasts an even number of states, so the least significant bit is not needed to decide anything.

Check with Karnaugh

The green of street A is on in states 0 to 5.

Two groups of four come out: the blue box is \( \overline{Q_4}\,\overline{Q_3} \) and the green shading is \( \overline{Q_4}\,\overline{Q_2} \).

\( \overline{Q_4}\,\overline{Q_3} + \overline{Q_4}\,\overline{Q_2} = \overline{Q_4}\,(\overline{Q_3} + \overline{Q_2}) \)
\( = \overline{Q_4} \cdot \overline{Q_3\,Q_2} = \overline{Q_4} \cdot \overline{F} \)
The last step is De Morgan. Written this way, the green reuses the same F that the yellow needs and one OR gate is saved.

How to read it

  • U1, the NE555, oscillates as an astable: R1, R2 and C1 set a period of one second.
  • U2, the CD4520, advances one state on each rising edge of the clock. Pushbutton S1 returns it to zero; R3 holds the reset low while it is not pressed.
  • U3A and U4A build the auxiliary signals: \( \overline{Q_4} \) and F, and from F comes \( \overline{F} \) through U3B. All four run down the vertical bars.
  • Six AND gates, one per light, take from the bars what each equation calls for.
  • U6, the ULN2003A, turns on the LEDs: each output pulls the cathode of the corresponding LED to ground.

What is not drawn

  • The power supply of U3, U4 and U5: pin 14 to +9 V and pin 7 to ground on all three.
  • A 100 nF capacitor between +9 V and ground next to each IC, and a 100 µF electrolytic at the power input.
  • Unused inputs: those of U3C to U3F and of U5D go to ground. In CMOS a floating input takes any value and makes the IC draw current.
Why the reds go through a gate. Red A is Q4 and Red B is \( \overline{Q_4} \): they could go straight to the ULN2003A. But each ULN2003A input draws about 3 mA, and a CMOS output delivering that current has its voltage drop. Q4 and \( \overline{Q_4} \) also feed other gates, which need a clean high level. U5B and U5C, with their two inputs tied together, act as a buffer.

Bill of materials

The clock: NE555 as an astable

\( t_{\text{high}} = 0.693\,(R_1 + R_2)\,C_1 \) \( t_{\text{low}} = 0.693\,R_2\,C_1 \)
\( T = t_{\text{high}} + t_{\text{low}} = 0.693\,(R_1 + 2R_2)\,C_1 \) With R1 = 10 kΩ, R2 = 68 kΩ and C1 = 10 µF, T ≈ 1.01 s. The counter advances on rising edges, so it does not matter that the high time is longer than the low time.

All the traffic light times are multiples of T: green 6 T, yellow 2 T and red 8 T. If R2 is replaced by a 100 kΩ trimmer in series with a fixed 22 kΩ resistor, the full cycle can be adjusted without changing the proportion between the lights.

Building in stages

  1. Power supply. A 9 V battery or power supply with the 100 µF electrolytic. Measure before putting in any IC.
  2. Clock. Build the NE555 and test pin 3 with an LED and a 1 kΩ resistor to ground: it should blink once per second.
  3. Counter. Add the CD4520 and watch Q1 to Q4 with test LEDs: they should count in binary. Q4 changes every 8 seconds.
  4. Decoder. Add the CD4069 and the two CD4081s. With a multimeter, F should go high in states 6, 7, 14 and 15.
  5. Lights. Add the ULN2003A, the six 470 Ω resistors and the LEDs. With the reset pushbutton you always start from the green of street A.

Testing each stage before moving on to the next saves a lot of time: if something fails, the error is in the last thing that was added.

Care with the 4000 family

  • CMOS ICs are damaged by static electricity: touch something connected to ground before handling them and keep them in their tube or in conductive foam.
  • No input may be left floating. Those not in use go to ground or to +9 V.
  • Never apply a signal to an input while the IC is unpowered.
  • The operating voltage of the 4000 series runs from 3 to 15 V; the NE555, from 4.5 to 16 V. At 9 V both are comfortable.

Going further

Three improvements to tackle with the same circuit as a base:

  • All red. At real intersections there is an instant with both streets at red between one phase and the other, so that whoever went through on yellow can finish crossing.
  • Flashing yellow. A switch that, at night, turns off the greens and reds and makes the yellows blink with the clock.
  • Pedestrian pushbutton. Have the light stay green for street A until someone requests it from street B.
