    Legislation: the law around engineering

    Every signature an engineer puts down has legal consequences: a report, a contract, a drawing, an expert opinion. Law is not foreign to the profession; it is the framework in which the profession is practiced.

    Legal norm Technical standard Sources of law InfoLEG Professional practice

    01Why an engineer studies law

    Almost everything an engineer does at work has a legal side, even when it does not look like it. Accepting a quotation means entering into a contract. Signing the drawing of an installation means taking on a responsibility that can last for years. Hiring a technician means entering into an employment relationship with rules of its own. Testifying in a trial as an expert witness means acting as an auxiliary of the court. And setting up a company with a partner means choosing, knowingly or not, how much of your personal assets is exposed.

    This subject does not train lawyers. It trains engineers who know when a problem has a legal dimension, what questions to ask a lawyer and what mistakes to avoid before it becomes necessary to call one. The Engineering and Society page on the profession and professional ethics introduced the topic at the first level; here it is developed with the specific rules.

    Educational content, not legal advice

    These pages explain the general framework and cite the rules by their number. Laws change —labor law went through major reforms in 2024— and every real case has details that change the answer. For a specific situation, the current text of the law and a licensed lawyer have the final word.

    02Legal rules and technical standards

    Every day an engineer works with two kinds of rules that use the same word (“norma” in Spanish) and are very different things:

    Legal ruleTechnical standard
    ExamplesA statute, a decree, a municipal ordinanceAn IRAM, IEC or ISO standard; the AEA (Argentine Electrotechnical Association) wiring regulations for electrical installations
    Who issues itA state body with the competence to do soA standards body, by consensus among manufacturers, users and specialists
    Whether it is mandatoryBinds everyone from its official publicationIt is voluntary, unless a legal rule makes it mandatory
    If it is not complied withA penalty is provided forThere is no penalty of its own; but departing from it can show that due diligence was not exercised

    The last row is the one that matters most in practice. Even if a technical standard is not mandatory, in a trial over an accident it is used as the measure of what a prudent professional would have done. Following it protects; departing from it without a documented reason exposes you.

    03Where law comes from

    The sources of law are the places from which the rules that a judge applies arise:

    • Statute law, in the broad sense: any general rule issued by the competent authority, from the Constitution down to a ministry resolution. In the Argentine system it is the main source.
    • Custom: repeated practices that people consider binding. The Civil and Commercial Code (Código Civil y Comercial, CCyC) admits it when a statute refers to it or in situations that the statute does not regulate.
    • Case law (“jurisprudencia”): the way the courts decide cases. In Argentina one ruling does not bind other judges, but those of the Supreme Court strongly guide all of them.
    • Legal scholarship (“doctrina”): what jurists write. It is not binding, but it helps with interpretation.

    Article 8 of the Civil and Commercial Code lays down a principle worth keeping in mind: ignorance of the law is no excuse for failing to comply with it. No one can claim not to have known of a published rule.

    04Where to look up the rules

    SourceWhat it has
    Boletín Oficial de la República Argentina (the Official Gazette)The official publication: a national rule exists from the moment it appears there
    InfoLEGNational rules with their updated text and the rules that amended them
    SAIJ (Argentine Legal Information System)National and provincial legislation, case law and legal scholarship
    Provincial official gazettes and municipal compilations of ordinancesLocal rules: provincial professional-practice laws, building codes
    Professional councils and associationsThe professional-practice laws, codes of ethics and fee schedules of their jurisdiction

    A law cited from memory or copied from some random site may be out of date. The right habit is to look up the number of the rule in InfoLEG or SAIJ and read the updated text, which incorporates later amendments.

    07Activities

    Activity 1 · Look up a rule

    Look up Law 19,587 on occupational health and safety in InfoLEG. Note its date, which decree regulates it and which later rules amended it.

    Activity 2 · Technical standard versus legal rule

    Find out which legal rule makes the AEA wiring regulations for electrical installations in buildings mandatory in the jurisdiction where you live.

    Activity 3 · The legal side of a project

    Choose a real or imaginary electronics project and list all the acts with legal consequences that it involves, from the first quotation to after-sales service.

    08Common mistakes

    • Believing that law is a matter for lawyers. The decisions that create legal problems are made by the engineer, long before a lawyer gets involved.
    • Treating a technical standard as simply optional. It is voluntary, but it is the yardstick used to measure diligence.
    • Citing a law without checking its current text. Many have been amended several times.
    • Thinking that a verbal agreement is not binding. Most contracts do not require written form; the difficulty is proving them.

    09Self-assessment

    What is the difference between an IRAM standard and a statute as regards whether they are mandatory?

    A statute binds from its official publication; an IRAM standard is voluntary unless a legal rule makes it mandatory.

    What are the sources of law?

    Statute law, custom, case law and legal scholarship; in the Argentine system, statute law is the main one.

    Can you claim that you did not know about a published law?

    No: under Article 8 of the Civil and Commercial Code, ignorance of the law is no excuse.

    Where do you look up the updated text of a national law?

    In InfoLEG or SAIJ, which incorporate later amendments.
