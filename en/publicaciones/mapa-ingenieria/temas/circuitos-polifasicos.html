    Polyphase circuits

    When the neutral of a building is cut, some apartments receive more than 300 V and their appliances burn out. The phasor diagram of an unbalanced wye explains exactly why.

    Three-phase Wye and delta Neutral Millman Three-phase power

    01Three voltages 120° apart

    A three-phase generator has three windings spaced 120° apart in space, and produces three voltages of equal amplitude 120° apart in time. The entire electrical grid of the world is generated, transmitted and distributed this way, for three reasons that appear in this unit: with three conductors you transmit three times the power of a single phase with two conductors, the total instantaneous power is constant —and motors turn without vibrating— and two different voltages are obtained from the same grid.

    \[ \mathbf{V}_a = V_f\angle 0^\circ \qquad \mathbf{V}_b = V_f\angle{-120^\circ} \] \[ \mathbf{V}_c = V_f\angle 120^\circ \qquad \mathbf{V}_a + \mathbf{V}_b + \mathbf{V}_c = 0 \] The sum of the three voltages is zero at every instant: this is what allows the neutral to carry no current when the loads are equal.

    02Wye and delta

    The three phases can be connected in two ways. In wye (Y) each load goes between one phase and the common neutral; in delta (Δ), between two phases. The voltages between phases —line voltages— are \( \sqrt3 \) times the phase voltages:

    \[ V_L = \sqrt3\,V_f \qquad P = \sqrt3\,V_L I_L \cos\varphi \] In Argentina the low-voltage grid is 3 × 380/220 V, 50 Hz: 220 V between each phase and neutral, 380 V between phases (the current standard takes it to 400/230 V). A house receives one phase and the neutral; a workshop or a building, all three.

    03The neutral and imbalance

    If the three loads of a wye are equal, their currents add up to zero and the neutral carries nothing. But residential consumption is never balanced: in a building, each apartment is on a different phase and each one draws what it wants. The neutral then carries the sum of the three currents, and it also performs a function that is noticed only when it is missing: it holds the common point of the loads at zero volts.

    If the neutral is cut, the common point of the loads is left floating and shifts toward the most heavily loaded phase. Its voltage comes directly from Millman’s theorem from the previous topic:

    \[ \mathbf{V}_{N'} = \frac{\mathbf{V}_a/Z_a + \mathbf{V}_b/Z_b + \mathbf{V}_c/Z_c}{1/Z_a + 1/Z_b + 1/Z_c} \] Each load is subjected to \( \mathbf{V}_x - \mathbf{V}_{N'} \) instead of to its phase voltage. The phase with the least consumption is the one that receives the highest voltage.

    A frequent accident

    A neutral cut at the service entrance of a building is one of the most common causes of appliances burned out en masse: the apartments on the least loaded phase suddenly receive more than 300 V. That is why the regulations prohibit installing fuses or switches that interrupt only the neutral, and why overvoltage protection devices exist that disconnect the installation when the voltage goes out of range.

    04Constant power

    In a single phase the instantaneous power oscillates at twice the grid frequency and passes through zero a hundred times per second. In a balanced three-phase system, the three oscillations are out of phase and their sum is exactly constant. A three-phase motor then receives a constant torque and runs smoothly, whereas a single-phase one vibrates at twice the grid frequency. It is one of the reasons why industry uses three-phase even when the power could be transmitted in single-phase.

    07Common mistakes

    • Adding the phase voltages as plain numbers. They are phasors at 120°: the line voltage is \( \sqrt3 \) times the phase voltage, not double.
    • Believing the neutral “carries nothing.” Only with perfectly balanced loads and no harmonics.
    • Protecting the neutral with its own fuse. If only the neutral opens, the common point floats and overvoltages appear.
    • Using \( P = 3VI\cos\varphi \) with line voltages. With line values it is \( \sqrt3\,V_L I_L\cos\varphi \).
    • Thinking a neutral cut only switches things off. The voltage on the least loaded phase rises.

    08Self-assessment

    What is the line voltage of a grid with a 220 V phase voltage?

    \( \sqrt3 \times 220 \approx 380 \) V.

    When does no current flow through the neutral?

    When the three loads are equal and linear: their currents add up to zero.

    What happens to the least loaded phase if the neutral is cut?

    The common point shifts toward the most loaded phase and the least loaded one receives a voltage higher than nominal.

    Why does a three-phase motor run more smoothly than a single-phase one?

    Because the instantaneous power of a balanced three-phase system is constant.

    Which theorem gives the voltage of the common point with the neutral cut?

    Millman’s.
