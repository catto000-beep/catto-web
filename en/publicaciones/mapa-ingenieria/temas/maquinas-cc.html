    DC machines and brushless motors

    A DC motor is most efficient when only lightly loaded: the maximum efficiency of a small motor is at one tenth of its stall current, far from the maximum torque.

    Back EMF Excitation H-bridge Maximum efficiency BLDC

    01The easiest motor to control

    In a DC motor, the rotor conductors carry current inside the stator field and experience a force. A segmented commutator with brushes reverses the current in each coil just as it passes from one pole to the other, so that the torque always pushes in the same direction. As they turn, the same conductors generate a back EMF proportional to the speed. Two equations describe the whole motor:

    \[ V = R\,I + k\,\omega \] \[ T = k\,I \] In steady state. The same constant \( k \) links the back EMF to the speed (V·s/rad) and the torque to the current (N·m/A). The voltage sets the speed; the load sets the current.

    This simplicity made the DC motor the favorite for speed and position control for a century, and it remains in millions of small applications: toys, battery-powered tools, windshield wipers.

    03Curves, control and efficiency

    Solving, the speed falls in a straight line with torque, from the no-load speed \( V/k \) to zero at the stall torque \( kV/R \). Changing the voltage shifts the line in parallel: that is why the speed is controlled with an H-bridge that applies the supply voltage modulated by pulse width, with an average value proportional to the duty cycle. The efficiency depends on the operating point and has a maximum:

    \[ \eta_{max} = \left(1 - \sqrt{\frac{I_0}{I_{stall}}}\right)^2 \] \( I_0 \) is the no-load current, which overcomes friction, and \( I_{stall} = V/R \) the current with the rotor locked. The maximum occurs at \( I = \sqrt{I_0\, I_{stall}} \), well before the stall torque: a DC motor is most efficient when only lightly loaded.

    04Brushless motors

    Brushes wear out, produce sparks and interference, and limit the speed. The brushless motor (BLDC) turns the construction inside out: the magnets go on the rotor and the windings on the stator, and the commutation is done by an electronic circuit that knows the rotor position from Hall-effect sensors or from the back EMF of the phase that is not conducting. With six-step commutation, the behavior is that of a DC motor; with vector control and sinusoidal currents (permanent-magnet synchronous motor), the torque is smoother and the efficiency higher.

    BrushedBrushless
    ControlA DC voltage or an H-bridgeA three-phase inverter with position feedback
    Service lifeLimited by the brushesLimited by the bearings
    Typical efficiency60 to 80%85 to 95%

    05In the laboratory

    Activity 1 · Measuring k and R

    Measure the winding resistance with the rotor locked and the constant k as the generated voltage divided by the speed, spinning the motor with another one.

    Activity 2 · PWM control

    Control a motor with an H-bridge and a microcontroller, and measure the speed as a function of duty cycle with and without load.

    Activity 3 · A BLDC

    Observe with the oscilloscope the three phases and the Hall signals of a computer fan or a drone motor with its controller.

    06Common mistakes

    • Sizing the H-bridge for the rated current. At startup the stall current flows, V/R.
    • Running a series motor without load. The speed runs away and can destroy it.
    • Forgetting the diode or the freewheeling path. The winding inductance generates overvoltages when switching off.
    • Braking a DC motor abruptly. It acts as a generator and returns energy to the supply.

    07Self-assessment

    What no-load speed does a motor with k = 0.05 V·s/rad have at 12 V?

    240 rad/s, about 2290 rpm.

    What stall torque does the same motor give with R = 1.2 Ω?

    \( kV/R = 0.05 \cdot 12/1.2 = 0.5 \) N·m, with 10 A.

    What maximum efficiency does it have if the no-load current is 0.1 A?

    \( (1 - \sqrt{0.1/10})^2 = 0.81 \): 81%.

    How does a BLDC know when to commutate?

    From the rotor position, measured with Hall sensors or estimated from the back EMF.
