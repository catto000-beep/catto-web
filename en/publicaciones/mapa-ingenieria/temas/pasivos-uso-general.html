    General-purpose passive components

    A divider of two 1 % resistors can be off by up to 50 mV on 5 V, but in 4000 simulated dividers the standard deviation is 20 mV. The worst case is safe; the statistics are realistic.

    E series Tolerance Temperature coefficient Ceramic dielectrics Electrolytic life

    01Resistors

    The ideal model of passive components was covered in passive components, in Circuit Theory I; here what matters is real components and how they are chosen. In a resistor what matters is the value, the tolerance, the temperature coefficient (TCR, in ppm/K), the power rating, the maximum voltage, the noise and the long-term stability:

    TechnologyCharacteristics
    Carbon filmCheap, 5 %, high and negative TCR; falling out of use
    Metal film1 % or better, TCR of 15 to 100 ppm/K, low noise
    Thick film (SMD chip)The most widely used; 1 % to 5 %, TCR of 100 to 200 ppm/K
    Thin film (SMD chip)Precision of 0.1 %, TCR of 5 to 25 ppm/K
    WirewoundPower and precision; inductive
    Metal foilMaximum stability, TCR close to 1 ppm/K

    Values follow the standard series of IEC 60063, with logarithmic steps: E12 (10 %) has 12 values per decade, E24 (5 %) 24 and E96 (1 %) 96. The rated power holds up to a temperature, typically 70 °C, and above it is reduced linearly. SMD chips are designated by their size in inches: 0402, 0603, 0805, 1206.

    02Capacitors

    The dielectric decides almost everything in a capacitor: the capacitance per volume, the stability, the losses and the life.

    TypeCharacteristics
    Class 1 ceramic (C0G, NP0)Stable: 0 ± 30 ppm/K, no voltage effect; up to tens of nF
    Class 2 ceramic (X7R, X5R)X7R varies ±15 % between −55 and 125 °C, and loses capacitance with the applied DC voltage
    Y5V, Z5U ceramicA lot of capacitance, but varies from +22 to −82 % with temperature
    Film (polypropylene, polyester)Low losses and self-healing; filters, audio, snubber networks
    Aluminum electrolyticA lot of cheap capacitance; polarized, high ESR, limited life
    Tantalum and polymerCompact and low ESR; tantalum ones fail short-circuited if overstressed

    The electrolyte of an aluminum capacitor slowly evaporates, and its useful life depends on temperature. As a rule of thumb, it doubles for every 10 °C below the rated temperature:

    \[ L = L_0 \cdot 2^{(T_0 - T)/10} \] A 2000-hour capacitor at 105 °C operating at 65 °C lasts about 32,000 hours. The ripple current heats it from the inside and has to be added. It is often the component that limits the life of a power supply.

    03Inductors and transformers

    A commercial inductor is specified by its inductance, its DC resistance, its saturation current, where the inductance drops by a given percentage, its thermal current and its self-resonant frequency, above which it behaves like a capacitor. Core materials were covered in magnetic materials. General-purpose transformers, mains ones, are chosen for their rating in VA, their voltages, their regulation between no load and full load, their insulation class and their electrical safety.

    04Tolerances: worst case and statistics

    The output of a circuit depends on all of its components, and each has a tolerance and drifts with temperature. There are two ways to analyze it: the worst case, which combines all the extremes and guarantees compliance, but oversizes; and the statistical analysis or Monte Carlo, which draws thousands of circuits and estimates what fraction complies.

    \[ V_o = V_i\,\frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2} \] In a divider, the worst case is \( R_2 \) at its maximum and \( R_1 \) at its minimum. If both resistors come from the same integrated network, their variations track each other and the ratio is much more stable than each value.

    05In the lab

    Activity 1 · Real distribution

    Measure fifty resistors from the same lot and plot the histogram. Does it fill the whole tolerance or is it concentrated?

    Activity 2 · Ceramic capacitors

    Measure a 10 μF X7R capacitor with the LCR meter applying different bias voltages and compare with the manufacturer's curve.

    Activity 3 · Drift

    Heat a divider with a heat gun and measure the variation of its output, with discrete resistors and with an integrated network.

    06Common mistakes

    • Using the nominal value of an X5R or X7R ceramic. With the applied DC voltage it can lose more than half of its capacitance.
    • Forgetting power derating. A 1/4 W resistor does not dissipate 1/4 W inside a hot enclosure.
    • Designing with the tolerance alone. Drift with temperature and aging add to it.
    • Placing an electrolytic next to the heat sink. Every 10 °C more takes away half of its life.

    07Self-assessment

    How many values per decade does the E24 series have?

    Twenty-four.

    A 1000 h electrolytic at 105 °C operates at 65 °C. How long does it last, according to the rule of thumb?

    About 16,000 hours.

    What is the worst-case error of a 1:2 divider with 1 % resistors?

    ±1 % of the output: ±50 mV on 5 V.

    What ceramic capacitor is used in a precision oscillator or filter?

    A class 1 one, C0G or NP0.
