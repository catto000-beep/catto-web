    Discrete-time signals

    A 7 kHz sinusoid sampled at 8 kHz gives exactly the same samples as a 1 kHz one. In discrete time, frequency only makes sense between zero and half the sampling frequency.

    Sequences Normalized frequency Difference equation z-plane DFT

    01From signal to sequence

    When a converter samples a signal every \( T \) seconds, what is left is a sequence of numbers \( x[n] = x(nT) \), defined only for integer \( n \). What happens between samples is lost, and by the sampling theorem that does not matter if the signal had no frequencies above \( f_s/2 \). All digital processing works on sequences, and its tools are the same as those of circuits, in discrete form: convolution, difference equations and the Z transform in place of the Laplace transform. The presentation of the two kinds of signal is in continuous-time and discrete-time signals.

    02Frequency in discrete time

    A sampled sinusoid is \( x[n] = \cos(\Omega n) \), with \( \Omega = 2\pi f / f_s \) the normalized frequency in radians per sample. Two properties distinguish it from the continuous one:

    • It is periodic in Ω with period 2π. \( \cos((\Omega + 2\pi) n) = \cos(\Omega n) \) for every integer \( n \): the frequencies \( \Omega \) and \( \Omega + 2\pi \) are indistinguishable. It is aliasing seen from the samples. Only the interval from 0 to π makes sense, which corresponds to 0 up to \( f_s/2 \).
    • It is not always periodic in n. It is only so if \( \Omega / 2\pi \) is a rational number.

    03Discrete systems

    A linear time-invariant discrete system is described by its impulse response \( h[n] \), and the output is the discrete convolution \( y[n] = \sum_k h[k]\, x[n - k] \). In practice almost all of them are written as a difference equation, which says how to compute each output sample from the previous inputs and outputs:

    \[ y[n] = \sum_{k=0}^{M} b_k\, x[n-k] - \sum_{k=1}^{N} a_k\, y[n-k] \] If there are no terms with previous outputs (\( N = 0 \)), the impulse response lasts \( M + 1 \) samples: it is an FIR system. If there are, the feedback makes it infinite: it is an IIR system.

    04The Z transform and the z-plane

    The Z transform turns the delay of one sample into a product by \( z^{-1} \), and the difference equation into a rational transfer function \( H(z) \). The z-plane plays the role of the s-plane, related by \( z = e^{sT} \):

    In the s-planeIn the z-plane
    Imaginary axis \( j\omega \)Unit circle \( |z| = 1 \)
    Left half-plane (stable)Inside the unit circle
    Pole at \( -\sigma \pm j\omega \)Pole at \( r e^{\pm j\Omega} \), with \( r = e^{-\sigma T} \) and \( \Omega = \omega T \)

    The frequency response is \( H(z) \) evaluated on the circle, \( z = e^{j\Omega} \). A pole near the circle produces a peak at that frequency; a zero on it, a null.

    05The discrete Fourier transform

    To compute the spectrum of a finite-length sequence on a computer, the DFT is used, which gives \( N \) values of the frequency response equally spaced around the unit circle. The FFT is an algorithm that computes it with \( N \log_2 N \) operations instead of \( N^2 \): for 4096 points, about 50,000 instead of 16 million. It is the tool used by the labs on converters to measure the signal-to-noise ratio.

    07Common mistakes

    • Confusing normalized frequency with analog frequency. Ω = π corresponds to \( f_s/2 \), not to 1 Hz.
    • Forgetting that feedback can make the system unstable. A single pole outside the unit circle is enough.
    • Expecting every sampled sinusoid to be periodic. Only if Ω/2π is rational.
    • Taking the DFT as the continuous spectrum. It is samples of it, with a resolution of \( f_s/N \).

    08Self-assessment

    What normalized frequency does a 1 kHz sinusoid sampled at 8 kHz have?

    \( \Omega = 2\pi/8 = \pi/4 \) radians per sample.

    Is a system with poles at 0.9 e±j0.5 stable?

    Yes: they are inside the unit circle.

    Is y = 0.5x[n] + 0.5x[n−1] FIR or IIR?

    FIR: it does not use previous outputs; its impulse response has two samples.

    What frequency resolution does a 1024-point DFT give at 48 kHz?

    \( 48\,000/1024 \approx 46.9 \) Hz.
