    Wave motion

    A signal traveling along a cable is a wave, with its speed, its wavelength and its reflections, and it obeys the same equation as a shaken string. All of transmission-line theory comes from there.

    Wave equation Superposition Beats Standing wave SWR

    01What travels in a wave

    A wave carries energy and momentum, not matter. The particles of the medium oscillate around their position and stay where they were; what moves forward is the disturbance. This holds for sound, for a string and also for the signals in a cable.

    \[ \frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial x^2} = \frac{1}{v^2}\frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial t^2} \qquad\qquad y(x,t) = A\sin(kx - \omega t + \varphi) \] The wave equation and its harmonic solution. \( k = 2\pi/\lambda \) is the wavenumber and \( \omega = 2\pi f \) the angular frequency; the minus sign between the two terms indicates travel toward \( +x \), and the plus sign, toward \( -x \).
    \[ v = \lambda f \] The most used relation in all of wave physics. The speed is set by the medium; the frequency, by the source. That is why, when passing from one medium to another, the wavelength changes and the frequency never does.

    02Superposition

    When two waves coincide at a point, the displacements add. It is a principle of deceptive simplicity: from it come interference, beats, standing waves and, at bottom, the whole of Fourier analysis.

    Lab · superposition of two waves

    Two waves and their sum. Change the frequency of the second, its amplitude and its phase, and try reversing its direction of travel: that is where the standing wave appears.

    The three cases to recognize

    With equal frequency and the same direction, the sum is another wave of the same frequency whose amplitude depends on the phase shift: maximum in phase, zero in antiphase. With slightly different frequencies a beat appears, a slow envelope of frequency \( |f_1 - f_2| \), which is what you hear when tuning a guitar and what makes a superheterodyne receiver work. And with opposite directions, the wave stops advancing: it becomes standing, with fixed nodes where the amplitude is always zero.

    03Standing waves and reflection

    A wave that reaches the end of its medium is reflected. If the end is fixed, it comes back inverted; if it is free, it comes back upright. The superposition of the incident wave with the reflected one gives the standing wave.

    \[ \lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n} \qquad\qquad f_n = n\,\frac{v}{2L} \] Modes of a string fixed at both ends. Only certain frequencies “fit” in the available length, and that is the reason a stringed instrument sounds in tune and not like noise.

    The same phenomenon, in a cable carrying a signal, is a problem. If the end is not terminated with the correct impedance, part of the signal comes back and superimposes on the one arriving: steps appear, overshoots and —in radio frequency— a standing wave along the line. The measure of how much comes back is the standing wave ratio:

    \[ \Gamma = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0} \qquad\qquad \mathrm{SWR} = \frac{1 + |\Gamma|}{1 - |\Gamma|} \] With the load equal to the characteristic impedance, \( \Gamma = 0 \) and the SWR is 1: nothing comes back. With the end open or short-circuited everything comes back, and the SWR goes to infinity.
    The Doppler effect

    When the source and the observer approach each other, the perceived frequency rises; as they move apart, it drops. It is the phenomenon of the sound of a passing siren, and also the principle of the speed radar, which measures the shift of the wave bouncing off a car. In electronics it also appears as a problem in mobile communications: the Doppler shift of a moving device has to be compensated in the receiver.

    04In the lab

    Activity 1 · Modes of a string

    With a generator and an electromagnetic driver on a taut string, find the frequencies that produce one, two and three antinodes. Verify the proportionality with \( n \) and obtain the wave speed; compare it with \( \sqrt{T/\mu} \).

    Activity 2 · Audible beats

    With two audio generators near 440 Hz and a speaker, separate them little by little and count the beats per second. Verify that they match the difference in frequencies, and observe the envelope on the oscilloscope.

    Activity 3 · Reflection in a cable

    Send a narrow pulse down a long coaxial cable and observe the end with the oscilloscope, first open, then short-circuited and finally terminated with 50 Ω. Identify the sign of each reflection and verify that with the correct termination it disappears.

    05Common mistakes

    • Believing that the wave carries matter. It carries energy; the medium oscillates and stays.
    • Thinking that the frequency changes when the medium changes. It is set by the source; what changes is \( \lambda \).
    • Confusing the speed of the wave with the speed of the particles of the medium. They are different things and very different values.
    • Believing that there is no energy in a standing wave. There is: what there is not is net transport.
    • Leaving a signal cable unterminated. What is not absorbed comes back, and superimposes on what arrives.
    • Adding intensities instead of amplitudes. What superimposes is the amplitudes; the intensity goes with the square.

    06Self-assessment

    What does a wave carry?

    Energy and momentum. The medium oscillates around its position and does not move along.

    When passing to another medium, what changes?

    The speed and the wavelength. The frequency is set by the source and does not change.

    What is the frequency of a beat?

    The difference between the two frequencies that are superimposed.

    When does a standing wave appear?

    When two equal waves traveling in opposite directions are superimposed, almost always by reflection at an end.

    What is the SWR of a properly terminated line?

    One: with \( Z_L = Z_0 \) the reflection coefficient is zero and nothing comes back.

    What happens on reflection at a fixed end?

    The wave comes back inverted. At a free end it comes back upright.
