    Legal rights and duties of the professional

    The signature both authorizes and binds: the same act that gives legal value to a drawing makes the signer responsible for what it says.

    Professional signature Fees Duty to inform Professional secrecy Intellectual property

    01The signature both authorizes and binds

    The professional license gives the engineer a set of rights that other people do not have, and in return imposes more demanding duties. Both are concentrated in the same act: the signature. A drawing, a certificate or a report signed by a licensed professional has legal value because someone qualified answers for it. The unit on professional practice explained how the license is obtained; this one explains what comes with it.

    02The professional’s rights

    RightBasis and scope
    Carrying out the reserved activitiesWith exclusivity as against anyone who does not hold the degree; the professional can report illegal practice
    Charging feesThose agreed; if none were agreed, those set by the judge using the fee schedules as a reference
    Intellectual property in their workLaw 11,723 protects drawings, designs, reports and software: the client pays to use them for the purpose commissioned, not to reproduce them in other works, unless otherwise agreed
    Technical autonomyTo decide according to their professional judgment and to refuse to sign what they consider unsafe or incorrect, even when working as an employee
    Defense before the disciplinary tribunalA procedure with the right to respond and to present evidence before any penalty, by virtue of the due process guarantee

    03Legal duties

    The professional’s duties are not found in a single law: they come together from the professional-practice law of each jurisdiction, the Civil and Commercial Code, the Criminal Code and technical standards.

    DutyWhere it comes from
    Acting only within your own competence and with a current licenseThe professional-practice law of the jurisdiction
    Acting with prudence and full knowledge of the factsArticle 1725 of the Civil and Commercial Code: the greater that duty, the greater the diligence required, and a professional is held to that of an expert
    Preventing harmArticle 1710: every person must avoid causing unjustified harm and take reasonable measures to prevent it from occurring
    Informing the clientArticle 1256: the essential aspects of the work, the risks, the limits of what can be guaranteed
    Complying with technical and regulatory standardsThose that are mandatory by legal rule, and the others as a measure of good practice
    Keeping professional secrecyCode of ethics and Article 156 of the Criminal Code
    Keeping the documentationTo be able to show what was done and by what criteria if there is a claim years later
    The expert standard

    Article 1725 is the one that weighs most in a trial: a professional is not judged like an ordinary person, but as someone who knew, or should have known, what a good engineer knows. “I didn’t know that the regulations required that” is not a defense: it is a confession of a breach.

    04Obligation of means or obligation of result

    Article 1768 of the Civil and Commercial Code regulates the activity of the liberal professions: their liability is governed by the rules on obligations to do and is, in general, fault-based —it must be proven that the professional acted with fault—, unless a specific result was promised. The difference is the one between a work contract and a service contract:

    • If the engineer undertook to advise on a technology, they have fulfilled the obligation if they advised diligently, even if the technology later chosen does not perform as expected.
    • If they undertook to deliver a working system with certain performance, they are liable if it does not work, unless they prove an external cause.

    That is why it matters so much how the scope of a job is written: promising results where you can only guarantee diligence transforms the liability.

    05The professional and consumer protection

    Law 24,240 on consumer protection expressly excludes the services of liberal professionals who require a university degree and a license: the relationship with the client is governed by the Civil and Commercial Code and by the oversight of the professional association. But the exclusion does not extend to the advertising that the professional does for their services, which must comply with that law. And when an engineer also manufactures or sells products, that activity is fully covered.

    08Common mistakes

    • Believing that the client buys the drawings forever. Unless otherwise agreed, the client buys their use for the purpose commissioned.
    • Promising results in an advisory job. It turns an obligation of means into an obligation of result.
    • Throwing away the project documentation when it is finished. It is the only proof of how the work was done.
    • Signing out of obedience to the employer. The responsibility remains with whoever signs.

    09Self-assessment

    What does Article 1725 of the Civil and Commercial Code establish?

    That the greater the duty to act with prudence and full knowledge of the facts, the greater the diligence required: a professional is judged as an expert.

    Is the liability of a liberal professional strict or fault-based?

    In general fault-based, unless a specific result was promised (Article 1768).

    Does the consumer protection law apply to the services of a licensed engineer?

    No, except as regards the advertising of those services.

    What does Law 11,723 protect in an engineer’s work?

    Their drawings, designs, reports and software as intellectual works.
