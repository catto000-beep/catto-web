    Polarization

    Two crossed polarizers let nothing through. Put a third one between them and 12.5% gets through, and adding a filter increases what passes because a polarizer does not only filter: it reorients.

    Malus's law Circular polarization Brewster Birefringence LCD

    01The direction in which the field oscillates

    The fact that the electromagnetic wave is transverse leaves a freedom that longitudinal waves do not have: the electric field can oscillate in any direction in the plane perpendicular to the propagation. That direction is the polarization, and sound does not have it because it oscillates in the same direction in which it travels.

    StateWhat the field doesHow it is achieved
    LinearAlways oscillates in the same planeA polarizer, or a single-element antenna
    CircularRotates, describing a circleTwo equal components shifted by 90°
    EllipticalRotates, describing an ellipseThe general case: any amplitudes or phase shift
    UnpolarizedChanges randomly all the timeAny thermal source: the Sun, a lamp

    The last row is the natural state of almost all light: a lamp emits photons from atoms that are not coordinated with each other, and the result averages over all directions. Polarizing it is filtering, and that is why it always costs energy.

    Circular polarization is not a detail

    In satellite communications circular polarization is used because the relative orientation between the satellite and the ground antenna changes, and with linear polarization it would have to be tracked. Also a right-hand circular wave and a left-hand one are independent: two channels can be transmitted on the same frequency. And in the ionosphere, Faraday rotation turns the plane of a linear wave unpredictably, something that does not affect a circular one.

    02Malus’s law

    A polarizer lets through only the component of the field parallel to its axis. Since the intensity goes with the square of the field, the fraction that passes is:

    \[ I = I_0\cos^2\theta \] With \( \theta \) the angle between the incoming polarization and the axis of the polarizer. If the light enters unpolarized, the first polarizer lets through exactly half, whatever its orientation: it is the average of \( \cos^2 \) over all angles.
    Lab · cascaded polarizers

    Three polarizers with adjustable axes. Start with the first and the third crossed at 90°: nothing gets through. Then insert the middle one at 45° and see what happens.

    Why the middle one lets light through

    It seems contradictory that adding a filter could increase what gets through, and it is not. The middle polarizer does not merely filter: it reorients. What comes out of it is polarized at 45°, and that direction does have a component along the axis of the third. Without it, what reaches the third is perpendicular to its axis and has none. It is the cleanest demonstration that a polarizer projects a vector, rather than simply letting through “the light that was already there.”

    03Polarizing without a polarizer

    Reflection polarizes. When light strikes a transparent surface at the Brewster angle, what is reflected is completely polarized in the plane parallel to the surface:

    \[ \tan\theta_B = \frac{n_2}{n_1} \] For air and glass it gives about 56°, and for air and water, 53°. That is the basis of polarized sunglasses: the glare from asphalt or water is mostly horizontal, so a vertical filter removes it and lets the rest through.

    Brewster reflectionPolarizes what is reflectedPolarized sunglasses, laser windows
    Rayleigh scatteringPolarizes the sky at 90° from the SunPhotography filters; orientation of some insects
    BirefringenceTwo indices depending on the polarizationQuarter-wave plates, calcite

    The screen you are looking at

    An LCD is pure polarization. Behind it there is a white lamp, in front two crossed polarizers —which by themselves would give black— and in between a layer of liquid crystal whose molecules rotate the plane of polarization. With no voltage, the layer rotates it 90° and the light passes through both polarizers: pixel on. With voltage, the molecules align, stop rotating anything and the second polarizer blocks: pixel off. Each pixel is a Malus experiment, and that is why the screen of a laptop goes dark when viewed through rotated polarized sunglasses.

    04In the lab

    Activity 1 · Verifying Malus

    With two polarizers, a stable source and a photodiode with an amplifier, measure the intensity every ten degrees of rotation of the second. Plot it against \( \cos^2\theta \) and verify that it gives a straight line.

    Activity 2 · The third one in the middle

    Cross two polarizers until extinction and measure. Insert a third between them and sweep it from 0 to 90°, measuring. Locate the maximum and compare it with the 12.5 % predicted by theory.

    Activity 3 · Brewster angle

    Reflect a beam off a glass plate while varying the angle and observe the reflection through a polarizer. Find the extinction angle and from it obtain the refractive index of the glass.

    05Common mistakes

    • Believing that a polarizer “lets through the light that was already in its direction.” It projects: it transmits the component.
    • Expecting the first one to let through more than half. With unpolarized light it is always 50 %, in any orientation.
    • Thinking that adding a filter cannot increase what gets through. The middle one proves otherwise.
    • Using Malus with unpolarized light. Malus holds from the second polarizer onward.
    • Confusing circular polarization with unpolarized light. Circular is perfectly defined; it just rotates.
    • Forgetting that polarization is transverse. Sound is not polarized: it oscillates in the direction in which it travels.

    06Self-assessment

    How much light passes through the first polarizer?

    Half, if it enters unpolarized, whatever its orientation.

    What fraction passes through three polarizers at 0°, 45° and 90°?

    One eighth: \( \tfrac12 \times \cos^2 45° \times \cos^2 45° = 12.5\ \% \) of the light that entered.

    Why does the middle one let light through if the other two are crossed?

    Because it reorients: what comes out of it is at 45°, and that direction does have a component along the axis of the third.

    What is the Brewster angle?

    The angle of incidence at which the reflected light is totally polarized. Its tangent is the ratio of the indices.

    Why do satellites use circular polarization?

    Because it does not depend on the relative orientation, and the ionosphere rotates the plane of a linear wave unpredictably.

    How does an LCD pixel work?

    Two crossed polarizers with liquid crystal in between: with no voltage it rotates the polarization 90° and light passes; with voltage it does not rotate it and the second polarizer blocks it.
