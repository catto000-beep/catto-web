    Magnetic materials

    Without an air gap, a 30-turn ferrite inductor has 2.8 mH but saturates at 0.31 A. With half a millimeter of air gap it drops to 0.2 mH and withstands 4.3 A: the energy is stored in the air.

    01Magnetization and hysteresis

    A ferromagnetic material responds to the field \( H \) with a flux density \( B \) much greater than that of vacuum, but not linearly: the magnetization curve rises with a high slope, the permeability, and flattens out at saturation, where the material can no longer align more domains. When a cycle is traversed, the downward curve does not coincide with the upward one: this is the hysteresis loop, with a remanence \( B_r \) and a coercive field \( H_c \). The physical origin was covered in magnetic properties of matter.

    \[ B = \mu_0\,\mu_r\,H \] The relative permeability \( \mu_r \) is not constant: it depends on the field, the temperature and the frequency, and falls to almost 1 at saturation. Soft materials have narrow loops and are used in cores; hard ones, wide loops, and are used as magnets.

    Ferrites are oxide ceramics: their high resistivity almost eliminates eddy currents and so they work at high frequency, but they saturate at less than half the flux density of iron and their saturation drops with temperature. All lose their magnetism above the Curie temperature.

    Permanent magnets are chosen for their remanence, their coercive field and their maximum energy product: barium or strontium ferrite, cheap; AlNiCo, stable with temperature; and neodymium–iron–boron and samarium–cobalt, the most powerful.

    03Core losses

    A core in AC dissipates through hysteresis, proportional to the area of the loop and to the frequency, and through eddy currents, proportional to the square of the frequency, which are reduced by laminating the iron or using high-resistivity materials. Manufacturers give the losses per unit volume, which are fitted with the Steinmetz equation:

    \[ P_v = k\,f^{\alpha}\,\hat{B}^{\beta} \] For ferrites, \( \alpha \) is between 1 and 2 and \( \beta \) between 2 and 3, with \( k \), \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) valid over a range of frequency and flux density. Since the losses grow faster than the flux density, at high frequency one works far from saturation.

    04The air gap

    An inductor that carries DC current, or the transformer of a flyback supply, needs to store energy. A closed core of high permeability has a lot of inductance, but saturates with very little current. An air gap of length \( \ell_g \) adds a series reluctance that dominates the magnetic circuit:

    \[ L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A_e}{\ell_g + \ell_e / \mu_r} \] \[ I_{sat} = \frac{B_{max}\,(\ell_g + \ell_e / \mu_r)}{\mu_0 N} \] \( A_e \) is the effective cross-section and \( \ell_e \) the effective magnetic length of the core. The maximum energy, \( \tfrac12 L I_{sat}^2 \), grows with the air gap: almost all of it is stored in the air. The flux that fringes at the edges of the gap raises the actual inductance somewhat.
    Lab · ferrite inductor with an air gap

    A ferrite E core with Ae = 97 mm², ℓe = 79 mm and μr = 2000, designed for a maximum flux density of 0.3 T. As a function of the air gap, the inductance (blue, left scale) and the saturation current (yellow, right scale).

    In an E-E or E-I core the air gap can be made in two ways. If only the center leg is ground down, the air gap sits in that leg and the flux crosses it once. If instead a spacer is placed between the two halves, the spacer sits in all three legs and the flux crosses it twice: down the center leg (one spacer) and back through an outer leg (another spacer). The total air gap is then twice the spacer thickness: to obtain a given \( \ell_g \), each spacer has to measure \( \ell_g/2 \). The design of power inductors and transformers continues in Power Electronics.

    06Common mistakes

    • Designing with the Bsat at 25 °C. In a hot ferrite the saturation is considerably lower.
    • Seeking maximum inductance with no air gap. With DC current, the core saturates immediately.
    • Forgetting that a spacer counts twice. It is on the outer legs and on the center one.
    • Using silicon steel at high frequency. Eddy currents heat it.

    07Self-assessment

    What happens to the inductance and the saturation current when the air gap is doubled?

    If the air gap dominates, the inductance falls to almost half and the saturation current almost doubles.

    And when the turns are doubled with the same air gap?

    The inductance is multiplied by four and the saturation current is halved; the maximum energy does not change.

    Why are ferrites useful at high frequency?

    Because of their high resistivity, which almost eliminates eddy currents.

    What distinguishes a soft material from a hard one?

    The soft one has a narrow hysteresis loop, with little coercive field; the hard one, a wide one, and it retains magnetization.
