    Modulators and demodulators

    Half a decibel of difference between the two branches of an I/Q modulator and five degrees of quadrature error leave the image sideband barely 26 dB down. That is why transmitters are calibrated.

    I/Q modulator I/Q demodulator Costas loop Image rejection Carrier leakage

    01The I/Q modulator

    Any modulation can be written as a complex envelope \( I(t) + jQ(t) \), and every modern modulator generates it in the same way: two mixers fed with the local oscillator in phase and in quadrature, and a summer:

    \[ s(t) = I(t)\cos\omega_c t - Q(t)\sin\omega_c t \] With I and Q generated by a processor and two converters, the same hardware produces AM, FM, SSB, QPSK or QAM: only the software changes. It is the basis of the software-defined radio of the last unit.

    The I/Q demodulator does the inverse operation: it multiplies the received signal by the cosine and the sine of the local oscillator and filters, and recovers I and Q. For that, the phase of the oscillator has to match that of the received carrier: it is synchronized with a Costas loop, a PLL that uses the product of I and Q as a phase detector, or it is corrected digitally after sampling.

    03Imperfections: imbalance and leakage

    A real I/Q modulator has the two branches slightly different: a gain difference \( \varepsilon \) and a quadrature error \( \varphi \). With a tone, instead of a single sideband the opposite one also appears, the image, with a rejection of:

    \[ IRR = \frac{1 + 2\varepsilon\cos\varphi + \varepsilon^2}{1 - 2\varepsilon\cos\varphi + \varepsilon^2} \] \( \varepsilon \) is the gain ratio of the branches (1 if they are equal). With 0.1 dB of imbalance and 1° of error, the rejection is about 40 dB; uncalibrated analog modulators are around 30 to 40 dB. A DC voltage on I or Q also produces carrier leakage, a line at the center of the spectrum.
    Lab · imbalance of an I/Q modulator

    On the left, the spectrum of an I/Q modulator generating a tone at \( f_c + f_m \): the image appears at \( f_c - f_m \). On the right, the constellation of a QPSK with the same imbalance: the ideal points in gray and the transmitted ones in color.

    Current transmitters and receivers measure these errors with a test signal and correct them digitally, predistorting I and Q, and reach rejections of more than 60 dB.

    04In the lab

    Activity 1 · Single sideband

    Generate a single-sideband tone with an integrated I/Q modulator and measure with the spectrum analyzer the rejection of the opposite sideband and the carrier leakage.

    Activity 2 · Correcting

    Adjust by software the gain and phase of one of the branches until the image is minimized, and a DC offset to minimize the leakage.

    Activity 3 · Costas loop

    Simulate in Python a Costas loop that recovers the carrier of a BPSK with a frequency offset.

    05Common mistakes

    • Assuming that the two branches are equal. An error of a few degrees limits the image rejection to 30 dB.
    • Forgetting the DC offset of the converters. It shows up as carrier leakage.
    • Demodulating without recovering the phase. The constellation rotates and the symbols get confused.
    • Confusing the modulator's image with the receiver mixer's image. They have the same origin, but appear in different places.

    06Self-assessment

    What image rejection does a modulator with no gain imbalance and 1° of quadrature error give?

    About 41 dB.

    What signal has to be put on Q to generate SSB with the phasing method?

    The Hilbert transform of the message.

    What does a Costas loop do?

    It recovers the carrier phase from the modulated signal, using the product of I and Q as a phase detector.

    What does a DC offset on the I branch produce?

    Carrier leakage: a line at fc.
