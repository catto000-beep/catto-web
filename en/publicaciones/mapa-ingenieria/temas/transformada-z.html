    The Z-transform and notions of filtering

    The same as Laplace, for sequences. The imaginary axis becomes the unit circle, and everything you knew about stability carries over by replacing “left of the axis” with “inside the circle”.

    Z-transform Unit circle Poles and zeros Notch Digital filters

    01Laplace, for sequences

    The Z transform is to discrete signals what Laplace is to continuous ones. The definition replaces the integral with a sum and the exponential with a power:

    \[ X(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]\,z^{-n}, \qquad z \in \mathbb{C} \] Each sample is multiplied by \( z^{-n} \). Since \( z^{-1} \) is the delay of one sample, the transform is the difference equation written in another way.

    And the relationship with the \( s \)-plane is direct: sampling with period \( T \) corresponds to the substitution

    \[ z = e^{sT} \]

    which takes the imaginary axis of the \( s \)-plane to the unit circle of the \( z \)-plane, and the left half-plane to the interior of the circle. Everything we knew about stability carries over by replacing “left of the axis” with “inside the circle”.

    In the \( s \)-planeIn the \( z \)-plane
    Imaginary axisUnit circle \( |z| = 1 \)
    Left half-planeInterior of the circle
    Origin \( s = 0 \)\( z = 1 \), DC
    \( \omega = \pi/T \), half the sampling frequency\( z = -1 \)
    Stable: poles on the leftStable: poles inside

    The fourth row has an important consequence: in the \( z \)-plane the whole frequency range fits in one turn. Going around the circle from \( z = 1 \) to \( z = -1 \) sweeps from DC up to half the sampling frequency, and continuing to turn means starting over. There is aliasing again, turned into geometry.

    02Designing by placing poles and zeros

    From the previous relationship comes a direct design technique: a zero on the circle cancels that frequency; a pole near the circle boosts it. With that you build filters by hand, without formulas, just by arranging points.

    \[ |H(e^{j\Omega})| = |K|\,\frac{\prod_i |\,e^{j\Omega} - z_i\,|}{\prod_k |\,e^{j\Omega} - p_k\,|} \] The same geometric reading as in the \( s \)-plane, but going around the circle instead of along the axis: product of distances to the zeros over product of distances to the poles.

    03The region of convergence, once again

    As in Laplace, \( X(z) \) alone is not enough. Except that here the regions are annuli instead of strips:

    If the region…The sequence is…
    Is the exterior of the outermost poleCausal: it is zero for \( n \lt 0 \)
    Is the interior of the innermost poleAnticausal
    Is an annulus between polesTwo-sided
    Contains the unit circleHas a Fourier transform and the system is stable

    And the usual conclusion, translated: a causal system is stable if and only if all of its poles are inside the unit circle. The lab shows it with the last case: raising the radius above 1, the impulse response stops dying out and starts to grow.

    The pair to know by heart

    \( a^{n}u[n] \longleftrightarrow \dfrac{1}{1 - az^{-1}} \), with region \( |z| \gt |a| \). From this comes almost everything: the first-order filter, the geometric sum and the condition \( |a| \lt 1 \) for convergence. And its anticausal version, \( -a^{n}u[-n-1] \), has exactly the same expression with region \( |z| \lt |a| \).

    04Basics of digital filtering

    The geometric reading is enough to design several useful things by eye:

    What you wantWhat to place where
    Low-passPole near \( z = 1 \), zero at \( z = -1 \)
    High-passPole near \( z = -1 \), zero at \( z = 1 \)
    Reject a frequency \( f_0 \)Pair of zeros on the circle at \( \pm 2\pi f_0/f_s \)
    Boost a frequencyPair of poles near the circle at that angle
    Remove DCA zero exactly at \( z = 1 \)
    Comb, for mains harmonicsZeros spread at multiples of the 50 Hz angle

    The rule of thumb for the bandwidth of a resonator is that a pole of radius \( r \) gives a peak whose width at −3 dB is approximately \( 2(1-r) \) in radians per sample. With \( r = 0.99 \) the peak is extremely narrow; with \( r = 0.8 \), wide and smooth.

    The constraint that cannot be violated

    If the filter coefficients are real —and they are, because they are programmed with real numbers— the complex poles and zeros have to come in conjugate pairs. You cannot place a lone pole at \( 0.9e^{j\pi/4} \) without its twin at \( 0.9e^{-j\pi/4} \): the resulting filter would need complex arithmetic. That is why all the labs in this topic show the poles in pairs symmetric about the real axis.

    05In the lab

    Activity 1 · A 50 Hz notch by hand

    With \( f_s = 1 \) kHz, calculate the angle that corresponds to 50 Hz and place the pair of zeros on the circle. Add a pair of poles at the same angle with \( r = 0.95 \) to narrow the rejection, and write down the coefficients of the resulting biquad.

    Activity 2 · Measuring the width of the resonator

    Program \( y[n] = 2r\cos\theta\,y[n-1] - r^2 y[n-2] + x[n] \) for \( r = 0.9 \), \( 0.99 \) and \( 0.999 \), measure the −3 dB bandwidth of each and compare it with the rule \( 2(1-r) \).

    Activity 3 · Crossing the circle

    With the same program, raise \( r \) to 1.01 and observe the output. Determine in how many samples a 16-bit accumulator overflows and relate it to the position of the pole.

    06Common mistakes

    • Placing a complex pole without its conjugate. With real coefficients they must come in pairs.
    • Forgetting the region of convergence. The same \( X(z) \) describes a causal or an anticausal sequence depending on the region.
    • Confusing \( |z| = 1 \) with the sampling frequency. A full turn is \( f_s \); the useful half goes up to \( z = -1 \).
    • Placing a pole exactly on the circle. It is an oscillator, not a filter: it is not BIBO stable.
    • Designing in the \( s \)-plane and moving to the \( z \)-plane carelessly. The bilinear transformation warps the frequency axis, and you have to prewarp.

    07Self-assessment

    What is the relationship between the \( s \) and \( z \) planes?

    \( z = e^{sT} \). The imaginary axis goes to the unit circle and the left half-plane to the interior.

    When is a causal discrete system stable?

    When all of its poles are inside the unit circle.

    Where does a zero go to remove DC?

    At \( z = 1 \), which is the point that corresponds to zero frequency.

    How do you reject a frequency \( f_0 \)?

    With a pair of zeros on the circle at the angle \( \pm 2\pi f_0/f_s \).

    How wide is the peak of a pole of radius \( r \)?

    Approximately \( 2(1-r) \) radians per sample: the closer to the circle, the narrower.

    Why do complex poles come in pairs?

    Because the filter coefficients are real. A lone pole would force the use of complex arithmetic.
