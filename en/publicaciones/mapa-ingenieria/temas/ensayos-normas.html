    Standards-based testing

    A power supply that must give 5.00 V ± 1% measures 5.045 V with an uncertainty of 10 mV. It seems to comply, but there is almost a 16% probability that it does not.

    IEC standards Electromagnetic compatibility Electrical safety Decision rule Guard band

    01A test anyone can repeat

    A standards-based test is a measurement with a procedure fixed in advance: what is measured, with which instruments, under which environmental conditions, with which setup and by which criterion it is accepted. The goal is that two different laboratories, testing the same product, reach the same result. This is how a product is certified as safe, as not interfering with other equipment, and as withstanding the environment where it is going to operate.

    International standards are issued by the IEC (electrotechnology) and ISO (everything else), and in Argentina IRAM (the Argentine standards institute) adopts them and issues its own standards. Some are mandatory by regulation: low-voltage electrical products sold in the country must certify their safety under the regime of the Secretariat of Commerce, and radio equipment must obtain homologation from ENACOM (the national communications regulator).

    In immunity tests the result is not a number but a behavior, classified into criteria: A, it operates normally during the test; B, it degrades temporarily and recovers on its own; C, it recovers with operator intervention. Each product standard sets which criterion it requires.

    03How it is carried out and reported

    A valid test requires calibrated instruments with traceability, the environmental conditions set by the standard, the exact setup it describes (distances, cables, ground plane) and a record of everything. The report, according to ISO/IEC 17025, identifies the item tested, the standard and its version, the method, the equipment, the conditions, the results with their uncertainty and, if applicable, the statement of conformity with the decision rule that was used. That rule is what connects the measurement uncertainty, seen in errors and uncertainty, with the decision to accept or reject.

    04Deciding with uncertainty

    If a power supply must give 5 V ± 1% and the laboratory measures 5.045 V with an expanded uncertainty of 10 mV, the true value may be between 5.035 and 5.055 V: outside the tolerance. Does it comply or not? Decision rules (ILAC-G8 guide, JCGM 106 document) resolve it in two ways:

    • Simple acceptance. It is accepted if the measured value is within the tolerance. The risk of accepting something that does not comply is shared between the laboratory and the customer, and is acceptable only if the uncertainty is small compared with the tolerance.
    • Guard band. The acceptance limits are shrunk by \( U \) on each side. Conformity is declared only if the measured value falls within them; between the guard band and the limit, neither one thing nor the other can be stated.
    \[ TUR = \frac{T}{U} \] The ratio between the half-width of the tolerance \( T \) and the expanded uncertainty. Traditional practice asks for at least 4:1; with 10:1 the guard band hardly reduces the acceptance zone.

    07Common mistakes

    • Declaring conformity without considering the uncertainty. Near the limit, the decision may be wrong.
    • Citing a standard without its version. Requirements change between editions.
    • Improvising the setup. In electromagnetic compatibility, a cable moved changes the result.
    • Testing with instruments without a valid calibration. The result loses traceability and validity.

    08Self-assessment

    What TUR does a tolerance of ±0.5% on 10 V measured with U = 10 mV have?

    \( 50\ \text{mV} / 10\ \text{mV} = 5 \).

    With a guard band, up to what value is an upper tolerance of 5.05 V accepted with U = 10 mV?

    Up to 5.04 V.

    What does criterion B mean in an immunity test?

    That the equipment temporarily loses some function during the test and recovers on its own, without intervention.

    Which document defines the competence requirements for a testing laboratory?

    The ISO/IEC 17025 standard.
