    The professional practice of engineering

    A university degree is not enough to sign: you need the license from the council or association of the jurisdiction where you work.

    Qualifying degree Reserved activities License Professional councils COPITEC

    01From the degree to the signature

    Graduating is not enough to sign. Between the university degree and the first signed drawing there are two steps regulated by law:

    1. The qualifying degree (título habilitante), issued by a university with an accredited program, which defines what the graduate is qualified to do.
    2. The professional license (matrícula) from the professional council or association of the jurisdiction where the engineer will work, which authorizes practice and subjects the professional to the oversight of that body.

    Signing as an engineer without a license is the illegal practice of the profession, and a document signed that way has no value before the bodies that require it.

    02Degree scope and reserved activities

    The Ley de Educación Superior (Higher Education Law, Law 24,521) includes in its Article 43 the programs whose practice may put the public interest at risk —people’s health, safety and property—, and among them is Electronic Engineering. For those programs the Ministry of Education sets the minimum content, the number of hours, the intensity of practical training and the reserved activities, and CONEAU (the national university evaluation and accreditation agency) periodically accredits each program.

    Degree scope (alcances)Reserved activities
    What they areEverything the graduate is qualified to doWhat only a holder of that degree may do, because it affects the public interest
    Who sets themEach university, in the curriculumThe Ministry of Education, with the advice of the Council of Universities (Resolution 1254/2018)
    ExclusivityNo: other degrees may share themYes, although they may be shared with other degrees listed in the same article

    The reserved activities of Electronic Engineering revolve around designing, calculating and planning electronic, telecommunications and control systems and equipment; directing and overseeing their construction, installation and maintenance; certifying their operation and safety; and planning and directing everything related to health and safety in their activity.

    03The license and the professional councils

    Because the provinces retained oversight of the professions, the license is local. In national jurisdiction —the City of Buenos Aires for matters that depend on the Nation and for activities of federal scope— Decree-Law 6070/58, ratified by Law 14,467, applies; it created the professional engineering councils. For electronic engineers, the national body is COPITEC, the Professional Council of Telecommunications, Electronics and Computer Engineering. Each province has its own law and its own association or council; for example, in the province of Buenos Aires, Law 10,416 created the Colegio de Ingenieros (Association of Engineers).

    Function of the council or associationIn practice
    Granting and overseeing the licenseVerifying the degree and registering the professional
    Registering professional commissionsRecording which work each professional signs and for whom
    Safeguarding ethicsA disciplinary tribunal that applies the code of ethics
    Defending the practice of the professionReporting illegal practice and advising licensed members

    Anyone who works in several provinces needs to register in each one, although some have reciprocity agreements that simplify the process.

    04Ways of practicing

    • Independent practice: the professional contracts directly with their clients, sets or agrees on their fees, invoices and takes responsibility for their work.
    • As an employee: working for a company or the State. The professional remains professionally responsible for what they sign, even if the company assigned them the task.
    • As a technical representative: a company that carries out reserved work needs a licensed professional who answers technically for it; this is common among installation contractors and in public works.

    05Fees

    For decades engineering fees were set by mandatory fee schedules. Since the deregulation of 1991 they are freely agreed between the professional and the client, and the councils’ fee schedules remained as a reference. They are still important in two situations: when the price was not agreed, because the judge uses them to set it; and for expert witnesses’ fees in a trial.

    Agreeing the fee in writing, together with the scope of the work, avoids most of the conflicts with clients.

    08Common mistakes

    • Signing without a license in that jurisdiction. It is illegal practice and the document is worthless.
    • Confusing degree scope with reserved activities. Being qualified is not the same as having exclusivity.
    • Believing that an employee is not answerable for what they sign. Professional liability is personal.
    • Lending your signature. Signing work that was neither done nor supervised by you is a serious ethical breach and shifts all the responsibility onto whoever signs.

    09Self-assessment

    What two requirements are needed to practice as an engineer?

    The qualifying degree and the license in the jurisdiction where you work.

    Which article of Law 24,521 includes Electronic Engineering among the programs of public interest?

    Article 43.

    Which body licenses electronic engineers in national jurisdiction?

    COPITEC, created under Decree-Law 6070/58.

    What is a technical representative?

    The licensed professional who answers technically for the reserved work that a company carries out.
