    Safe work procedures

    In an installation with a ground rod, a ground fault leaves the enclosure of a device at more than 100 V without the circuit breaker noticing. What saves the person who touches it is the residual-current device.

    Job safety analysis Work permit Five golden rules TT system Residual-current device

    01What a safe work procedure is

    A safe work procedure describes step by step how to carry out a risky task: what has to be prepared, what hazards appear at each step, what controls are applied and who gives authorization. It is built with a job safety analysis (JSA): the task is divided into steps, the hazards of each one are listed and a measure is assigned following the hierarchy of controls from industrial hygiene and safety.

    For the highest-risk tasks a work permit is also used: a document signed by the person who authorizes the work, who verifies the conditions before starting, and which is valid for a defined time and place.

    02Lockout: the five golden rules

    To work de-energized on an electrical installation it is not enough to open a switch. Lockout follows five steps in order, and the work starts only when all of them have been completed:

    1. Disconnect all sources of voltage, with a visible or reliable break.
    2. Lock the switching devices with a padlock and tag them with a card, so that no one closes them. Each person who works puts on their own padlock.
    3. Verify the absence of voltage with a detector tested before and after on a known source.
    4. Ground and short-circuit all the conductors, to discharge capacitances and to protect against an accidental energization.
    5. Mark off and signpost the work area.

    The same principle of lockout and tagout applies to every hazardous energy: pneumatic, hydraulic, mechanical or thermal. In electronics it also appears in the filter capacitors of power supplies and drives, which keep lethal voltages for minutes after disconnection: they must be discharged and verified.

    03Electric shock and protection by a residual-current device

    The harm is produced by the current that passes through the body, and it depends on its magnitude, duration and path. Standard IEC 60479-1 divides the time–current plane into zones: below half a milliampere it is not perceived; around 10 mA there are contractions that prevent letting go of the conductor, and with a few tens of milliamperes for fractions of a second there is a risk of ventricular fibrillation, which is the main cause of death by electrocution.

    In an installation with a TT system, the neutral is grounded at the transformer and the exposed conductive parts of the installation are connected to their own ground rod. If a phase touches the enclosure of a device, the fault current returns through the earth, and the enclosure is left at a voltage with respect to the ground:

    \[ I_f = \frac{U_0}{R_A + R_B} \] \[ U_c = I_f\, R_A \] \( U_0 \) = 220 V is the phase voltage, \( R_A \) the resistance of the grounding of the exposed conductive parts and \( R_B \) that of the neutral. With resistances of a few ohms, the fault current is a few amperes: it does not make a 16 A circuit breaker trip, and the enclosure stays at more than 100 V for as long as the fault lasts.

    That is why in the TT system the protection is provided by the residual-current device (RCD), which detects the difference between the current leaving through the phase and the current returning through the neutral. With a 30 mA device, the condition of standard IEC 60364-4-41 is \( R_A \cdot I_{\Delta n} \le 50 \) V, and the RCD has to open in less than 300 ms at its rated current and in less than 40 ms at five times that current. In Argentina, the AEA (Argentine Electrotechnical Association) regulations and SRT Resolution 900/15 set the conditions for grounding and its measurement. Installations were covered in protection and sizing of installations.

    05Common mistakes

    • Trusting that an open switch means no voltage. It has to be verified, and there may be backfeed from another source.
    • Using a single padlock for several people. Each person locks out with their own and removes it themselves.
    • Believing that the circuit breaker protects people. It protects the cables; in a TT system a ground fault does not make it trip.
    • Forgetting charged capacitors. A disconnected power supply can have hundreds of volts stored.

    06Self-assessment

    What is the maximum grounding resistance of the exposed parts with a 30 mA RCD and a 50 V limit voltage?

    \( 50/0.03 \approx 1667 \) Ω.

    With RA = 20 Ω and RB = 5 Ω, what fault current flows and at what voltage is the enclosure left?

    8.8 A and 176 V.

    Why are the conductors grounded and short-circuited after verifying the absence of voltage?

    To discharge capacitances, and so that an accidental energization trips the protections with no voltage in the work area.

    Which tasks require a work permit?

    The high-risk ones: hot work, work at height, in confined spaces and electrical work, among others.
