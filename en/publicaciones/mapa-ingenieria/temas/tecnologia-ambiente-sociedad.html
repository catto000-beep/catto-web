    Technology, environment and society

    If the population grows 0.8% per year and output per person 2%, halving emissions in 25 years requires energy and carbon per unit of output to fall by more than 5% per year.

    IPAT Kaya identity Rebound effect Decoupling Precautionary principle

    01A two-way relationship

    Technology is not neutral with respect to the environment: every technique changes which resources are extracted, what energy is used and what waste is generated. But it does not determine it alone either: the impact depends on how many people use it, how much they consume and what decisions societies make about it. The steam engine, electrification, the automobile and the mobile phone changed production, cities and the relationship with the environment all at once. The ethical and social framework of the profession was covered in sustainable development and in social impact, in Engineering and Society.

    02The IPAT identity

    Paul Ehrlich and John Holdren proposed in 1971 to break environmental impact down into three factors:

    \[ I = P \times A \times T \] \( P \) is the population, \( A \) the affluence or consumption per person, and \( T \) the impact per unit consumed, which depends on technology. It is an accounting identity, not a model: it serves to organize the discussion, because the three factors are not independent.

    Applied to carbon dioxide emissions, the decomposition is known as the Kaya identity:

    \[ \mathrm{CO_2} = P \cdot \frac{\mathrm{GDP}}{P} \cdot \frac{E}{\mathrm{GDP}} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{CO_2}}{E} \] The last two factors are the energy intensity of the economy and the carbon intensity of energy. Emissions fall only if those two intensities decline faster than population and output per person grow.

    03Efficiency, rebound and decoupling

    Improving efficiency does not guarantee consuming less. William Stanley Jevons observed in 1865 that more efficient steam engines increased coal consumption in England, because they made new uses profitable: this is the Jevons paradox. In a milder form, the rebound effect says that part of the savings from an efficiency improvement is lost because the service becomes cheaper and is used more. LED lighting consumes far less per lumen, and more spaces are lit for longer.

    We speak of decoupling when output grows and impact does not follow. It is relative if the impact grows more slowly than output, and absolute if it falls while output grows. Only absolute decoupling reduces the harm.

    04Assessing technologies

    Faced with a new technology there are questions that engineering can answer with data: how much energy and materials it consumes over its whole life cycle, what waste it leaves, who benefits and who is harmed, what happens if it spreads widely. Technology assessment and the precautionary principle, which asks that protective measures not be postponed for lack of scientific certainty when there is a risk of serious harm, are the institutional tools for doing so before the effects become irreversible. The precautionary principle appears in the 1992 Rio Declaration and in Argentina’s General Environmental Law, which is covered in the unit on the environmental legal framework.

    Activity 2 · Rebound

    Estimate the energy that the lighting of a house used with incandescent lamps and the energy it uses today with LEDs, counting the number of light points and the hours of use.

    Activity 3 · Debate

    Discuss in a group a recent electronic technology using the three IPAT factors: who uses it, how much they use it and how much impact each use has.

    06Common mistakes

    • Believing that efficiency alone reduces impact. The rebound can offset it.
    • Confusing relative with absolute decoupling. Emissions growing less than GDP does not mean they fall.
    • Reading IPAT as a causal model. It is an identity: the factors influence each other.
    • Attributing the whole problem to a single factor. Population, consumption and technology act together.

    07Self-assessment

    Which two factors of the Kaya identity depend on technology?

    The energy intensity of output and the carbon intensity of energy.

    At what annual rate must emissions fall to be halved in 25 years?

    \( 0.5^{1/25} - 1 \approx -2.7 \) % per year.

    What is the Jevons paradox?

    That an efficiency improvement can increase the total consumption of the resource, because it makes its use cheaper.

    What is the difference between relative and absolute decoupling?

    In relative decoupling the impact grows more slowly than output; in absolute decoupling it falls while output grows.
