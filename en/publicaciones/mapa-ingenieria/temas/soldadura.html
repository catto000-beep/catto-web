    Soldering in electronics

    The same oven that melts the paste well on a light board leaves a heavy board 11 °C colder and barely 43 s above the melting point. The thermal mass of the board is also part of the profile.

    Soft soldering SAC305 Reflow Thermal profile IPC-A-610

    01Soft soldering

    In electronics, soft soldering is used: an alloy that melts below 450 °C joins the parts without melting them. The solder wets the copper and reacts with it, forming a thin layer of intermetallic compounds, which provides the electrical and mechanical bond; if that layer grows too much, through excess time or temperature, the joint becomes brittle. The flux removes the oxides and protects the surface while soldering.

    02The methods

    MethodUse
    Manual, with a soldering ironPrototypes, repairs and touch-ups; temperature-controlled tip
    WaveThrough-hole components: the board passes over a wave of molten solder
    SelectiveA small solder fountain solders through-hole components point by point on mixed boards
    ReflowSurface mount: paste applied with a stencil, components placed by machine, and an oven
    Vapor phaseReflow in the vapor of a liquid that boils at a fixed temperature; heats evenly

    Plastic components absorb moisture from the environment; in the oven it vaporizes and can crack the package. That is why they are classified by their moisture sensitivity level (MSL), come in sealed bags with desiccant and, if the time out of the bag is exceeded, are baked before soldering.

    03The reflow profile

    In reflow, the board travels through a multi-zone oven on a belt. Its temperature follows a profile: a preheat ramp, a soak that activates the flux and evens out the temperature of large and small components, a peak above the melting point (liquidus) and a controlled cooling. Paste manufacturers give a window; for SAC305, typical values are:

    ParameterTypical value
    Ramp-up rateno more than 3 °C/s
    Soak between 150 and 200 °C60 to 120 s
    Time above liquidus (217 °C)60 to 90 s
    Peak temperature235 to 250 °C
    Coolingno more than 6 °C/s

    The board does not reach the temperature of each zone: it approaches it with a time constant that depends on its thermal mass. With a first-order model the effect can be seen:

    \[ \frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{T_{zone}(t) - T}{\tau} \] In practice, the profile is measured with thermocouples attached at several points of a real board, and the zone temperatures and the belt speed are adjusted until all of them fall within the window.

    04Defects and inspection

    DefectTypical cause
    Cold or non-wetted jointInsufficient temperature or time, oxidized surfaces
    BridgeExcess paste, misaligned stencil
    TombstoningThe two pads of a chip melt at different times and the component stands up
    Solder ballsWet paste or preheating too fast
    Voids in BGATrapped flux gases

    The IPC-A-610 standard defines the acceptance criteria for assemblies in three classes: 1, general-purpose products; 2, dedicated-service products; and 3, high-reliability products. Inspection is done by eye or with a magnifier, with automated optical inspection (AOI) and, for joints hidden under a BGA, with X-rays. The long-term reliability of the joints was covered in reliability: thermal fatigue is their main failure mechanism.

    06Common mistakes

    • Using the profile of one board for another. Thermal mass changes the actual profile even if the oven is the same.
    • Raising the iron temperature to compensate for a dirty tip. The pads and the components get damaged.
    • Leaving moisture-sensitive components out of the bag. The package cracks in the oven.
    • Mixing leaded and lead-free alloys. The resulting joints are unreliable.

    07Self-assessment

    At what temperature does SAC305 melt?

    Between 217 and 220 °C.

    What is the soak of the profile for?

    To activate the flux and let all the components reach an even temperature before melting.

    What causes tombstoning?

    One pad melts before the other and surface tension stands the component up.

    In the lab, how is time above liquidus recovered on a heavy board?

    By lowering the belt speed or raising the temperature of the reflow zones.
