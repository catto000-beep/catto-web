    Heat transfer mechanisms

    The thermal chain of a transistor is solved exactly like a resistive divider: power plays the role of current, temperature that of voltage, and each interface that of a resistance. With that analogy, calculating a heat sink is a circuits exercise.

    Conduction Convection Radiation Thermal resistance Heat sinks

    01Three ways to move heat

    Heat always goes from the hot body to the cold one, and it arrives by three paths that act at the same time. In electronics all three are present in any piece of equipment, but they do not weigh the same.

    MechanismHow it travelsLawWhere it dominates
    ConductionBy contact, without the matter moving \( \dot{Q} = kA\,\Delta T/L \)From the chip to the package and from there to the heat sink
    ConvectionThe fluid moves and carries the heat away \( \dot{Q} = hA\,\Delta T \)From the heat sink to the air
    RadiationElectromagnetic waves, no medium needed \( \dot{Q} = \varepsilon\sigma A(T^4 - T_a^4) \) Contributes little below 100 °C; in a vacuum it is the only thing left

    The third row has the fourth power, so radiation grows very fast with temperature. At 60 °C over a 25 °C ambient, polished aluminum (emissivity 0.05) radiates barely about 12 W per square meter; black anodized —emissivity close to 0.9— radiates almost twenty times more, and the heat sink gains ten or fifteen percent overall, because the fins shade one another. That is why heat sinks are black, and not for aesthetics.

    \[ \sigma = 5.67\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{W/m^2K^4} \] Stefan–Boltzmann constant. In a satellite there is no air, so there is no convection: all the electronics is cooled exclusively by radiation, and that is why spacecraft thermal design is a different problem.

    02The analogy that solves everything

    Fourier's law for a flat wall has exactly the form of Ohm's law, and that coincidence is no accident: both describe a flow pushed by a potential difference against a resistance.

    \[ \Delta T = \dot{Q}\,R_{th} \qquad\longleftrightarrow\qquad V = I\,R \] The dissipated power plays the role of current, the temperature difference that of voltage, and the thermal resistance —in degrees per watt— that of resistance. And thermal resistances in series add up, just like electrical ones.
    Thermal quantityUnitEquivalent to
    Dissipated power \( \dot{Q} \)WCurrent
    Temperature differenceKVoltage
    Thermal resistance \( R_{th} \)K/WResistance
    Thermal capacitance \( C_{th} \)J/KCapacitance

    The last row explains the transient regime: a heat sink with a lot of mass takes time to heat up, just as a large capacitor takes time to charge, and the time constant is \( R_{th}C_{th} \). That is why a transistor withstands power peaks far above what it could withstand in steady state.

    03The junction-to-ambient chain

    Heat is born at the semiconductor junction and has to reach the air. On the way it crosses three resistances in series, and the temperature drops across each one:

    \[ T_j = T_a + P\,(R_{jc} + R_{cs} + R_{sa}) \] \( R_{jc} \) junction-to-case, which the manufacturer sets; \( R_{cs} \) case-to-heat-sink, which depends on the mounting; and \( R_{sa} \) heat-sink-to-ambient, which is the only one you choose by buying. \( T_j \) cannot exceed the \( T_{j\max} \) in the datasheet, almost always 150 °C.

    The complete thermal chain, with values from real datasheets. You move the power and the ambient temperature, choose the package, interface and heat sink, and the junction temperature appears along with how much power the assembly can handle.

    Thermal paste does not conduct: it fills

    Two apparently flat metal surfaces really touch over less than 1% of their area; the rest is air, which is an excellent insulator. The paste is not there because it conducts well —it conducts much worse than metal— but to displace that air. Hence more paste is worse: the layer has to be the minimum that fills the imperfections, and anything beyond that adds resistance.

    04In the lab

    Activity 1 · Measuring a thermal resistance

    Mount a power resistor on a heat sink with a thermocouple attached to the fin. Dissipate a known power, wait for steady state and calculate \( R_{sa} \) from the difference with respect to ambient. Compare with the heat sink datasheet.

    Activity 2 · How much the paste contributes

    Repeat the previous measurement with dry contact, with paste and with a silicone pad, always with the same tightening torque. Calculate \( R_{cs} \) in the three cases and rank them.

    Activity 3 · The transient

    Record the heat sink temperature against time from the moment the power is applied until steady state. Fit an exponential, obtain the time constant and from it the thermal capacitance of the assembly.

    05Common mistakes

    • Using too much thermal paste. The useful layer is the minimum; the excess adds resistance.
    • Using \( R_{ja} \) when there is a heat sink. That value assumes the package in open air and it is not valid for the real assembly.
    • Calculating with the room's ambient. Inside the enclosure it is quite a bit hotter.
    • Forgetting that mica insulates but gets in the way. It adds thermal resistance in exchange for electrical insulation.
    • Designing for \( T_{j\max} \). That is the destruction limit, not the operating point: leave a margin.
    • Always neglecting radiation. Below 100 °C it contributes little, but in a vacuum it is the only thing.

    06Self-assessment

    What role does power play in the thermal analogy?

    That of current. The temperature difference plays voltage and the thermal resistance plays resistance.

    Why are heat sinks black?

    Because of emissivity: black anodizing radiates much better than polished aluminum, and that adds ten or fifteen percent.

    What is thermal paste really for?

    To displace the air trapped between two surfaces that only touch over a tiny fraction of their area.

    How is the electronics of a satellite cooled?

    Only by radiation: with no air there is no convection.

    What sets the thermal time constant?

    The product \( R_{th}C_{th} \), just as in an RC circuit. That is why a heat sink with mass withstands power peaks.

    Which of the three resistances in the chain is chosen by buying?

    \( R_{sa} \), the heat sink's. \( R_{jc} \) is set by the manufacturer of the semiconductor and \( R_{cs} \) depends on the mounting.
