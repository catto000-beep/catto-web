    Introduction to analog integrated circuit design

    A 2 pF capacitor sets the bandwidth of an integrated op amp and creates a zero that steals phase from it. A 1/gm resistor in series makes it disappear.

    01From the specification sheet to silicon

    Designing an analog integrated circuit means sharing out a budget: with a limited current and area you have to obtain gain, bandwidth, phase margin, output swing, noise and offset. Applied Electronics I introduced the basics, matching, Pelgrom's law and design with \( g_m/I_D \), in analog integrated circuit design. Here we go through the design of a complete block using everything seen in the subject: feedback, frequency response and compensation.

    02The two-stage op amp

    The most widely taught CMOS op amp has a differential pair with a mirror load, a differential stage that provides gain and converts to a single-ended output, followed by a common source with an active load. Each stage contributes a gain on the order of the intrinsic gain of the transistor, and the product easily exceeds 70 dB:

    \[ A_0 = g_{m1}\,(r_{o2} \parallel r_{o4}) \cdot g_{m6}\,(r_{o6} \parallel r_{o7}) \] \[ \mathrm{GBW} = \frac{g_{m1}}{2\pi C_c} \] The Miller capacitor \( C_c \), between the input and the output of the second stage, sets the dominant pole. The gain–bandwidth product depends only on the input transconductance and on \( C_c \).

    03Miller compensation and the zero

    Miller compensation splits the poles: the first goes down and the second goes up to near \( g_{m6}/C_L \). But \( C_c \) also creates a direct path from the input to the output of the second stage, which produces a right-half-plane zero: it adds phase lag like a pole, and takes margin away from the amplifier:

    \[ p_2 \approx \frac{g_{m6}}{C_L} \] \[ z = \frac{1}{C_c\,(1/g_{m6} - R_z)} \] Without a resistor \( R_z \), the zero is at \( g_{m6}/C_c \), in the right half-plane. With \( R_z = 1/g_{m6} \) in series with \( C_c \) it goes to infinity, and with a larger \( R_z \) it moves to the left half-plane, where it adds phase.

    The classic design rules, from Allen and Holberg, call for \( g_{m6} \ge 10\, g_{m1} \) to push the zero away and \( C_c \ge 0.22\, C_L \) so that the second pole lies at more than 2.2 times the GBW: with that the phase margin is around 60°. The slew rate is set by the tail current charging \( C_c \), \( I_5/C_c \), unless the second stage cannot charge the load fast enough: \( (I_6 - I_5)/C_L \).

    04Other topologies and blocks

    The folded cascode obtains high gain in a single stage: the only high-impedance node is the output, the load itself compensates it and there is no Miller zero. It is the usual choice for capacitive loads, as in switched-capacitor circuits. With a class AB output stage, rail-to-rail swing is obtained, and with two complementary pairs at the input, a rail-to-rail input.

    Around the amplifiers, an analog chip needs other blocks: a bandgap reference, which combines the base–emitter voltage, which falls with temperature, with a voltage proportional to absolute temperature to obtain about 1.2 V almost independent of it; a constant-gm bias; internal LDO regulators and data converters. All are designed with the same method: specify, choose the topology, size by hand with \( g_m/I_D \) and verify in the simulator.

    05In the lab

    Activity 1 · Simulation

    Size the two-stage op amp in a SPICE simulator with models of an open technology, such as SKY130, and compare the simulated phase margin with the one calculated by hand.

    Activity 2 · Corners and Monte Carlo

    Repeat the simulation at the process and temperature corners and with Monte Carlo of the offset. Identify which specification is lost first.

    Activity 3 · Slew rate

    Simulate the response to a large step and measure the rising and falling slew rate. Explain the asymmetry.

    06Common mistakes

    • Forgetting the right-half-plane zero. With small \( g_{m6} \), the zero gets closer and the phase margin calculated with two poles is optimistic.
    • Designing for the nominal load. The second pole depends on \( C_L \); with more load the margin falls.
    • Shrinking \( C_c \) to gain GBW without looking at the second pole. The GBW goes up and the phase margin goes down.
    • Trusting the hand calculation without simulating the corners. Currents and capacitances change with process and temperature.

    07Self-assessment

    What GBW does an op amp with gm1 = 100 μS and Cc = 2 pF have?

    \( 10^{-4}/(2\pi \cdot 2\cdot10^{-12}) \approx 8 \) MHz.

    With gm6 = 2 mS and Cc = 2 pF, where is the zero without Rz?

    At \( 2\cdot10^{-3}/(2\pi \cdot 2\cdot10^{-12}) \approx 159 \) MHz, in the right half-plane.

    What slew rate does a 20 μA tail give with Cc = 2 pF?

    10 V/μs.

    What value of Rz eliminates the zero?

    \( R_z = 1/g_{m6} \).
