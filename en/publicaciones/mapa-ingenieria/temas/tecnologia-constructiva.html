    Construction technology

    An external trace of 35 μm copper carrying 3 A with a 10 °C rise needs 1.37 mm of width; on an inner layer, 3.56 mm. Inside the board the heat gets out worse.

    01The printed circuit board

    The printed circuit board is at once the mechanical support and the wiring of a device. It is made on a laminate: the most common is FR-4, glass-fiber cloth with epoxy resin, which is specified by its glass transition temperature (Tg), its permittivity and its thickness, typically 1.6 mm. For microwaves, PTFE or ceramic laminates are used, with lower losses and more stable permittivity. Copper is specified in ounces per square foot: 1 oz is equivalent to about 35 μm.

    A board can have one, two or many layers. In multilayer boards, the inner layers are etched separately and pressed together with sheets of resin (prepreg); the internal power and ground planes give a low-inductance return and shielding. The drawings and the representation standards were covered in computer-aided design environments.

    The manufacturer publishes its design rules: minimum trace width and spacing, minimum drill diameter, annular ring around each hole and distance to the edge. The design is sent to it as Gerber or ODB++ files, with the drill file.

    03Trace width

    A trace heats up by the Joule effect and the heat has to escape to the air or to the laminate. The IPC-2221 standard gives an empirical relationship between the current, the allowed temperature rise and the cross-section of the trace:

    \[ I = k\,\Delta T^{0.44}\,A^{0.725} \] \( I \) in amperes, \( \Delta T \) in °C and \( A \) in mil² (thousandths of an inch squared), with \( k \) = 0.048 for external layers and 0.024 for internal ones. The IPC-2152 standard replaced those curves with others based on more complete measurements; those of IPC-2221 are still used as a quick estimate and tend to be conservative.

    04Insulation, impedance and heat

    Between conductors at different voltages two distances have to be respected: the clearance (clearance), which prevents arcing, and the creepage distance (creepage), measured along the surface, which prevents conduction through dirt and moisture. Safety standards, such as IEC 62368-1, set them according to the voltage, the pollution degree and the type of insulation; a milled slot in the board lengthens the creepage distance.

    In fast signals, the trace and the ground plane form a transmission line —microstrip on outer layers, stripline on inner ones— whose impedance depends on the width, the dielectric thickness and its permittivity; manufacturers control it on request, typically 50 Ω. To remove heat from a component, copper planes and thermal vias are used, which conduct it to the opposite side or to a heat sink.

    The equipment ends up in an enclosure, which is specified by its ingress protection rating according to IEC 60529: the first digit indicates the protection against solids, from 0 to 6, and the second against water, from 0 to 9 (9 is a high-pressure, high-temperature water jet). An IP65 enclosure is dust-tight and withstands water jets.

    06Common mistakes

    • Sizing an internal trace as if it were external. It needs more than double the width for the same heating.
    • Ignoring the voltage drop. A trace that does not heat up can still lose tens of millivolts.
    • Forgetting creepage distances in the mains area. The safety distances are not those of the board manufacturer.
    • Designing without the manufacturer's rules. A spacing that cannot be manufactured is discovered only when ordering the board.

    07Self-assessment

    What width does IPC-2221 require for 1 A on an external 1 oz layer with a 10 °C rise?

    About 0.30 mm, 12 mil.

    What happens to the width if 2 oz copper is used?

    It is halved: the required cross-section is the same.

    What is the difference between clearance and creepage distance?

    The first is measured in a straight line through the air; the second, following the surface of the insulator.

    What does IP67 indicate?

    Dust-tight and protected against temporary immersion in water.
