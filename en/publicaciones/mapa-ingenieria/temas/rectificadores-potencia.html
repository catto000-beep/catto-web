    Power rectifiers

    With a 30° delay, a three-phase thyristor bridge connected to 400 V delivers 468 V instead of 540, and its power factor drops from 0.95 to 0.83. Regulating the voltage with the firing angle consumes reactive power.

    01Uncontrolled rectifiers

    A diode rectifier delivers a fixed DC voltage, which depends only on the line. With a highly inductive load, the output current is nearly constant and each diode conducts for a portion of the cycle:

    ConfigurationAverage voltagePulses per cycle
    Single-phase bridge\( \frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi} V \approx 0.90\,V \)2
    Three-phase bridge (six-pulse)\( \frac{3\sqrt2}{\pi} V_{LL} \approx 1.35\,V_{LL} \)6
    Two bridges with a wye-delta transformerTwice as much, with less ripple12

    With a capacitor at the output, on the other hand, the line current is a series of narrow peaks near the voltage maximum: this is how the rectifiers of nearly all electronic equipment work, and how they pollute the grid with harmonics, a topic of power quality. Active power factor correction is covered in switching power supplies.

    02Controlled rectifiers

    By replacing the diodes with thyristors, each one is triggered with a delay \( \alpha \) relative to the instant at which a diode would start to conduct. In a three-phase bridge with an inductive load:

    \[ V_{dc} = \frac{3\sqrt2}{\pi} V_{LL} \cos\alpha - \frac{3}{\pi}\,\omega L_s I_d \] The second term is the commutation drop: the line inductance \( L_s \) prevents the current from passing instantaneously from one thyristor to the next, and during an angle \( \mu \) two conduct at the same time. Between 90° and about 150° the average voltage is negative: the bridge works as an inverter and returns energy to the line, if there is a source on the DC side, such as a braking motor.

    The current of each phase is a nearly rectangular 120° wave, shifted by \( \alpha \) relative to its voltage. It contains the harmonics of order \( 6k \pm 1 \) —5, 7, 11, 13…— with amplitudes close to \( 1/h \) of the fundamental, and its power factor is approximately \( 0.955 \cos\alpha \).

    03Applications

    Controlled rectifiers feed DC motors (DC machines), electrolysis plants, furnaces and high-power battery chargers, and are the basis of high-voltage direct-current transmission, where one bridge rectifies at one end and another, with α close to 150°, inverts at the other. For small and medium powers they were replaced by diode rectifiers followed by a switching converter, or by active rectifiers with transistors, which draw sinusoidal current with unity power factor.

    04In the laboratory

    Activity 1 · Single-phase bridge

    With a half-controlled bridge on a resistive and an inductive load, measure the average voltage as a function of the firing angle and compare it with the formula.

    Activity 2 · Harmonics

    With a power quality analyzer, measure the current spectrum of a three-phase bridge and verify orders 5, 7, 11 and 13.

    Activity 3 · Commutation

    Add inductances in series with the line and observe the notches in the output voltage.

    05Common mistakes

    • Forgetting the commutation drop. With a weak grid, the average voltage is considerably lower than the ideal one.
    • Pushing the firing angle close to 180° in inverter mode. The outgoing thyristor does not manage to turn off and the bridge fails.
    • Confusing the displacement factor with the power factor. Harmonics lower the power factor even if cos φ is high.
    • Applying the inductive-load formulas to a resistive load. With a resistive load and α greater than 60° the current becomes discontinuous and the average voltage is different.

    06Self-assessment

    What average voltage does a three-phase diode bridge give on 400 V?

    \( 1.35 \cdot 400 \approx 540 \) V.

    What firing angle gives 270 V, with no line inductance?

    60°, because cos 60° = 0.5.

    What harmonics does the current of a six-pulse bridge have?

    Those of order 6k ± 1: 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19…

    What is needed for the bridge to work as an inverter?

    An energy source on the DC side that sustains the current, and α between 90° and about 150°.
