    MOS transistor: long channel, short channel and scaling

    With a 30 nm channel, the textbook MOS formula predicts five times more current than the transistor delivers. And the supply voltage of processors does not go below 0.7 V because of a thermodynamic limit: 60 mV per decade.

    Threshold voltage Short channel Velocity saturation Dennard scaling FinFET

    01The MOS capacitor and inversion

    The MOS transistor is a capacitor with a semiconductor as one of its plates: a conducting gate, a very thin oxide and a P-type silicon substrate. A positive voltage on the gate first pushes holes away from the surface (depletion) and, if it keeps rising, attracts so many electrons that the surface of the P-type silicon starts to behave as N-type: this is inversion. That layer of electrons, only a few nanometers thick, is the channel that joins the N⁺ source and drain regions.

    The gate voltage at which inversion appears is the threshold voltage:

    \[ V_{th} = V_{FB} + 2\phi_F + \frac{\sqrt{2\, \varepsilon_s\, q\, N_A\, (2\phi_F)}}{C_{ox}} \] \[ C_{ox} = \frac{\varepsilon_{ox}}{t_{ox}} \qquad \phi_F = V_T \ln \frac{N_A}{n_i} \] \( V_{FB} \) is the flat-band voltage, which depends on the gate material and on the oxide charges. It is written \( V_{th} \) so as not to confuse it with the thermal voltage \( V_T \).

    With a 2 nm oxide, \( C_{ox} = 17.3 \) fF/µm²: almost the whole history of the MOS is that of making this capacitor larger and larger per unit area, because the channel charge, and with it the current, is proportional to \( C_{ox} \).

    02The long-channel model

    With an overdrive voltage \( V_{ov} = V_{GS} - V_{th} \), the channel charge per unit area is \( C_{ox} V_{ov} \), and the electrons drift from source to drain with the channel mobility. Integrating along the channel gives the quadratic model:

    \[ I_D = \mu_n C_{ox} \frac{W}{L} \left( V_{ov} V_{DS} - \frac{V_{DS}^2}{2} \right) \qquad V_{DS} < V_{ov} \] \[ I_D = \frac{\mu_n C_{ox}}{2} \frac{W}{L}\, V_{ov}^2 \,(1 + \lambda V_{DS}) \qquad V_{DS} \geq V_{ov} \] In saturation the channel pinches off next to the drain; \( \lambda \) models how the pinch-off point moves toward the source as \( V_{DS} \) rises.

    For small signals, \( g_m = \sqrt{2\, \mu_n C_{ox} (W/L)\, I_D} = 2 I_D / V_{ov} \). Compared with the bipolar, \( g_m / I_D = 2/V_{ov} \) versus \( 1/V_T \): with \( V_{ov} = 0.2 \) V the MOS gives 10 S/A and the bipolar 38.7. For the same current, the MOS amplifies less.

    Below threshold it is not zero

    With \( V_{GS} < V_{th} \) the current does not disappear: it falls exponentially, as in a bipolar, \( I_D \propto e^{V_{GS}/(n V_T)} \). The subthreshold slope \( S = n V_T \ln 10 \) cannot go below 60 mV per decade at room temperature. For a transistor that is off to conduct a million times less than when it is on, a threshold of at least 360 mV is needed, and that number is the reason that the supply voltage of processors stopped falling near 0.7 V.

    03Short channel

    When the channel length drops to hundreds of nanometers, the quadratic model goes wrong for several reasons at once:

    EffectWhat happens
    Velocity saturationThe field along the channel exceeds \( 10^4 \) V/cm and the electrons stop accelerating: the current starts to grow with \( V_{ov} \) instead of \( V_{ov}^2 \), and no longer improves as \( 1/L \)
    Drain-induced barrier lowering (DIBL)The drain, very close to the source, lowers the channel barrier: the threshold falls as \( V_{DS} \) rises and the off-state current goes up
    Threshold roll-off with \( L \)Part of the depletion charge under the gate is supported by the source and drain, and the gate has less control
    Hot carriersHigh-energy electrons become embedded in the oxide and the transistor degrades with use
    Gate tunneling leakageWith oxides thinner than 2 nm electrons tunnel through it; SiO₂ was replaced by hafnium oxide, with a higher dielectric constant, which gives the same \( C_{ox} \) with more thickness

    Velocity saturation is incorporated into the model with a critical field \( E_c = 2 v_{sat} / \mu_n \): the saturation current becomes

    \[ I_{D,sat} = \frac{W\, C_{ox}\, v_{sat}\, V_{ov}^2}{V_{ov} + E_c L} \] \[ V_{DS,sat} = \frac{V_{ov}\, E_c L}{V_{ov} + E_c L} \] If \( E_c L \gg V_{ov} \) the quadratic model is recovered; if \( E_c L \ll V_{ov} \), the current is \( W C_{ox} v_{sat} V_{ov} \) and no longer depends on \( L \).

    04Dennard scaling and its end

    In 1974 Robert Dennard and his colleagues at IBM showed that if all the dimensions and all the voltages of a MOS transistor are divided by the same factor \( \kappa \), and the doping levels are multiplied by it, the transistor works the same and improves in every respect:

    QuantityScales as
    Dimensions \( L, W, t_{ox} \) and voltages\( 1/\kappa \)
    Doping\( \kappa \)
    Current per transistor\( 1/\kappa \)
    Gate delay\( 1/\kappa \)
    Power per transistor\( 1/\kappa^2 \)
    Transistors per area\( \kappa^2 \)
    Power per areaConstant

    That last row is what sustained Moore's law for thirty years: chips with twice as many transistors, faster, that gave off the same heat. It broke down around 2005, when the threshold voltage could no longer keep falling because of the 60 mV per decade limit and with it the supply voltage stalled. Since then the power per area grows with each generation, clock frequencies have stayed near 3 to 5 GHz, and the performance gains have come from adding more cores, not from making them faster.

    05The technologies that followed

    TechnologyIntroductionWhat it solves
    Strained siliconIntel, 90 nm, 2003Deforms the channel lattice to raise the mobility
    High-k dielectric and metal gateIntel, 45 nm, 2007More \( C_{ox} \) without tunneling leakage
    FinFET (tri-gate)Intel, 22 nm, 2011The channel is a fin surrounded by the gate on three sides: better control, less DIBL
    Gate-all-around (nanosheets)Samsung, 3 nm, 2022The gate completely surrounds the channel

    Node names (“7 nm”, “3 nm”) stopped being a physical measurement more than a decade ago: they are commercial labels for the generation, and the actual gate length is considerably larger.

    07In the lab

    Activity 1 · Threshold and transconductance parameter

    With a small-signal MOSFET (2N7000 or BS170), measure \( I_D \) in saturation for five values of \( V_{GS} \). Plot \( \sqrt{I_D} \) against \( V_{GS} \): the line crosses the axis at \( V_{th} \) and its slope gives the transconductance parameter.

    Activity 2 · The subthreshold region

    With the same transistor, measure currents between 10 nA and 10 µA with \( V_{GS} \) below the threshold and obtain the slope in mV per decade. Compare it with the 60 mV limit.

    Activity 3 · Simulating the short channel

    In a simulator with BSIM models of a 180 nm technology, plot \( I_D \) against \( V_{GS} \) in saturation for \( L \) of 180 nm and 2 µm. Verify which one follows a parabola and which one a straight line.

    08Common mistakes

    • Using the quadratic model in a short-channel transistor. It overestimates the current by a factor of several.
    • Confusing \( V_{th} \) with \( V_T \). One is the threshold, hundreds of millivolts; the other the thermal voltage, 25.85 mV.
    • Believing that a transistor that is off does not conduct. Subthreshold current is most of the standby consumption of a modern chip.
    • Reading the node name as a length. “3 nm” is a commercial label.
    • Handling gates without protection. An electrostatic discharge punctures the oxide.

    09Self-assessment

    What is inversion in a MOS capacitor?

    The accumulation, under the gate, of so many minority carriers that the surface of the substrate changes type and forms a conducting channel.

    In a long channel, how does the saturation current change when \( V_{ov} \) is doubled? And with extreme velocity saturation?

    In a long channel it is quadrupled; with extreme velocity saturation, it is doubled.

    Why can the subthreshold slope not be less than 60 mV per decade at 300 K?

    Because the current is carried by carriers that surmount a barrier through thermal agitation, with a Boltzmann distribution: \( V_T \ln 10 = 59.5 \) mV is the minimum per decade.

    According to Dennard, what happens to the power per area when scaling?

    It stays constant, because the power of each transistor falls as \( 1/\kappa^2 \) and the number per area rises as \( \kappa^2 \).

    What problem did the high-k dielectric solve?

    The tunneling leakage of very thin oxides: it gives the same capacitance per area with a greater physical thickness.
