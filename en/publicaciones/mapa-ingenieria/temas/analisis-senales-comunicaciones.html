    Signals and systems in communications

    A rectangular pulse leaves 5 % of its energy outside twice its null-to-null width. A raised-cosine pulse of the same duration leaves 100 times less: the pulse shape decides how much it invades its neighbors.

    Bandwidth Parseval Linear phase Equalizer Intermodulation

    01Thinking in frequency

    In communications almost everything is reasoned in the frequency domain: what part of the spectrum a signal occupies, what a channel lets through, where an interference falls. The tools are those of Signals and Systems —the series and the Fourier transform, their properties, the spectra and Parseval's relation—, applied to concrete questions: how much bandwidth a signal needs and what a channel does to it.

    One principle runs through the whole subject: the duration and the bandwidth of a signal are inversely related. A pulse of duration \( T \) occupies a bandwidth on the order of \( 1/T \). Transmitting faster requires more spectrum.

    02How wide is a bandwidth?

    Except in ideal cases, the spectrum of a real signal does not end at any frequency: it decays, but never reaches zero. That is why there are several definitions of bandwidth, and one has to say which one is being used:

    DefinitionWhat it measures
    −3 dBUp to where the power spectral density falls to half
    Null-to-nullBetween the first zeros of the spectrum on either side of the carrier. In baseband, up to the first zero: for a rectangular pulse, \( 1/T \); the modulated signal occupies \( 2/T \) null-to-null
    Fractional power (99 %)The band that contains 99 % of the energy: the one used by regulatory bodies
    Noise equivalentThat of an ideal rectangular filter that passes the same noise power
    \[ E = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |x(t)|^2\, dt = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |X(f)|^2\, df \] By Parseval, energy can be counted in time or in frequency. The fraction contained in a band is the integral of \( |X(f)|^2 \) over that band divided by the total energy.

    03Distortionless transmission

    A channel does not distort a signal if the output is an attenuated and delayed copy of the input, \( y(t) = K\,x(t - t_0) \). In frequency, this requires two things over the whole band of the signal:

    \[ |H(f)| = K \] \[ \arg H(f) = -2\pi f\, t_0 \] Constant magnitude and linear phase: all the components are attenuated equally and delayed by the same amount. If the first condition fails there is amplitude distortion; if the second fails, phase distortion, which does not change the power spectrum but does change the shape of the signal.

    The ear is not very sensitive to phase distortion, but digital data is: if the components of a pulse arrive with different delays, the pulse spreads and invades its neighbors (intersymbol interference). That is why modems and digital receivers have equalizers, filters that compensate for the magnitude and phase of the channel.

    04Nonlinear systems

    A saturated amplifier or a mixer is not linear, and generates frequencies that were not at the input. With two tones \( f_1 \) and \( f_2 \) at the input, harmonics and intermodulation products appear at \( m f_1 \pm n f_2 \). The third-order ones, \( 2f_1 - f_2 \) and \( 2f_2 - f_1 \), are the most harmful: they fall right next to the signals, inside the same channel, and no filter separates them. The linearity of the amplifiers of a transmitter or of the front end of a receiver is specified with the third-order intercept point.

    06Common mistakes

    • Giving a bandwidth without saying which one. At −3 dB and at 99 % they can differ by several times.
    • Believing that a flat-magnitude filter does not distort. If its phase is not linear, it deforms the pulses.
    • Using rectangular pulses in a band-limited channel. Their spectrum decays slowly and spills over into the neighboring channels.
    • Forgetting intermodulation when raising the power. It grows three times faster than the signal, in decibels.

    07Self-assessment

    What bandwidth up to the first null does a 1 µs rectangular pulse have, in baseband?

    1 MHz.

    What fraction of the energy of a rectangular pulse falls inside the first null?

    About 90 %.

    Where do the third-order intermodulation products of 100 and 101 MHz fall?

    At 99 and 102 MHz.

    What phase condition does distortionless transmission require?

    Phase linear with frequency: constant group delay.
