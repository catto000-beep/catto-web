    Capacitance and capacitors

    A 10 µF X7R ceramic can end up below 3 µF when 10 V is applied to it, and that does not appear in the tolerance printed on it. It is the cause of power supplies that oscillate and filters that do not filter.

    Capacitance Dielectrics Dielectric strength ESR RC transient

    01What capacitance is

    Two conductors separated by an insulator store charge when a potential difference is applied to them. Capacitance measures how much charge they store per volt, and it depends only on the geometry and on the material in between: not on the voltage or on the charge.

    \[ C = \frac{Q}{V} \qquad\qquad C = \varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\frac{A}{d} \ \text{ for the parallel-plate capacitor} \] A farad is enormous: a 1 F parallel-plate capacitor with air and a 1 mm gap would need plates about 10.6 kilometers on a side (113 km² of area). That is why we work in microfarads, nanofarads and picofarads.

    The formula gives the only three ways to increase capacitance: more area, less separation or a better dielectric. The whole history of capacitor technology is a tug-of-war with those three variables, and each family chose one.

    \[ U = \tfrac{1}{2}CV^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}QV \qquad\qquad u = \tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon E^2 \] The stored energy, and the energy density per unit volume. The second form makes clear that the energy is in the field, not in the plates.

    02The dielectric, and its limit

    When an insulator is placed between the plates, its molecules polarize and create an opposing field that weakens the original one. With the same charge there is less voltage, so the capacitance goes up by a factor of \( \varepsilon_r \), the relative permittivity.

    Dielectric\( \varepsilon_r \)Strength in kV/mmWhere it is used

    Dielectric strength is the maximum field the material withstands before it suddenly conducts. Exceeding it does not degrade the capacitor: it punctures it, and the failure is permanent. The last row shows the central compromise: barium titanate has a very high permittivity and a poor strength, and that is why high-capacitance ceramics are low-voltage.

    Lab · parallel-plate capacitor

    Build the capacitor by choosing the dielectric, area and separation, and apply a voltage to it. Capacitance, charge, energy and the internal field are calculated, and you are warned if the field exceeds the strength of the material.

    03The capacitors you actually buy

    The ideal capacitor of the textbooks does not exist. The real one has series resistance, series inductance and leakage, and its capacitance changes with temperature, with voltage and with time.

    The X7R that does not have what it says

    A 10 µF, 16 V X7R ceramic can end up at less than 3 µF when 10 V is applied to it, and that does not appear in the printed tolerance: it is the voltage coefficient, characteristic of barium titanate, whose permittivity drops as it polarizes. It is the cause of switching power supplies that oscillate and of filters that do not filter, and the error is invisible when measuring the capacitor with an instrument that tests it at one millivolt. The practical solution is to choose a rated voltage two or three times higher than the working voltage.

    And on the losses side there is the ESR, the equivalent series resistance, which is what makes a capacitor heat up. In a switching power supply, ripple current flows continuously through the output capacitor, and the power \( I^2 R_{ESR} \) heats it from the inside: that accelerates the drying out of the electrolyte, which in turn raises the ESR. It is the most common failure mechanism of a power supply, and the one the kinetics unit explained with Arrhenius.

    04Combinations and the transient

    \[ C_{\parallel} = C_1 + C_2 + \ldots \qquad\qquad \frac{1}{C_{series}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \ldots \] The opposite of resistors, and for the same reason: in parallel the areas add, and in series the separations add.

    And charging it through a resistor produces the transient that governs half of a digital circuit:

    \[ v(t) = V\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right) \qquad \tau = RC \] In one time constant it reaches 63%, and in five 99.3%. That \( RC \) is what limits the speed of a digital line: the capacitance of the trace and the output resistance form this very circuit.

    05In the lab

    Activity 1 · Measuring the voltage coefficient

    Measure the capacitance of a 10 µF X7R ceramic with an LCR meter and then in a circuit with increasing DC bias voltage, obtaining the capacitance from the cutoff frequency of an RC filter. Plot capacitance against applied voltage.

    Activity 2 · The time constant

    Charge a known capacitor through different resistors and record the curve with the oscilloscope. Measure \( \tau \) at 63% and compare with \( RC \). Estimate the error introduced by the input impedance of the probe.

    Activity 3 · ESR and temperature

    Measure the ESR of new electrolytics and of others salvaged from old equipment. Relate it to the condition of the equipment and estimate the heating with a ripple current of 1 A rms.

    06Common mistakes

    • Believing capacitance depends on the applied voltage. In the model it does not; in a real ceramic, it does a lot.
    • Adding capacitances in series. In series the reciprocals add, the opposite of resistors.
    • Using \( U = QV \) without the medium. The stored energy is \( \tfrac{1}{2}QV \).
    • Reversing an electrolytic. It is polarized, and backwards it is destroyed, often noisily.
    • Ignoring the ESR. It is what heats the capacitor and what ends up killing it.
    • Choosing the rated voltage with no margin. Two or three times the working voltage is advisable, especially with ceramics.

    07Self-assessment

    What does the capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor depend on?

    On the area, the separation and the permittivity of the dielectric. Not on the voltage or the charge.

    What happens if the dielectric strength is exceeded?

    The material suddenly conducts and the capacitor is punctured. The failure is permanent.

    Why can a 10 µF X7R end up with 3 µF?

    Because of the voltage coefficient of barium titanate: its permittivity drops as it polarizes.

    How are capacitances combined in parallel?

    They add, because it is equivalent to adding areas.

    What fraction of the final voltage does an RC reach in one time constant?

    63%. In five time constants it reaches 99.3%.

    What is ESR and why does it matter?

    The equivalent series resistance. With the ripple current it dissipates \( I^2R \) and heats the capacitor from the inside, which accelerates its degradation.
