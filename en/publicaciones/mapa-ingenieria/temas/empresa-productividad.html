    The company, productivity and standards

    A line with 90 % availability, 86 % performance and 97 % good parts seems to be working well, but its overall effectiveness is 76 %: of 450 planned minutes, only 340 produce good parts at the ideal speed.

    Production system Organizational structure Productivity Standards Overall equipment effectiveness (OEE)

    01The company as a production system

    An industrial company transforms inputs —materials, energy, labor, capital, information— into products and services that it sells in a market. It can be seen as a system with inputs, a transformation process, outputs and control loops: planning sets objectives, measurement compares what was achieved and management corrects. Its legal forms, such as the corporation or the limited liability company, were covered in commercial companies.

    StructureHow it groupsAdvantage and limit
    FunctionalBy specialty: production, engineering, sales, financeTechnical efficiency; coordination between areas is slow
    DivisionalBy product, customer or regionEach division answers for its result; duplicates resources
    MatrixBy function and by project at the same timeFlexible; each person has two bosses

    02Productivity

    Productivity relates what is produced to the resources used. It can be partial —parts per labor hour, per kWh, per kilogram of material— or total, when outputs are compared with all the inputs valued in money. It is not the same as output: producing more by using proportionally more resources does not improve productivity. It is improved with better methods, better technology, less waste, better quality and better-trained people.

    03Standards

    A standard is an agreed reference that makes it possible to plan, compare and control. In a company there are time standards, the time a qualified worker needs for a task with a defined method; product standards, the specifications and technical norms it must meet; materials and cost standards; and management standards, such as the ISO 9001 quality or ISO 14001 environmental standards. Without standards there is no way to know whether a result is good or bad.

    04Overall equipment effectiveness

    Overall equipment effectiveness (OEE), born with total productive maintenance in Japan, measures what fraction of the planned time is converted into good parts at the ideal speed. It breaks down into three factors:

    \[ OEE = D \cdot R \cdot C \] Availability \( D \) discounts unplanned stops —failures, tool changes—; performance \( R \) compares what was produced with what would have been produced at the ideal cycle during the operating time, and discounts minor stops and reduced speed; quality \( C \) is the fraction of good parts. They are the six big losses grouped in pairs.

    06Common mistakes

    • Confusing output with productivity. Producing more is not better if proportionally more is used.
    • Looking at only one factor of overall effectiveness. A machine that is always available but slow, or that produces defects, yields little.
    • Using the actual cycle as the ideal cycle. It hides the speed losses.
    • Setting standards without a defined method. The standard time is valid for one method; if the method changes, it has to be measured again.

    07Self-assessment

    With the initial lab values, what are D, R and C?

    \(D = 405/450 = 0.90\); \(R = 700\cdot 30\text{ s} / 405\text{ min} = 0.864\); \(C = 680/700 = 0.971\).

    And the overall effectiveness?

    0.756: equivalent to 680 good parts at 30 s each, 340 minutes of the 450 planned.

    What advantage and what limit does a matrix structure have?

    It combines specialties in each project; in exchange, each person reports to two bosses.

    What is a standard time?

    The time a qualified worker needs, at a normal pace and with a defined method, plus allowances for rest and personal needs.
