    Fluid dynamics

    The flow that enters is the flow that leaves, and where the fluid speeds up the pressure drops. With those two ideas you can explain the wing of an airplane, the flowmeter and the fan of a heat sink.

    Flow rate Continuity Bernoulli Viscosity Reynolds

    01Flow rate and continuity

    The flow rate is the volume that passes through a cross-section per unit time, \(Q = A \cdot v\), in \(\text{m}^3\text{/s}\). If the fluid is incompressible and there are no leaks, what enters at one end leaves at the other:

    \[ A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2 = Q \] Where the pipe narrows, the fluid speeds up. This is why, when you partly cover the end of a hose, the jet comes out faster.
    The same equation, in a circuit

    The continuity equation is the conservation of mass, and its electrical twin is Kirchhoff’s current law: the current entering a node is the current leaving it. Flow rate and current play the same role; so do pressure and voltage.

    02Bernoulli’s equation

    For an ideal fluid —incompressible, inviscid and in steady flow—, the energy per unit volume is conserved along a streamline:

    \[ p + \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 + \rho g h = \mathrm{constant} \] The three terms are energy per unit volume: pressure, kinetic and potential.
    The consequence that is hard to believe

    Where the fluid moves faster, the pressure is lower. It is not magic: the fluid sped up because a pressure difference pushed it, and that energy came out of the pressure term. This is where the lift of a wing, the atomizer, the shower curtain that clings to you, and the fact that two ships sailing side by side attract each other come from.

    03What is measured with Bernoulli

    InstrumentWhat it doesUsed in
    Venturi tubeMeasures flow rate from the pressure drop at a throatIndustrial plants, carburetors
    Pitot tubeMeasures velocity by comparing total and static pressureAircraft, wind tunnels
    Orifice plateSimpler and more dissipative version of the VenturiFlow measurement in piping
    Torricelli’s theorem\( v = \sqrt{2 \cdot g \cdot h} \) at an orificeDraining tanks

    The flowmeter of an industrial process is usually a Venturi with two pressure sensors and a microcontroller that calculates \(Q = A_2 \cdot \sqrt{2\Delta p/(\rho(1-(A_2/A_1)^2))}\). That is where this subject and instrumentation electronics come together.

    04Viscosity, Reynolds and head loss

    No real fluid is ideal: viscosity dissipates energy and makes the pressure drop along a straight pipe. The Reynolds number compares inertial forces with viscous ones and tells you what kind of flow there is:

    \[ \operatorname{Re} = \frac{\rho v D}{\mu} \] It is dimensionless. Below 2300 the flow is laminar; above 4000, turbulent.
    RegimeReynoldsHow it behaves
    Laminar< 2300Orderly layers; the loss grows with v
    Transition2300 to 4000Unstable
    Turbulent> 4000Chaotic mixing; the loss grows with \(v^2\)

    The head loss in a straight section is calculated with the Darcy–Weisbach equation, \(\Delta p = f \cdot (L/D) \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot \rho \cdot v^2\), where the factor \(f\) comes from the Moody chart as a function of Reynolds and of the roughness of the pipe. That is the calculation that decides the size of a pump.

    Cavitation

    If at some point the absolute pressure falls below the vapor pressure of the liquid, bubbles form and collapse violently downstream. It erodes pump impellers and propellers, and sounds as if stones were flowing through. In a heavily constricted Venturi it is easy to reach that condition.

    05What it is good for in electronics

    • Air cooling. Choosing a fan requires knowing the necessary flow rate and the back pressure of the enclosure: the fan curve crosses the system resistance curve.
    • Liquid cooling. In high-power equipment, the pump is sized from the flow rate and head loss of the circuit.
    • Flowmeters. Turbine, Venturi, ultrasonic (transit-time or Doppler), and electromagnetic based on Faraday’s law: all of them are electronic instrumentation.
    • Hot-wire anemometers. They measure air speed from the power needed to keep a wire hot: a forced-convection problem solved with a Wheatstone bridge.
    • Enclosure testing. The protection rating against water jets is specified in flow rate and pressure.

    06In the lab

    Activity 1 · Verifying continuity

    With a hose and two nozzles of different diameter, measure the flow rate by volume and time, and calculate the exit velocity in each case. Check that the product \(A \cdot v\) is maintained.

    Activity 2 · Torricelli with a bottle

    Drill holes in a bottle at different heights and measure the range of the jet. Verify that the exit velocity follows \(\sqrt{2gh}\) and that the maximum range occurs with the hole at mid-height.

    Activity 3 · Curve of a fan

    Measure the flow rate of an enclosure fan against different restrictions and plot its curve of flow rate against back pressure. Cross it with the system curve to find the actual operating point.

    07Common mistakes

    • Applying Bernoulli between points that are not on the same streamline, or with a pump or turbine in between without adding its term.
    • Using Bernoulli in highly viscous flow and ignoring the head loss.
    • Believing that more velocity means more pressure. It is exactly the opposite.
    • Confusing volumetric flow rate with mass flow rate. In gases the difference matters, because the density changes.
    • Computing Reynolds with mixed units. It is dimensionless: if the number does not come out unitless, there is a mistake.
    • Forgetting that air is a fluid and treating it differently from water.

    08Self-assessment

    A 50 mm pipe carries 2 L/s. What is the velocity of the water?

    \(A = \pi \cdot 0.025^2 = 1.96 \cdot 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2\), so \(v = 0.002/(1.96 \cdot 10^{-3}) \approx\) \(1.02\text{ m/s}\).

    If the diameter is reduced to half, by how much does the velocity increase?

    The area drops to a quarter, so the velocity quadruples.

    Is that 50 mm pipe with water at 1 m/s laminar or turbulent?

    \(\mathrm{Re} = 1000 \cdot 1 \cdot 0.05/10^{-3} = 50{,}000\): turbulent, by a wide margin. With water, laminar flow in pipes is rare.

    At what velocity does water leave an orifice 2 m below the surface?

    \(v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.8 \cdot 2} \approx\) \(6.3\text{ m/s}\), the same as a body would have after falling that height.

    Why can the roof of a house be blown upward in a strong wind?

    Because the air accelerates as it passes over it and the pressure above drops below that inside. The difference, multiplied by the area, is an enormous force.

    What does a Pitot tube measure?

    The difference between total and static pressure, from which the velocity follows: \(v = \sqrt{2\Delta p/\rho}\). It is the airspeed instrument of an aircraft.
