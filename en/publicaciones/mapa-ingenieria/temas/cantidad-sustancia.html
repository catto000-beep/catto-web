    Notation and amount of substance

    Counting atoms seems impossible, yet it is done every day: a doping of 10¹⁶ atoms per cubic centimeter is two parts per million, and the resistivity of a wafer depends on that number.

    Mole Avogadro Molar mass Doping Parts per million

    01The mole: a very large dozen

    Atoms are so small that counting them one by one is useless. Chemistry uses a package of fixed size, just as the dozen is used for eggs, only vastly larger:

    \[ 1\ \text{mol} = 6.022\times10^{23}\ \text{entities} \qquad (\text{Avogadro's number},\ N_A) \] Since 2019 that number has been an exact definition, like the speed of light: the mole is defined as that quantity, and not the other way around.

    The beauty of the number is that it was chosen so that the mass of one mole, in grams, coincides with the atomic mass in the periodic table. One mole of silicon weighs 28.09 g; one mole of copper, 63.55 g. That turns a balance into an atom counter.

    \[ n = \frac{m}{M}, \qquad N = n\,N_A \] With \( m \) the mass in grams and \( M \) the molar mass in g/mol. From the balance to the number of atoms, in two steps.

    02Saying how much of something there is

    Concentration units are chosen according to the order of magnitude of the problem. In solution chemistry, molarity is used; in materials, parts per million; in semiconductors, atoms per cubic centimeter.

    UnitDefinitionUsed in
    Molarity, Mmoles of solute / liter of solutionLaboratory solutions
    Molality, mmoles of solute / kg of solventColligative properties
    % by massg of solute / 100 g of solutionAlloys, commercial reagents
    ppm, ppbParts per million or per billionPurity of materials
    Atoms/cm³How many there are in one cubic centimeterDoping of semiconductors

    The last two are the ones in this field, and it pays to know how to convert from one to the other. All that is needed is a single piece of data: how many silicon atoms there are per cubic centimeter, which comes from its density and its molar mass.

    \[ N_{\text{Si}} = \frac{\rho\,N_A}{M} = \frac{2.33\ \mathrm{g/cm^3} \times 6.022\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{mol^{-1}}} {28.09\ \mathrm{g/mol}} \approx 5.0\times10^{22}\ \mathrm{atoms/cm^3} \] You need to have that number at hand: it is the reference against which any doping is measured.

    03From the mole to the resistivity of a wafer

    With the atomic density of silicon, a doping in atoms/cm³ translates immediately into parts per million. And since each dopant atom contributes one free carrier, that concentration determines the resistivity, which is what is finally measured with an ohmmeter.

    Lab · doping, parts per million and resistivity

    Move the dopant concentration and watch the three ways of saying the same thing, plus what the instrument reads. The scale is logarithmic because useful doping spans five orders of magnitude.

    04Formulas and nomenclature

    The empirical formula gives the simplest ratio between the atoms; the molecular formula, the actual number per molecule. Hydrogen peroxide is HO as an empirical formula and H₂O₂ as a molecular formula.

    The compounds that show up all the time in electronics are worth having identified:

    FormulaNameWhere it appears

    Si₃N₄Silicon nitridePassivation layer of integrated circuits

    The calculation that shows up in any process

    To oxidize silicon and form the gate of a MOSFET, the reaction is \( \mathrm{Si} + \mathrm{O_2} \to \mathrm{SiO_2} \). For each mole of silicon consumed, one mole of oxide is formed, but the oxide takes up more volume: the thickness ratio is 0.44 to 1. That is, to grow 100 nm of oxide, 44 nm of silicon is consumed, and the surface ends up lower than where it started. That calculation, which is pure stoichiometry, defines the geometry of a transistor.

    06Common mistakes

    • Confusing molar mass with atomic mass. They are the same number with different units: u per atom, g/mol per mole.
    • Mixing up molarity and molality. One divides by the volume of solution and the other by the mass of solvent.
    • Giving ppm without saying of what. It is not the same by mass as by number of atoms.
    • Using liters where the problem is in cm³. In semiconductors everything is counted per cubic centimeter.
    • Forgetting that the empirical formula does not identify the compound. Several different substances share the same one.

    07Self-assessment

    How many atoms are there in 28.09 g of silicon?

    One mole: \( 6.022\times10^{23} \). The molar mass is chosen precisely for that.

    How many silicon atoms are there per cm³?

    About \( 5.0\times10^{22} \), which comes from the density times Avogadro's number divided by the molar mass.

    How many ppm does a doping of \( 5\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}} \) correspond to?

    To one: \( 5\times10^{16}/5\times10^{22} = 10^{-6} \).

    What happens to the resistivity if the doping is doubled?

    It falls to about half: there are twice as many carriers. The proportion is not exact because the mobility also drops as the doping increases.

    How much silicon is consumed when 100 nm of oxide grows?

    44 nm. The ratio is 0.44 and it comes from the densities and the molar masses.

    Empirical or molecular formula of hydrogen peroxide?

    Empirical HO, molecular H₂O₂. The empirical formula only gives the ratio.
