    The National Constitution

    The right to practice a profession, to own an invention or to enjoy a healthy environment is not granted by a statute: it is in the Constitution, and no statute can override it.

    Supremacy Federalism Rights and guarantees Article 14 bis Amparo

    01The supreme law

    The Constitución Nacional (National Constitution) is the rule that organizes the State and sets out the rights of the inhabitants. It was enacted in 1853 and amended in 1860, 1866, 1898, 1957 and 1994. The 1994 reform was the most extensive: it added the rights to a healthy environment and of consumers, the amparo action (a fast-track judicial remedy), the Council of the Judiciary (Consejo de la Magistratura) and constitutional rank for a series of human rights treaties.

    Its central feature is supremacy (Article 31): the Constitution, the laws of Congress made in pursuance of it, and the treaties are the supreme law of the Nation, and the provinces are bound to respect it even if their own rules say otherwise. Any rule that contradicts it is invalid.

    02How it organizes the State

    Article 1 adopts the representative, republican and federal form. Each word has consequences:

    FeatureWhat it means
    RepresentativeThe people govern through their elected representatives
    RepublicanSeparation of powers, fixed terms of office, publicity of government acts, accountability of officials
    FederalThe provinces retain all the power they did not delegate to the Nation (Article 121), enact their own constitution and elect their own authorities

    The three branches are the Legislative, a two-chamber Congress that makes the laws; the Executive, headed by the president, who carries them out and issues regulations implementing them; and the Judicial, with the Supreme Court at its head, which resolves disputes.

    Why the professional license is provincial

    Congress enacts the substantive codes —civil and commercial, criminal, labor— for the whole country (Article 75, clause 12). But the oversight of the practice of professions, the so-called police power, stayed in the hands of the provinces. That is why each province has its own law on the practice of engineering and its own council or association, and an engineer has to register where they work, as explained in the unit on professional practice.

    No right is absolute. Article 14 makes clear that they are exercised “in accordance with the laws that regulate their exercise” (our translation), and Article 28 sets the limit on those laws: they may not alter the rights they regulate. Requiring a degree and a professional license to sign drawings is a reasonable regulation of the right to work; prohibiting a licensed professional from practicing without cause would not be.

    04Treaties and judicial review

    The 1994 reform gave constitutional rank to a list of human rights treaties (Article 75, clause 22), among them the American Convention on Human Rights and the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights. Other treaties rank above statutes but below the Constitution.

    In Argentina judicial review (control de constitucionalidad) is diffuse: any judge, when deciding a specific case, may declare a rule unconstitutional and decline to apply it. The declaration is valid only for that case and does not repeal the rule; but when the Supreme Court does it, the other courts usually follow.

    05The guarantees

    Rights need tools to defend them. Article 43 establishes three:

    • Amparo: a fast action against an act of an authority or of a private party that infringes, in a manifestly arbitrary or unlawful way, a recognized right, when there is no more suitable judicial remedy.
    • Habeas data: allows you to find out what personal data appear in records or databases and to demand their correction or deletion. It is the basis of Law 25,326 on personal data protection, which applies to any system that stores data about individuals.
    • Habeas corpus: protects physical liberty against unlawful detention.

    08Common mistakes

    • Believing that constitutional rights are absolute. They are exercised in accordance with the laws that regulate them.
    • Assuming that a national license is valid across the whole country. Professional practice is regulated by each province.
    • Thinking that a judge who declares a law unconstitutional repeals it. The judge merely stops applying it in that case.
    • Forgetting habeas data when designing systems. Personal data have constitutional protection.

    09Self-assessment

    What does Article 31 establish?

    The supremacy of the Constitution, the national laws made in pursuance of it, and the treaties over provincial rules.

    Which article protects an inventor’s property?

    Article 17.

    Why does each province have its own law on the practice of engineering?

    Because the police power over professions was not delegated to the Nation and is retained by the provinces (Article 121).

    Which guarantee allows you to request the correction of personal data in a database?

    Habeas data, under Article 43.
