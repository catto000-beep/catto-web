    Voltage, current and power at low frequency

    An analog voltmeter of 20 kΩ/V measuring a 100 kΩ divider reads 4 V where there are 5. It is not out of calibration: connecting it changed the circuit.

    Loading effect Burden voltage Wattmeter Aron method Clamp meter

    01Every instrument modifies what it measures

    To measure a voltage, the voltmeter is connected in parallel and draws some current; to measure a current, the ammeter is inserted in series and adds resistance. In both cases the circuit changes when the instrument is connected, and what is measured is the modified circuit. This loading effect is a systematic error that can be calculated and, almost always, made negligible by choosing the instrument well.

    \[ V_m = V_{th}\, \frac{R_v}{R_v + R_{th}} \] \[ I_m = \frac{V_{th}}{R_{th} + R_a} \] Seen from the measurement points, the circuit is a Thévenin equivalent. The voltmeter, of resistance \( R_v \), forms a divider with \( R_{th} \); the ammeter, of resistance \( R_a \), adds in series. The error is negligible if \( R_v \gg R_{th} \) and \( R_a \ll R_{th} \).
    Lab · the loading effect of a voltmeter

    A divider of two equal resistors fed with 10 V: at the midpoint there are 5 V, with a Thévenin resistance equal to half of each resistor. Four voltmeters of different input resistance measure that point.

    The ammeter has its own effect: the burden voltage. On the 200 mA range, a typical multimeter has about 1 Ω between the shunt and the fuse. In a 5 V circuit with a load of 25 Ω, that lowers the current from 200 mA to 192 mA: a 3.8% error that no instrument accuracy can compensate.

    The moving-coil voltmeter is specified by its sensitivity in ohms per volt: a 20 kΩ/V one on the 10 V range has 200 kΩ of input resistance, and on the 1 V range, only 20 kΩ. The lower the range, the greater the loading effect.

    03Digital instruments

    A digital multimeter converts with a dual-slope or sigma-delta integrating ADC, which averages over a whole number of mains cycles and thus rejects the 50 Hz hum. Its DC input resistance is 10 MΩ on all ranges, and some offer more than 10 GΩ on the lowest ones. In AC there are two classes: average-responding meters calibrated for a sine wave, and true-RMS meters, which measure any waveform correctly within their bandwidth and crest factor. The difference is analyzed in non-sinusoidal signals.

    The uncertainty of the reading is evaluated as seen in errors and uncertainty.

    04Power measurement

    In DC, \( P = V I \), but the connection matters: if the voltmeter is placed directly across the load, the ammeter also measures the voltmeter’s current; if it is placed before the ammeter, it also measures the drop across it. The first error is relative to \( R_L / R_v \) and the second to \( R_a / R_L \): for low-resistance loads the voltmeter should go across the load, and for high ones, before the ammeter. The boundary is at \( R_L = \sqrt{R_a R_v} \).

    In AC, the active power is the average of the instantaneous product, which a wattmeter calculates directly:

    \[ P = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T v(t)\, i(t)\, dt \] \[ P = V_{rms}\, I_{rms} \cos\varphi \] The second expression holds only for sinusoidal voltage and current. Digital power analyzers sample the two signals and calculate the average of the product, which always holds, harmonics included.

    In three-phase three-wire systems two wattmeters are enough (the Aron method): the sum of their readings is the total power, with any load. If the load is balanced, moreover, \( \tan\varphi = \sqrt{3}\,(W_1 - W_2)/(W_1 + W_2) \).

    Activity 2 · Two connections for power

    Measure the power of a three-phase motor with two wattmeters and calculate the power factor.

    07Common mistakes

    • Measuring high-impedance circuits with an analog meter. The voltmeter becomes part of the circuit.
    • Forgetting the ammeter’s burden voltage. In low-voltage circuits it can be the largest source of error.
    • Calculating AC power as V times I. That is the apparent power; the power factor is missing.
    • Leaving the multimeter on the current range and measuring voltage. It is a short circuit through the shunt; the fuse, if it is the right one, saves it.

    08Self-assessment

    What does a 200 kΩ voltmeter read at a 5 V point with 50 kΩ Thévenin resistance?

    \( 5 \cdot 200 / 250 = 4 \) V: 20% less.

    What is the input resistance of a 20 kΩ/V voltmeter on the 50 V range?

    1 MΩ.

    With \( R_a = 0.1\ \Omega \) and \( R_v = 10\ \text{M}\Omega \), which connection is better for a 10 Ω load?

    \( \sqrt{0.1 \cdot 10^7} = 1000\ \Omega \): the load is smaller, so the voltmeter should go directly across it.

    Two wattmeters read 3 kW and 1 kW on a balanced three-phase load. What is the power factor?

    \( \tan\varphi = \sqrt 3 \cdot 2/4 = 0.866 \), \( \varphi = 40.9° \), \( \cos\varphi \approx 0.76 \).
