    The complex plane and its regions

    Complex numbers are not a trick: they are the natural tool for everything that oscillates. A phasor, an impedance and a pole are points of this plane, and the regions drawn in it tell you whether a circuit is stable.

    Complex plane Polar form Euler Regions Phasors

    01Why an electronics engineer needs complex numbers

    The word “imaginary” is a historical misfortune: it suggests that they do not exist. In electronics, the complex plane is the place where the most concrete things of the whole degree live.

    Object in the complex planeWhat it is in electronics
    A point in polar formA phasor: amplitude and phase of a sinusoidal signal
    A number \( R + jX \)An impedance: resistance and reactance
    Multiplying by \( e^{j\theta} \)Phase-shifting a signal by \( \theta \) radians
    A point in the left half-planeA stable pole: the response dies out
    The unit circleThe edge of stability of a discrete system
    The circle \( |\Gamma| = \text{const} \)A circle of the Smith chart

    This whole course is built on that plane: poles and zeros, the Laplace transform, the Z-transform and the frequency response are readings of points and regions inside it.

    02The three ways of writing the same thing

    \[ z = a + jb = r(\cos\theta + j\,\operatorname{sin}\theta) = r\,e^{j\theta} \] Rectangular, trigonometric and exponential. In engineering you write \( j \) and not \( i \), because \( i \) is already the current.
    ForUseBecause
    Adding and subtractingRectangularThe real and imaginary parts are added separately
    Multiplying and dividingPolar or exponentialThe moduli are multiplied and the angles are added
    Raising to a powerExponential\( z^n = r^n e^{jn\theta} \), De Moivre’s formula
    Differentiating and integratingExponentialThe exponential is its own derivative, up to a factor
    Euler’s formula
    \[ e^{j\theta} = \cos\theta + j\,\operatorname{sin}\theta \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{e^{j\theta} + e^{-j\theta}}{2} \qquad \operatorname{sin}\theta = \frac{e^{j\theta} - e^{-j\theta}}{2j} \]

    It is the bridge between what oscillates and what grows: it turns sines and cosines into exponentials, which are far easier to differentiate, integrate and multiply. The whole phasor method used in alternating current comes from here.

    03What each operation does in the plane

    Every operation with complex numbers is a geometric motion. Seeing it that way avoids memorizing formulas:

    OperationIn the planeWhere it appears
    AddingPlacing the vectors head to tailAdding two out-of-phase voltages
    Multiplying by \( r\,e^{j\theta} \)Scaling by \( r \) and rotating by \( \theta \)Passing through a network that attenuates and shifts phase
    Multiplying by \( j \)Rotating 90° without changing the sizeDifferentiating a sinusoid; inductive reactance
    ConjugatingReflecting across the real axisImpedance matching: \( Z_L = Z_S^* \)
    Inverting \( 1/z \)Inverting the modulus and changing the sign of the angleGoing from impedance to admittance
    SquaringDoubling the angle and squaring the modulusA mixer, which generates twice the frequency
    The n roots

    A complex number has n nth roots, spread over the vertices of a regular polygon inscribed in a circle of radius \( \sqrt[n]{r} \):

    \[ z_k = \sqrt[n]{r}\ e^{\,j\left(\frac{\theta + 2\pi k}{n}\right)}, \qquad k = 0, 1, \ldots, n-1 \]

    Those roots are the ones that appear when you solve the characteristic equation of a system, and their position in the plane decides whether the response oscillates, dies out or grows.

    04Regions: where an inequality draws a figure

    An inequality between complex numbers does not define an interval, as with the reals, but a region of the plane. Recognizing them by heart saves a lot of work:

    ConditionRegionWhat it means in the degree
    \( |z - z_0| = r \)Circle centered at \( z_0 \)A locus of constant modulus
    \( |z - z_0| \lt r \)Open diskNeighborhood of a point: the basis of limits
    \( r_1 \lt |z| \lt r_2 \)AnnulusRegion of convergence of the Z-transform
    \( \operatorname{Re}(z) \lt 0 \)Left half-planeStable zone of Laplace
    \( |z| \lt 1 \)Unit diskStable zone of the Z-transform
    \( |\arg z| \lt \alpha \)Angular sectorMinimum damping of a system

    The two highlighted rows are the reason this course starts with complex variable: the question “is this system stable?” is answered by looking at where its poles fall in the plane.

    05The phasor, which is all of this together

    A sinusoidal signal \( v(t) = V_p \cos(\omega t + \varphi) \) can be written as the real part of a rotating complex exponential:

    \[ v(t) = \operatorname{Re}\left\{ V_p\,e^{j(\omega t + \varphi)} \right\} = \operatorname{Re}\left\{ \underbrace{V_p e^{j\varphi}}_{\text{phasor}} \cdot e^{j\omega t} \right\} \] The phasor stores amplitude and phase; \( e^{j\omega t} \) is the rotation, which is common to all the signals in the circuit and cancels out.

    With that, a circuit with derivatives and integrals becomes an algebraic one: differentiating is multiplying by \( j\omega \) and integrating is dividing by \( j\omega \). From there come \( Z_L = j\omega L \) and \( Z_C = 1/(j\omega C) \), and Ohm’s law works again, now with complex numbers.

    The phasor assumes sinusoidal steady state

    It holds when everything oscillates at the same frequency and the transient has already died out. For switch-on, for a pulse or for an arbitrary signal, you need the more general tool studied later: Laplace, which is this same idea with an exponential that also grows or dies out, \( e^{st} \) with \( s = \sigma + j\omega \).

    06In the lab

    Activity 1 · Measuring a phasor

    With a generator and a two-channel oscilloscope, measure the amplitude and phase shift in an RC circuit. Draw the input phasor and the output phasor on graph paper and check the angle with \( \varphi = -\arctan(\omega RC) \).

    Activity 2 · The roots, on the screen

    Compute by hand the five fifth roots of unity and plot them. Check that they form a regular pentagon and that their sum is zero.

    Activity 3 · Impedance and admittance

    For a series RLC at three frequencies, compute \( Z \) in rectangular and in polar form, and its inverse \( Y = 1/Z \). Verify that inverting reflects the point across the unit circle and changes the sign of the angle, as in the lab on this page.

    07Common mistakes

    • Confusing \( j \) with \( i \). In electronics \( i \) is current; the imaginary unit is \( j \).
    • Adding in polar form. To add you have to convert to rectangular; only the product and the quotient are convenient in polar.
    • Losing the quadrant when computing the angle with \( \arctan(b/a) \): you have to look at the signs of \( a \) and \( b \), or use the two-argument function.
    • Mixing degrees and radians in the same calculation.
    • Forgetting that the nth root has n results and keeping only one.
    • Writing the phasor with the peak value when you had been working in RMS, or the other way around.

    08Self-assessment

    Write \( z = 3 + j4 \) in polar form.

    \( r = \sqrt{9+16} = 5 \) and \( \theta = \arctan(4/3) \approx 53.13^\circ \), that is, \( 5\,e^{j53.13^\circ} \).

    What does multiplying a phasor by \( j \) do to it?

    It rotates it 90° counterclockwise without changing its modulus. It is what differentiating a sinusoid does.

    What region is \( 1 \lt |z| \lt 2 \)?

    An annulus centered at the origin, between radii 1 and 2. It is the typical shape of a region of convergence in the Z-transform.

    Where do the poles of a continuous-time system have to be for it to be stable?

    In the left half-plane, with negative real part: that way the exponential \( e^{st} \) dies out instead of growing.

    How many cube roots does 8 have and where are they?

    Three, of modulus 2, spaced 120° apart: \( 2 \), \( 2e^{j120^\circ} \) and \( 2e^{j240^\circ} \). Only the first is real.

    Why does the conjugate appear in impedance matching?

    Because maximum power transfer occurs with \( Z_L = Z_S^* \): the same resistance and the opposite reactance, so that they cancel.
