    Costs: the engineer and costs

    With ARS 600,000 of fixed costs per month and ARS 100 of contribution per unit, you must sell 6000 units to avoid a loss. Selling 8000, a 10 % increase in sales raises profit by 40 %: the operating leverage is 4.

    Fixed and variable costs Contribution margin Break-even point Margin of safety Operating leverage

    01Classifying costs

    A cost is the value of the resources consumed to produce. According to how they vary with volume, they are fixed —rent, overhead salaries, depreciation, which do not change with the quantity produced within a given range— or variable —materials, components, process energy, which grow with each unit—. According to whether they can be assigned to a product, they are direct —the components of a board, the assembly time— or indirect —maintenance, supervision, quality—, which are allocated using some criterion.

    ElementIn an electronic unit
    Direct materialsComponents, board, cabinet, cables, packaging
    Direct laborAssembly, soldering, testing, from the standard time and the hourly cost
    Manufacturing overheadMachine depreciation, energy, maintenance, supervision, quality
    Selling and administrative expensesSales, warranties, offices

    In absorption costing the product bears a share of the fixed manufacturing costs; in variable costing only the variable ones, and the fixed ones are treated as a period cost. The second is better for making decisions, because it does not hide how the result changes with volume.

    02Contribution margin and break-even point

    Each unit sold at price \( p \) with variable cost \( c_v \) contributes a contribution margin \( p - c_v \) to cover the fixed costs \( CF \) and, after that, to yield profit. The volume at which profit is zero is the break-even point:

    \[ Q_e = \frac{CF}{p - c_v} \] \[ G = Q\,(p - c_v) - CF \] The margin of safety, \( (Q - Q_e)/Q \), tells how much sales can fall before a loss is incurred. The operating leverage, total contribution divided by profit, tells how much profit changes for each 1 % change in sales: a company with many fixed costs earns a lot when it sells more and loses a lot when it sells less.

    03The engineer and costs

    Most of the cost of a product is fixed at the design stage, long before it is manufactured: the choice of components, the number of board layers, the assembly technology, how easy it is to test and to assemble. Designing for manufacturing and testing, standardizing components, reducing variants and thinking about the cost of the whole life cycle —including warranties and recycling— are engineering decisions with economic consequences. The analysis of investments with cash flows, rates and present value is developed in Economics.

    05Common mistakes

    • Treating a fixed cost allocated per unit as variable. If production drops, the unit cost rises, because the fixed costs are spread over fewer units.
    • Forgetting that fixed costs are fixed only within a range. Moving to two shifts or to another plant changes them abruptly.
    • Looking only at the cost of components. Testing, warranties and rework can weigh as much as the materials.
    • Setting the price by adding a percentage to the cost without looking at the market. The market sets the price; the cost says whether it is worthwhile.

    06Self-assessment

    What is the break-even point with CF = 600,000, p = 250 and cv = 150?

    6000 units.

    What profit does selling 8000 give?

    \( 8000 \cdot 100 - 600\,000 = 200\,000 \) ARS.

    What is the margin of safety?

    25 %: sales can fall by a quarter before a loss is incurred.

    What happens to the break-even point if the price rises to 300?

    It drops to 4000 units, because the contribution becomes ARS 150.
