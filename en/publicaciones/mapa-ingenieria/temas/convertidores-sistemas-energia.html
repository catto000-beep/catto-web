    01Converters in generation

    Renewable generation reaches the grid through converters. A photovoltaic panel delivers direct current with a current-voltage characteristic that depends on irradiance and temperature; a DC-DC converter makes it work at its maximum power point and an inverter injects that power into the grid. A modern wind turbine produces AC of variable frequency, which a back-to-back converter —active rectifier, DC bus and inverter— adapts to the grid frequency. Storage systems based on batteries use bidirectional converters that charge or discharge according to the demand of the grid.

    02The maximum power point

    With the one-diode model, without parasitic resistances, the current of a panel of \( N_s \) cells in series is:

    \[ I = I_{sc}\,\frac{G}{1000} - I_0\left(e^{V / (n N_s V_T)} - 1\right) \] The power \( P = V I \) has a maximum, which moves with the irradiance \( G \) and with the temperature. The most widely used algorithm to track it is perturb and observe: the voltage is changed by one step; if the power rose, it continues in the same direction, and if it fell, the direction is reversed. It never stays still: it oscillates around the maximum.
    Lab · maximum power point tracking

    A 60-cell panel with Isc = 9 A and Voc = 38 V at 1000 W/m², with n = 1.3, at 25 °C. The tracker starts at 20 V and takes 80 steps: the first 40 with the initial irradiance and the other 40 with the final one. The curves are the power as a function of voltage; the dots, the path of the tracker.

    When there is partial shading, the curve of a set of panels has several maxima and the simple algorithm can get stuck at a local one; commercial inverters periodically sweep the whole curve.

    03The grid-connected inverter

    A grid-connected inverter does not set the voltage, which the grid imposes, but the current it injects. A phase-locked loop (PLL) measures the grid phase and the control generates a sinusoidal current reference in phase with it, with the amplitude that corresponds to the available power; an L or LCL filter attenuates the switching harmonics. It must detect the loss of the grid and disconnect, the anti-islanding protection, and meet the connection requirements of the utility.

    06Common mistakes

    • Choosing a voltage step without considering the trade-off. A large one oscillates; a small one is slow to follow changes in irradiance.
    • Trusting a simple tracker with partial shading. It can get stuck at a local maximum.
    • Voltage-controlling a grid-connected inverter. The grid imposes the voltage; the current must be controlled.
    • Omitting the anti-islanding protection. It endangers anyone working on a line believed to be de-energized.

    07Self-assessment

    Where is the maximum power point of the lab panel at 1000 W/m²?

    At about 32.3 V, with 273.8 W.

    And at 500 W/m²?

    At about 31.0 V, with 131 W: the voltage hardly changes, the current does.

    What does a grid-connected inverter control?

    The current it injects, in phase with the grid voltage.

    What does a STATCOM do?

    It injects reactive power with an inverter to regulate the grid voltage.
