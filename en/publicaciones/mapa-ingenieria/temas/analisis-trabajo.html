    Analysis of work and processes

    For 450 units per shift, a line of ten tasks needs a 60 s cycle and five stations, with 92 % efficiency. For 540, the largest-time rule uses seven stations even though the theoretical minimum is six.

    Methods study Standard time Product life cycle Process types Line balancing

    01Methods study and work measurement

    Work study has two parts. Methods study records how a task is done, examines it critically —what is done, where, when, by whom and how— and proposes a simpler, safer and more productive way. It relies on charts: the flow process chart, with its symbols for operation, inspection, transport, delay and storage; the flow diagram on the plant floor plan; and the two-handed process chart for assembly stations.

    Work measurement determines how long the task takes with the chosen method. With stopwatch timing, the observed time is corrected by the performance rating of the worker and allowances for fatigue and personal needs are added:

    \[ T_{est} = T_{obs} \cdot \frac{\text{rating}}{100} \cdot (1 + \text{allowances}) \] A worker observed at 50 s who works at 110 % of the normal pace, with 15 % allowances, gives a standard time of \( 50 \cdot 1.1 \cdot 1.15 \approx 63 \) s. Predetermined motion time systems, such as MTM, and work sampling are also used.

    02Markets, products and processes

    What and how much is produced is decided by the market: the analysis of demand, competition and prices defines the volume and the variety. Each product goes through a life cycle —introduction, growth, maturity and decline— that changes what matters at each stage: flexibility at the beginning, cost and efficiency at maturity. Depending on volume and variety, the process is organized in different ways:

    Process typeVolume and varietyExample in electronics
    ProjectA single, unique unitA radar station
    Job shop (by functions)Low volume, high varietyPrototypes and made-to-order equipment
    BatchMedium volumeLaboratory instruments
    LineHigh volume, low varietyAssembly of phones or televisions

    03Balancing a line

    On a line, the product passes through successive stations and all of them have the same time to work: the cycle time, which is set by demand. Balancing the line means distributing the tasks among the stations while respecting the precedence order, without any station exceeding the cycle and using the smallest possible number of stations:

    \[ c = \frac{\text{available time}}{\text{demand}} \] \[ N_{min} = \left\lceil \frac{\sum t_i}{c} \right\rceil, \quad \eta = \frac{\sum t_i}{N\,c} \] The exact problem is combinatorial; in practice heuristics are used. The largest task time rule assigns to each station, among the available tasks that fit in the remaining time, the longest one. It does not always reach the theoretical minimum.

    04In the laboratory

    Activity 1 · Flow process chart

    Make the flow process chart for the assembly of a power supply in the laboratory and propose a better method by eliminating transports and delays.

    Activity 2 · Standard time

    Time a soldering task ten times, rate the pace and calculate the standard time with allowances.

    Activity 3 · Manual balancing

    Balance the laboratory line by hand for 540 units and explain why it cannot be brought down to six stations, even though the theoretical minimum suggests it.

    05Common mistakes

    • Measuring without having improved the method. An inefficient way of working gets standardized.
    • Forgetting the allowances. A standard time without rest is impossible to sustain.
    • Setting a cycle shorter than the longest task. The task has to be split or the station duplicated.
    • Believing the heuristic gives the optimum. It is a good starting point, not a guarantee.

    06Self-assessment

    What cycle time corresponds to 300 units in a 450-minute shift?

    90 s.

    How many stations are needed at minimum, if the tasks add up to 275 s?

    \( \lceil 275/90 \rceil = 4 \).

    What efficiency does the line have with those four stations?

    \( 275/(4 \cdot 90) \approx 76 \) %.

    What process is suitable for producing varied prototypes?

    A job shop organized by functions.
