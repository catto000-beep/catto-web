    Power electronic devices

    At 20 A, an IGBT loses less than a MOSFET below about 19 kHz; above that the MOSFET wins, and at 100 kHz the IGBT would dissipate almost twice as much. The switching frequency chooses the device.

    Thyristor Power MOSFET IGBT Silicon carbide Switching losses

    01The device as a switch

    Power electronics converts electrical energy —from AC to DC, from DC to AC, from one voltage to another— with efficiencies of 90 to 99 %. To do so, the semiconductors do not work in their linear region, as in an amplifier, but as switches: fully closed, with little voltage, or fully open, with little current. The power they dissipate comes from their imperfections: the conduction drop, the leakage when blocking and, above all, the time they take to go from one state to the other.

    The thyristor is a four-layer structure that was covered in multijunction devices: once triggered, it stays latched as long as the current exceeds the holding current. The MOSFET conducts like a resistor, \( R_{DS(on)} \), which in silicon grows strongly with the blocking voltage; that is why above about 600 V the IGBT is preferred, which has a nearly fixed drop but turns off more slowly because of the tail current of the minority carriers. The wide-bandgap semiconductors, silicon carbide and gallium nitride, block more voltage with less resistance and switch faster.

    03Conduction and switching losses

    At each switching event, for a short time, the switch withstands voltage and current at the same time. With a duty cycle \( D \) and a switching frequency \( f \):

    \[ P_{MOS} = I^2 R_{DS(on)} D + E_{sw} f \] \[ E_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) + \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 \] \[ P_{IGBT} = (V_{CE0} + r_{CE} I)\, I\, D + (E_{on} + E_{off})\, f \] Conduction losses do not depend on frequency; switching losses grow with it. The junction temperature results from the total power and the thermal resistance to ambient, and must not exceed 150 to 175 °C.
    Lab · MOSFET or IGBT

    A 600 V MOSFET and IGBT switching 400 V at a duty cycle of 0.5. MOSFET: RDS(on) = 0.15 Ω when hot, tr + tf = 50 ns, Coss = 100 pF. IGBT: VCE0 = 0.9 V, rCE = 0.05 Ω, Eon + Eoff = 0.04 mJ per ampere. Thermal resistance to ambient of 1.5 K/W, at 40 °C.

    04Safe operating area and gate drive

    The safe operating area (SOA) in the datasheet delimits the voltage and current combinations that the device can withstand, depending on the pulse duration; in switching with an inductive load the trajectory must stay inside. The gate of a MOSFET or an IGBT is a capacitor: the gate driver charges and discharges it with peaks of amperes, and the gate resistor sets the switching speed, in a trade-off between losses and overvoltages. In bridges, the high-side switch needs an isolated driver or one with a floating supply, and a dead time that prevents the two switches of a leg from conducting at the same time.

    05In the laboratory

    Activity 1 · Switching

    With an inductive load and a double-pulse test, measure the voltage and current waveforms at turn-off of a MOSFET and of an IGBT, and integrate the switching energy.

    Activity 2 · Thyristor

    Measure the latching current and the holding current of an SCR with a DC supply and a variable load.

    Activity 3 · Gate resistor

    Change the gate resistor and observe the trade-off between switching speed and overvoltage.

    06Common mistakes

    • Taking RDS(on) at 25 °C. When hot it is between 1.5 and 2 times higher.
    • Forgetting the switching losses. At high frequency they can exceed the conduction losses.
    • Expecting a thyristor to turn off by removing the gate pulse. It stays latched until the current goes to zero.
    • Omitting the dead time in a bridge. The two switches of a leg short-circuit the supply.

    07Self-assessment

    With the lab data, how much does the MOSFET lose at 20 A and 20 kHz?

    30 W of conduction and 4.16 W of switching: 34.2 W.

    And the IGBT?

    19 W of conduction and 16 W of switching: 35 W. At that frequency they are almost tied.

    Why does the IGBT turn off more slowly than the MOSFET?

    Because of the tail current: the accumulated minority carriers have to recombine.

    What advantage does silicon carbide give?

    It blocks more voltage with less conduction resistance and switches fast, at a higher temperature.
