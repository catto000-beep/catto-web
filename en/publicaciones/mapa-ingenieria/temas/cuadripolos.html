    Two-port networks

    Two attenuators of 6 and 10 dB in cascade give 16 dB only if each one sees the impedance it was designed for. The transmission matrix tells you what happens with any other load.

    z and y parameters h parameters ABCD matrix Cascade Reciprocity

    01A box with two pairs of terminals

    Many circuits are used as a block with one input and one output: an amplifier, a filter, an attenuator, a transformer, a line. A two-port network describes them solely by what happens at their two pairs of terminals, regardless of how they are built inside. It has four variables, \( V_1, I_1, V_2, I_2 \), and since it is linear, two of them are determined by the other two. Depending on which are chosen as independent there are six sets of parameters; four are in constant use.

    02Impedance and admittance parameters

    \[ V_1 = z_{11} I_1 + z_{12} I_2 \] \[ V_2 = z_{21} I_1 + z_{22} I_2 \] Each z parameter is measured by leaving one pair of terminals open-circuited: \( z_{11} = V_1/I_1 \) with \( I_2 = 0 \). The y parameters, the inverses of these, are measured with a short circuit.

    If the two-port is reciprocal —any network of resistors, capacitors, inductors and transformers is—, \( z_{12} = z_{21} \): the transfer is the same in both directions. An amplifier is not reciprocal.

    03Hybrid parameters and the transistor

    \[ V_1 = h_{11} I_1 + h_{12} V_2 \] \[ I_2 = h_{21} I_1 + h_{22} V_2 \] They mix an impedance, a reverse voltage gain, a current gain and an admittance. In the common-emitter bipolar transistor they are called \( h_{ie} \), \( h_{re} \), \( h_{fe} \) and \( h_{oe} \), and they are the ones given in datasheets.

    The h parameters are natural for the transistor because they are measured under easy conditions: \( h_{ie} \) and \( h_{fe} \) with the output short-circuited for the signal, and \( h_{re} \) and \( h_{oe} \) with the input open. The small-signal model of the bipolar transistor amplifier is exactly this two-port.

    04Transmission parameters and the cascade connection

    \[ \begin{pmatrix} V_1 \\ I_1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} V_2 \\ -I_2 \end{pmatrix} \] They relate the input to the output. Their great advantage: the matrix of two two-ports in cascade is the product of their matrices, in the order in which they are connected.
    BlockABCD matrix
    Series impedance \( Z \)\( \begin{pmatrix} 1 & Z \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \)
    Shunt admittance \( Y \)\( \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ Y & 1 \end{pmatrix} \)
    Ideal transformer 1 : n\( \begin{pmatrix} 1/n & 0 \\ 0 & n \end{pmatrix} \)
    Lossless line of length \( \ell \)\( \begin{pmatrix} \cos\beta\ell & jZ_0\sin\beta\ell \\ j\sin\beta\ell/Z_0 & \cos\beta\ell \end{pmatrix} \)

    A ladder network is then analyzed by multiplying simple matrices, which is what RF circuit simulation programs do. The transmission line fits into the same calculation.

    05The two-port with source and load

    With a load \( R_L \) at the output, the transmission parameters directly give what matters:

    \[ Z_{in} = \frac{A R_L + B}{C R_L + D} \] \[ \frac{V_2}{V_1} = \frac{R_L}{A R_L + B} \] The input impedance depends on the load, and the gain on what the two-port has connected on both sides. That is why a block measured with one load does not perform the same with another.
    Lab · two-ports in cascade

    A 50 Ω generator drives two two-ports in cascade with a load \( R_L \). The ABCD matrices are multiplied and the input impedance and the gains are calculated. The attenuators are designed for 50 Ω; the transistor has \( h_{ie} = 2.5 \) kΩ, \( h_{re} = 2 \times 10^{-4} \), \( h_{fe} = 100 \) and \( h_{oe} = 20\ \mu \)S.

    06In the lab

    Activity 1 · Measuring the z parameters

    Build a T network of resistors, measure \( z_{11}, z_{12}, z_{21}, z_{22} \) with the terminals open and verify reciprocity.

    Activity 2 · Cascade

    Connect two attenuators in cascade, measure the total attenuation and compare it with the product of the matrices.

    Activity 3 · h parameters of a transistor

    Measure \( h_{ie} \) and \( h_{fe} \) of a biased transistor, with the output short-circuited for the signal through a capacitor, and compare with the datasheet.

    07Common mistakes

    • Multiplying the matrices in reverse order. Matrix multiplication does not commute.
    • Forgetting the sign of \( I_2 \). In the ABCD parameters the output current is taken as flowing out.
    • Using parameters measured at a different bias. The transistor’s h parameters change with the operating point.
    • Assuming the input impedance does not depend on the load. That is only so if \( C R_L \ll D \) and \( B \ll A R_L \).

    08Self-assessment

    What ABCD matrix does a 100 Ω series resistor followed by a 100 Ω shunt resistor have?

    \( \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 100 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0.01 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 100 \\ 0.01 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \).

    What input impedance does an ideal 1:2 transformer have with a 200 Ω load?

    \( 200 / 2^2 = 50\ \Omega \).

    When is a two-port reciprocal?

    When \( z_{12} = z_{21} \); all passive networks of linear elements are.

    What voltage gain does a common emitter with hfe = 100, hie = 2.5 kΩ and a 2 kΩ load have, neglecting hre and hoe?

    \( -h_{fe} R_L / h_{ie} = -80 \).
