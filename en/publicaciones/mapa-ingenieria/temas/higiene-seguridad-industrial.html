    Industrial hygiene and safety

    Fifteen minutes at 100 dBA use up the entire noise dose of an eight-hour working day. Every additional 3 dB cuts the permitted time in half.

    Risk agents Noise Daily dose Equivalent level Hierarchy of controls

    01Two complementary disciplines

    Safety at work deals with accidents: sudden events that cause injury, such as a fall, being caught in machinery or an electric shock. Hygiene deals with occupational diseases: harm that sets in slowly through repeated exposure to an agent, such as deafness from noise or lead poisoning from soldering. Both share the goal of anticipating, recognizing, evaluating and controlling risks before they cause harm.

    The Italian physician Bernardino Ramazzini published in 1700 the first systematic treatise on the diseases of trades, and proposed asking every patient what work they did. In Argentina, the legal basis is Law 19,587 on Hygiene and Safety at Work and its regulatory decree 351/79, which are covered in the legal framework unit. The presentation for technicians is in health and safety, in the Electronics Technician program.

    TypeExamplesIn electronics

    PsychosocialMental load, shifts, deadline pressureOn-call duty for critical systems

    For each agent, hygiene defines a measurable quantity and a reference value: the concentration in the air for a chemical contaminant, the sound level for noise, the illuminance for light. The evaluation compares the measured exposure with that value.

    03Noise: level, dose and equivalent level

    Noise is the most widespread physical agent in industry, and the deafness it causes is irreversible. The sound level is measured with A-weighting, in dBA, which imitates the sensitivity of the ear. SRT (Superintendency of Occupational Risks) Resolution 295/03 adopted as its criterion 85 dBA for 8 hours, with a 3 dB exchange rate: for every 3 dB more, which is twice the energy, the permitted time is cut in half:

    \[ T(L) = \frac{8\ \mathrm{h}}{2^{(L - 85)/3}} \] \[ D = \sum_i \frac{C_i}{T(L_i)} \] \[ L_{eq,8h} = 85 + 10 \log_{10} D \] \( C_i \) is the time spent at level \( L_i \). If the dose \( D \) exceeds 1, or the 8-hour equivalent level exceeds 85 dBA, the exposure is excessive. The measurement procedure is in SRT Resolution 85/12; always check the current version of the rules in the Boletín Oficial (Argentina’s Official Gazette).

    04The hierarchy of controls

    Once a risk has been evaluated, the measures are chosen in order of decreasing effectiveness. The first ones act on the hazard; the last ones depend on each person doing the right thing every time:

    LevelMeasureExample with noise
    1EliminationReplace a riveting process with a bonding one
    2SubstitutionA compressor with a lower sound level
    3Engineering controlsAcoustic enclosure, anti-vibration mounts
    4Administrative controlsTask rotation to lower the dose, signage
    5Personal protective equipmentHearing protectors

    Personal protective equipment is the last resort, not the first: it protects only if it is worn properly and all the time, and it does not reduce the risk for anyone else.

    06Common mistakes

    • Adding decibels as plain numbers. Two 85 dBA sources give 88 dBA, not 170.
    • Believing that a slightly higher level is only slightly riskier. With a 3 dB exchange rate, 91 dBA allows only 2 hours.
    • Starting with hearing protectors. They are the last step of the hierarchy of controls.
    • Confusing an accident with an occupational disease. The disease appears years later and is harder to attribute.

    07Self-assessment

    How much time per day is allowed at 94 dBA?

    1 hour: 9 dB above 85 is three halvings.

    A person spends 4 hours at 88 dBA and 4 hours at 80 dBA. What dose do they receive?

    \( 4/4 + 4/25.4 \approx 1.16 \): 116%, excessive.

    What 8-hour equivalent level corresponds to a dose of 200%?

    \( 85 + 10\log_{10} 2 = 88 \) dBA.

    Why are soldering fumes a chemical agent and not a physical one?

    Because the harm is caused by the substances that are inhaled: the flux compounds and the metals.
