    Kinematics and dynamics of the rigid body

    An extended body does not only translate: it also rotates. Every quantity of translation has its twin in rotation, and mass becomes moment of inertia.

    Rotation Moment of inertia Torque Rolling Angular momentum

    01Every quantity has its twin

    A rigid body is a model: a solid whose particles always keep the same distance from one another. It does not deform. With that idealization, any motion breaks down into translation of the center of mass plus rotation about it.

    And the remarkable thing is that rotation repeats, term by term, the structure of translation:

    Velocity vAngular velocity \( \omega \)\( v = \omega \cdot R \)
    Acceleration aAngular acceleration \( \alpha \)\( a = \alpha \cdot R \)
    Mass mMoment of inertia I\( I = \sum m_i \, r_i^2 \)
    Force FTorque \( M = F \cdot d \)\( M = r \times F \)
    \( \Sigma F = m\cdot a \)\( \Sigma M = I\cdot \alpha \)The second law, twice
    \( p = m\cdot v \)\( L = I\cdot \omega \)Angular momentum
    \( E_c = \tfrac{1}{2} m v^2 \)\( E_c = \tfrac{1}{2} I \omega^2 \)They add up if both are present
    \( P = F\cdot v \)\( P = M\cdot \omega \)Power
    The power of a motor

    The last row is the one used every day: a 1.5 kW motor turning at 1500 rpm delivers a torque of \(M = P/\omega = 1500/(1500 \cdot 2\pi/60) =\) \(9.55\,\mathrm{N \cdot m}\). That calculation decides which motor is needed to move a load, and it is the same for a 3D-printer servo as for a grinding mill.

    02Moment of inertia

    Mass measures the resistance to changes in velocity; the moment of inertia measures the resistance to changes in angular velocity. And unlike mass, it depends on the axis: the same part is harder or easier to spin depending on where you hold it.

    Body (axis through the center)Moment of inertia\( k = \dfrac{I}{m\cdot R^{2}} \)
    Hoop or thin-walled hollow cylinder\( m\cdot R^{2} \)1
    Disk or solid cylinder\( \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot m\cdot R^{2} \)0.5
    Hollow sphere\( \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot m\cdot R^{2} \)0.667
    Solid sphere\( \tfrac{2}{5} m R^2 \)0.4
    Rod about its center\( \dfrac{1}{12}\cdot m\cdot L^{2} \)—
    Rod about one end\( \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot m\cdot L^{2} \)—
    The parallel-axis theorem (Steiner)

    For an axis parallel to the one through the center of mass, at a distance d: \(I = I_{ ext{cm}} + m \cdot d^2\). That is why a rod held at one end has \( frac{1}{3} \cdot m \cdot L^2\) and not \(1/12\): \(1/12 + 1/4 = 1/3\).

    03Rotational dynamics and rolling

    A body that rolls without slipping satisfies \(v = \omega \cdot R\), and its kinetic energy is split between translation and rotation. Writing the conservation of energy for a drop through a height h:

    \[ m g h = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 + \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2 \quad \implies \quad a = \frac{g \, \operatorname{sin} \theta}{1 + k} \] With \(k = I/(m \cdot R^2)\). Neither the mass nor the radius appears: only the shape of the body.

    This gives a result that always surprises: in a race down an inclined plane, the solid sphere beats the disk, and the disk beats the hoop, regardless of how much each weighs or how big it is. The winner is the one that puts less energy into spinning.

    Lab · race of rolling bodies

    All five start together from the same height. Move with the time slider and watch the finishing order.

    04Angular momentum and its conservation

    If the net external torque is zero, \(L = I \cdot \omega\) is conserved. Since \(I\) can change —by moving mass closer to or farther from the axis—, the angular velocity changes in the opposite direction:

    • The skater who pulls in her arms reduces I and spins faster.
    • The cat that falls on its back rights itself in midair with no external torque: it changes its shape.
    • A satellite is stabilized by spinning an internal wheel; the spacecraft turns the opposite way. This is the principle of the reaction wheel, which orients space telescopes without using fuel.
    • A gyroscope resists changes in the orientation of its axis, and that property is what a MEMS gyroscope in a phone or a drone measures.
    The gyroscopic effect

    When a torque is applied to a spinning body, the axis does not move in the direction the force pushes but perpendicular to it: this is precession. It is what keeps a moving bicycle upright and what makes a tilted spinning top turn instead of falling over.

    05Where it shows up in electronics

    • Sizing a motor. The starting torque must overcome the moment of inertia of the load: \(M = I \cdot \alpha\). A stepper motor that falls short skips steps, and the error accumulates.
    • Encoders. They measure θ and, by differentiating, ω. The speed control of a motor is a loop around that measurement.
    • MEMS gyroscopes. They detect rotation through the Coriolis effect on a vibrating mass: the image stabilization of a camera and the control of a drone depend on them.
    • Disks and fans. The time a disk or a fan takes to reach operating speed comes from \(I \cdot \alpha = M_{\text{motor}} - M_{\text{friction}}\).
    • Flywheels. They store energy as \(\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot I \cdot \omega^2\), and are used as a reserve in large uninterruptible power supply systems.

    06In the lab

    Activity 1 · The race, on the bench

    Let a hoop, a disk and a sphere of the same diameter roll down an inclined board. Verify the finishing order and check that it does not change when bodies of different mass are used.

    Activity 2 · Measuring the moment of inertia of a wheel

    Hang a weight from a string wound around the axle of a wheel and measure the acceleration with which it descends. Solve for \(I\) from \(a = g/(1 + I/(m \cdot r^2))\). Compare with the theoretical value for a disk.

    Activity 3 · Reading a gyroscope

    With an MPU-6050, record the angular velocity while rotating the board 90°. Integrate ω numerically to obtain the angle and check how much it drifts because of the sensor’s drift: that accumulated error is the reason it is combined with the accelerometer in a complementary filter.

    07Common mistakes

    • Using a moment of inertia without saying about which axis. It makes no sense.
    • Forgetting the parallel-axis theorem when the axis does not pass through the center of mass.
    • Adding only the translational energy for a rolling body: \(\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot I \cdot \omega^2\) is missing.
    • Believing the heavier body wins the race. The mass cancels.
    • Confusing the torque of a force with angular momentum. One is the cause, the other is what changes: \(M = dL/dt\).
    • Applying \(v = \omega \cdot R\) when there is slipping. It only holds in pure rolling.

    08Self-assessment

    Which reaches the bottom of a plane first: a solid sphere or a hoop?

    The sphere: \(k = 0.4\) versus \(k = 1\), so its acceleration is \(g \cdot \sin heta/1.4\) versus \(g \cdot \sin heta/2\). It arrives first: it takes 16% less time (and at the bottom it moves about 20% faster).

    A 2 kW motor turns at 3000 rpm. What is its torque?

    \(\omega = 3000 \cdot 2\pi/60 = 314\text{ rad/s}\), so \(M = 2000/314 \approx\) \(6.4\,\mathrm{N \cdot m}\).

    How much energy does a flywheel with \(I = 0.5\,\mathrm{kg \cdot m^2}\) store at 6000 rpm?

    \(\omega = 628\text{ rad/s}\), \(E = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.5 \cdot 628^2 \approx\) \(98.6\text{ kJ}\). Doubling the speed quadruples the energy.

    A 1 m, 2 kg rod rotates about one end. What is its moment of inertia?

    \(I = \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot m \cdot L^2 = \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot 2 \cdot 1 =\) \(0.667\,\mathrm{kg \cdot m^2}\).

    Why does the skater speed up when she pulls in her arms?

    Because \(L = I \cdot \omega\) is conserved: as \(I\) decreases, \(\omega\) increases in the same proportion. The kinetic energy, on the other hand, increases, and comes from the muscular work of pulling the arms in.

    In what proportion is the energy of a rolling disk divided?

    Two thirds in translation and one third in rotation, because \(\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot I \cdot \omega^2 = \tfrac{1}{4} \cdot m \cdot v^2\), half of the translational energy.
