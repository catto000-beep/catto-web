    DC circuits

    The load that receives the maximum power is the one that equals the internal resistance, and right there the efficiency is 50%. That is why in radio frequency you match, and in a power system you never do.

    Ohm's law Kirchhoff Dividers Thévenin Maximum transfer

    01Current and Ohm's law

    Current is charge crossing a cross-section per unit of time. Its conventional direction is that of positive charges, even though in a metal what moves are electrons and they go the other way: it is an inherited convention that is better accepted than fought.

    \[ I = \frac{dq}{dt} \qquad\qquad V = I R \qquad\qquad P = VI = I^2R = \frac{V^2}{R} \] Ohm's law is not a universal law: it is a property of certain materials. A diode, an incandescent lamp and an electric arc do not obey it, and those that do are called ohmic.
    Electrons are very slow

    In a 1 mm² copper conductor carrying 1 A, the drift velocity of the electrons is about 0.07 mm per second: they would take four hours to travel one meter. What travels fast is not the electrons but the field, which is established at an appreciable fraction of the speed of light. That is why the lamp lights up instantly even though the electrons barely creep along.

    02Kirchhoff's two laws

    Solving any DC circuit comes down to writing down two things, and both are conservation principles in disguise:

    LawStatementWhat it conserves
    Node law\( \sum I = 0 \) at each nodeCharge: it does not accumulate
    Mesh law\( \sum V = 0 \) around each closed loop Energy: the field is conservative

    With those two and Ohm's law any resistive network can be solved, however large. And from there come the two dividers that you end up using from memory:

    \[ V_2 = V\,\frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2} \qquad\qquad I_2 = I\,\frac{R_1}{R_1 + R_2} \] The voltage divider with the resistors in series, the current divider with the resistors in parallel. Note that in the current divider the numerator is the resistance of the other branch.

    03Real sources and maximum transfer

    No real source holds its voltage when loaded: it has an internal resistance. Thévenin's theorem says that any linear network, however complicated, behaves seen from two terminals just like an ideal source in series with a resistance.

    \[ V = \varepsilon - I R_i \qquad\qquad P_L = \frac{\varepsilon^2 R_L}{(R_i + R_L)^2} \] The power delivered to the load is maximum when \( R_L = R_i \), and there it equals \( \varepsilon^2/4R_i \). But right at that point the efficiency is 50%: half the energy is dissipated inside the source.
    Lab · real source and power transfer

    A source with internal resistance feeding a variable load. The three curves are voltage, power and efficiency. Look for the maximum of the power and see where the efficiency ends up at that point.

    Why nobody designs for maximum transfer

    Matching the load to the internal resistance delivers the greatest possible power, and that is why it is done in radio frequency, where what matters is that signal reaches the antenna. But in a power system it would be absurd: a matched power grid would throw away half of what it generates heating its own lines. There the opposite is sought, \( R_i \ll R_L \), where the delivered power is not the maximum possible but the efficiency approaches 100%.

    04Measuring without disturbing

    Every instrument becomes part of the circuit it measures, and so it disturbs it. The criterion is that its resistance be negligible compared with that of the circuit, or enormous, depending on how it is connected.

    InstrumentConnectedIts resistance must beTypical value
    VoltmeterIn parallelAs high as possible10 MΩ
    AmmeterIn seriesAs low as possibleLess than 1 Ω
    OhmmeterAcross the isolated component—Injects a known current

    From the second row comes the most expensive mistake in the lab: connecting an ammeter in parallel with a source is putting a short circuit across it. And from the third, that an ohmmeter only measures well with the component disconnected: on the board it measures the component and everything in parallel with it.

    05In the lab

    Activity 1 · Internal resistance of a battery

    Measure the voltage of a battery at no load and with several known loads. Plot \( V \) against \( I \), and obtain \( \varepsilon \) from the y-intercept and \( R_i \) from the slope. Repeat with a depleted battery and compare.

    Activity 2 · The maximum transfer curve

    With the same source and a potentiometer as the load, measure \( V \) and \( I \) for about ten values. Plot the power against \( R_L \), locate the maximum and verify that it falls where \( R_L = R_i \).

    Activity 3 · Verifying Kirchhoff

    Build a two-mesh network with three resistors and two sources. Predict all the currents by solving the system, measure them and compare. Discuss whether the differences are explained by the tolerance of the resistors.

    06Common mistakes

    • Believing Ohm's law always holds. A diode, a lamp and an arc are not ohmic.
    • Inverting the current divider. It has the resistance of the other branch in the numerator.
    • Designing a power system for maximum transfer. That fixes the efficiency at 50%.
    • Connecting the ammeter in parallel. It is a short circuit, and it usually ends in a blown fuse.
    • Measuring resistance with the component on the board. You measure it together with everything in parallel with it.
    • Thinking electrons move fast. They drift at tenths of a millimeter per second; what moves fast is the field.

    07Self-assessment

    What does the node law conserve?

    Charge: what enters a node leaves it, because it does not accumulate.

    When is the power delivered to the load maximum?

    When \( R_L = R_i \), and there it equals \( \varepsilon^2/4R_i \). The efficiency at that point is 50%.

    Why is a power grid not matched?

    Because it would throw away half the energy heating its lines. There the goal is \( R_i \ll R_L \).

    What resistance should a voltmeter have?

    As high as possible, because it is connected in parallel and must not divert current.

    What does Thévenin's theorem say?

    That any linear network seen from two terminals is equivalent to an ideal source in series with a resistance.

    At what speed do electrons drift in a wire?

    At tenths of a millimeter per second. What travels fast is the electric field.
