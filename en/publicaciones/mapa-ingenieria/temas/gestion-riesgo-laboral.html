    Occupational risk management

    In the matrix, dielectric gloves lower the electric shock risk at the bench from 15 to 10. An isolation transformer with a residual-current device lowers it to 4: it pays to act on the hazard.

    Hazard and risk Risk matrix HAZOP ALARP ISO 45001

    01Hazard and risk

    A hazard is a source of possible harm: a 220 V voltage, a flammable solvent, a ladder. Risk combines the likelihood that the harm occurs with the severity of its consequences. The same hazard can give very different risks: 220 V in a closed panel with an RCD is not the same as 220 V in an open power supply on the bench.

    \[ R = P \times C \] \( P \) is the probability or expected frequency and \( C \) the consequence. In practice, both are estimated on scales from 1 to 5 and the risk is placed in a matrix; with enough data, as in probability theory, they are calculated quantitatively.

    02Identifying hazards

    What has not been identified cannot be managed. The techniques range from the simplest to the most systematic:

    TechniqueHow it worksWhen to use it
    ChecklistsPredefined questions by type of hazardRoutine inspections
    Job safety analysisSteps of the task with their hazardsManual and maintenance tasks
    What if...?A group proposes deviations and analyzes their effectsNew processes, changes
    HAZOPGuide words (more, less, no, reverse) applied to each process variableContinuous process plants
    FMEAFailure modes of each component, their effects and their detectionDesign of equipment and products

    The participation of workers is part of the method: the person who does the task knows deviations and shortcuts that do not appear in any drawing.

    03Evaluating: the risk matrix

    Each hazard is placed in a likelihood-by-consequence matrix, and the product is classified into levels that indicate what to do. A common classification for scales from 1 to 5:

    Value of RLevelAction
    1 to 4LowMaintain the existing controls
    5 to 9ModerateReduce when reasonable; plan
    10 to 16HighReduce as a priority before continuing, or with immediate measures
    20 and 25IntolerableDo not start or continue the work until it has been reduced

    The underlying criterion is the ALARP principle (as low as reasonably practicable): a risk is reduced as long as the cost of reducing it is not disproportionate to the benefit. Measures are chosen using the hierarchy of controls, and the risk that remains after applying them, the residual risk, is evaluated again.

    04A management system

    Evaluating once is not enough: processes, people and equipment change. A management system turns prevention into a cycle of continual improvement: plan, do, check and act. The ISO 45001 standard organizes it with the common structure of ISO management standards:

    • Context and leadership: management takes responsibility, and workers participate and are consulted.
    • Planning: hazard identification, risk assessment, legal requirements and objectives.
    • Support and operation: resources, competence, communication, operational controls and emergency preparedness.
    • Evaluation and improvement: monitoring, internal audits, management review, incident investigation and corrective actions.

    The legal requirements of the legal framework enter the system as one more requirement: the standard does not replace them.

    05In the laboratory

    Activity 1 · Matrix of the classroom-workshop

    Identify the hazards of the university laboratory with a checklist, estimate them in the matrix and propose controls for those that remain at a high level.

    Activity 2 · What if...?

    In a group, apply the technique to the charging of lithium batteries in the laboratory: what happens if one is short-circuited, if it is overcharged, if it is punctured, if no one is present.

    Activity 3 · FMEA of a device

    Do a simplified FMEA of a bench power supply, from the point of view of the safety of the person who uses it.

    06Common mistakes

    • Confusing hazard with risk. The hazard is the source; the risk is how likely and how serious its harm is.
    • Believing that the matrix gives an exact number. It orders and prioritizes; its values are estimates.
    • Lowering the risk with PPE alone. It reduces the likelihood a little and does nothing to the potential severity of the hazard.
    • Evaluating once and filing it away. Every change of process, equipment or staff requires evaluating again.

    07Self-assessment

    A hazard has likelihood 3 and consequence 4. At what level does it fall?

    R = 12: high.

    What does ALARP mean?

    Reducing the risk as far as is reasonably practicable, until the cost is disproportionate to the benefit.

    Which technique would you use to analyze the hazards of a continuous chemical plant?

    HAZOP.

    Why does an engineering control usually lower the risk more than an administrative one?

    Because it acts on the hazard without depending on each person following a procedure every time.
