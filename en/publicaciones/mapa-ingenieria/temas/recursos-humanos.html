    Human resources and labor relations

    Evaluated by points, the maintenance technician position is worth more than the product engineer position, but it pays less: it sits 17 index points below the salary line. Job evaluation brings such internal inequities to light.

    Selection Training Job evaluation Motivation Collective agreement

    01The human resources functions

    People are the resource that decides how all the others are used. The human resources area takes care of their complete cycle in the company:

    FunctionWhat it does
    PlanningEstimates how many people and with what profiles are needed, based on the production plans
    Recruitment and selectionAttracts candidates and chooses, through interviews, technical tests and references, the one who best fits the position
    Onboarding and trainingIntegrates newcomers and develops everyone’s competencies, including safety training
    Performance appraisalCompares what was achieved with what was expected and guides training and career
    CompensationSets salaries that are equitable between positions and competitive against the market
    Health, safety and well-beingSafe working conditions, as covered in occupational risk management

    02Motivation

    The Hawthorne studies showed that performance does not depend on pay alone. Maslow proposed a hierarchy of needs —physiological, safety, social, esteem and self-actualization— in which the higher ones motivate when the lower ones are met. Herzberg distinguished hygiene factors —salary, conditions, supervision—, whose absence produces dissatisfaction but whose presence does not motivate, from motivators —achievement, recognition, responsibility, the work itself—. McGregor contrasted two management assumptions: Theory X, which sees the worker as someone who avoids effort and has to be controlled, and Theory Y, which sees the worker as capable of self-direction if the work has meaning.

    03Point-factor job evaluation

    For salaries to be equitable, different positions have to be compared with the same yardstick. In the point-factor method, factors are chosen —education, experience, responsibility, effort, working conditions—, a weight is assigned to them, each degree of each factor is described and the position is rated, not the person who holds it:

    \[ \text{points} = \sum_i w_i\, g_i \] With the points of benchmark positions and their salaries, a pay line is fitted by least squares. A position far above or below the line indicates an internal inequity; the comparison with market surveys indicates whether the company pays more or less than its competitors.

    04Labor relations

    Labor relations link the company, the workers and their unions, and the State. The working conditions of each industry are set in collective agreements, negotiated between employers’ chambers and unions, which establish job categories, minimum wages, working hours and leave above the legal minimums. In Argentina, the general framework is the Employment Contract Law (Ley de Contrato de Trabajo), No. 20,744, which was covered in labor law; like any rule, its current text should be consulted on InfoLEG (the national legislative information service). Conflicts are channeled through negotiation, conciliation and, where applicable, direct action; a good work climate prevents them.

    05In the laboratory

    Activity 1 · Job description

    Write the description and profile of the test technician position at an electronics company: tasks, responsibilities, requirements.

    Activity 2 · Point evaluation

    In groups, evaluate three positions of the university laboratory with the point-factor method and compare the results between groups.

    Activity 3 · Collective agreement

    Read the job categories of the collective agreement for the electronics or metalworking industry and place the evaluated positions within them.

    06Common mistakes

    • Evaluating the person instead of the position. Job evaluation rates what the position demands, not how its holder performs it.
    • Believing that salary motivates everything. Its absence demotivates, but its presence is not enough.
    • Selecting only for technical knowledge. Teamwork and communication weigh as much as technical skill.
    • Quoting a law from memory. Rules change; the current text has to be consulted.

    What salary index does the lab’s pay line give it?

    About 118.

    What are Herzberg’s hygiene factors?

    Those that, if absent, produce dissatisfaction, but whose presence does not motivate: salary, conditions, supervision.

    What does a collective agreement establish?

    The categories, minimum wages, working hours and other working conditions of an industry, above the legal minimums.
