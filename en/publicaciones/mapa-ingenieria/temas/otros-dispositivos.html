    01Thermistors

    A thermistor is a semiconductor resistor, made of sintered metal oxides, whose resistance changes strongly with temperature. NTCs lower their resistance when heated, by about 4 % per degree at room temperature, ten times more than a metal; they are used to measure temperature and to limit the inrush current of power supplies. PTCs raise their resistance abruptly above a transition temperature, and serve as protection and as self-regulating heaters. Their resistance follows, over a limited range, the β equation:

    \[ R(T) = R_{25}\,\exp\!\left[\beta\left(\frac{1}{T} - \frac{1}{298.15\ \mathrm{K}}\right)\right] \] \[ \frac{1}{T} = A + B \ln R + C\,(\ln R)^3 \] The second is the Steinhart–Hart equation, with three coefficients that are fitted by measuring three temperatures; over a wide range it is much more accurate than the β equation. The measurement current heats the thermistor: it has to be kept small compared with its dissipation constant, in mW/K.

    The curve is highly nonlinear. Connected in a divider with a fixed resistor \( R_s \), the output voltage is an S-shaped curve, with an inflection point where it is almost straight. Placing the inflection at the center of the measurement range \( T_m \) gives the resistor:

    \[ R_s = R(T_m)\,\frac{\beta - 2T_m}{\beta + 2T_m} \] With a 10 kΩ NTC and \( \beta = 3950\ \text{K} \), for a range of 0 to 70 °C, centered at 35 °C, \( R_s \) ≈ 4.75 kΩ results. Sensor conditioning in general was covered in sensor conditioning.

    02Protection devices

    DeviceHow it actsUse
    FuseMelts by \( I^2 t \); fast or time-delayOvercurrent and short circuit, one time only
    Resettable PTC (polymeric)Goes to high resistance when heated and recovers on coolingUSB ports, batteries, moderate overloads
    Varistor (MOV)Zinc oxide that conducts above a voltage; degrades with each surgeMains overvoltages
    TVS suppressorLarge-area avalanche diode, very fastElectrostatic discharges and transients on data and power lines
    Gas discharge tube (GDT)Arc in a gas; large energy, but slow and with a high breakdown voltageTelephone and antenna lines, first stage of protection

    Protections are combined in stages: a discharge tube for the large energy, a separating resistance or inductance, and a TVS close to the circuit to clamp the voltage precisely.

    03Relays and optocouplers

    An electromechanical relay galvanically isolates the control circuit from the power circuit and has a very low contact resistance, but it is slow, has bounce and a limited mechanical life. Its contacts are rated for current and voltage, and the DC breaking capacity is much lower than the AC one, because the arc does not extinguish by itself. The coil needs a freewheeling diode so as not to destroy the transistor that drives it. A solid-state relay, with a triac or MOS transistors and an optocoupler, has no wear or bounce, but it dissipates and has leakage current. An optocoupler transmits a signal across an isolation barrier with an LED and a phototransistor, and is specified by its current transfer ratio (CTR), which falls as the LED ages.

    05Common mistakes

    • Using the β equation over the whole range. Outside the interval where it was fitted, the error reaches several degrees.
    • Forgetting the self-heating of the thermistor. A high measurement current puts it above the temperature to be measured.
    • Trusting a varistor forever. Each surge degrades it and it can end up short-circuited.
    • Switching DC with a relay rated for AC. The arc does not extinguish and burns the contacts.

    06Self-assessment

    What resistance does a 10 kΩ NTC with \( \beta = 3950\ \text{K} \) have at 70 °C?

    About 1.76 kΩ.

    What fixed resistor linearizes the divider best for measuring between 0 and 70 °C?

    One close to 4.75 kΩ, the one that places the inflection at 35 °C; in the E24 series, 4.7 or 5.1 kΩ.

    Why is a gas discharge tube combined with a TVS?

    The tube absorbs the large energy but lets a high spike through while it ignites; the TVS clamps the voltage quickly and precisely.

    What is the diode in parallel with a relay coil for?

    To give a path to the coil current when it is switched off and avoid the overvoltage.
