    Motor and transformer tests

    A machine’s nameplate says what it should do; the test says what it does. With very little energy —without loading it to full power— two or three tests give the equivalent-circuit parameters, the efficiency and the regulation, and allow deciding whether the machine is fit to work.

    No-load Short circuit Locked rotor Temperature rise

    01Before testing: method, instruments and safety

    A test is a planned measurement: the machine is put in a known condition, a few quantities are measured with suitable instruments, and the quantities that cannot be measured directly are calculated from them. The nameplate says what the machine should do; the test says what it does. Operating principles and starting methods are in AC motors and Transformers; this page shows how to check them.

    No-load

    Rated voltage and nothing connected to the output (the motor, with no load coupled). The machine draws little power, almost all of it to magnetize the core and overcome friction. It gives the iron losses and the excitation current.

    Short-circuit or locked rotor

    Output shorted (or shaft locked) and reduced voltage, just enough for rated current to flow. It gives the copper losses and the leakage impedance.

    Under load

    The machine delivers power to a real load or to a brake. It gives the efficiency, the regulation and the temperature rise, but it uses all the energy and needs a suitable load.

    Why two «small» tests replace a big one

    At full load, a 5 kVA transformer delivers 4 kW at cosφ=0.8. In the classic tests the supply provides only 60 W at no load and 110 W on short circuit. Since iron losses depend on voltage and copper losses on current, each test isolates one group of losses with a fraction of the energy. With both, the equivalent circuit is built and everything else is calculated from it: efficiency at any load, regulation, short-circuit current.

    QuantityInstrumentCare when testing
    VoltageTrue-RMS voltmeterIn parallel, taken at the machine terminals and not at the source: the voltage drop in the leads falsifies the short-circuit test.
    CurrentAmmeter or clamp meterIn series. Choose the range so that the reading falls in the top third of the scale (see section 06).
    Active powerWattmeter or power analyzerAt no load the power factor is 0.1 to 0.3: use a low-power-factor wattmeter or an analyzer. An ordinary one hardly moves its pointer and reads badly.
    Three-phase powerTwo wattmeters (Aron connection)P=P1+P2; if one reads negative, it is subtracted. See three-phase circuits.
    ResistanceVoltmeter-ammeter method, bridge or micro-ohmmeterWith direct current and low current, so as not to heat the winding. Always note the temperature.
    InsulationMegohmmeterWith the machine disconnected and discharged; 500 V DC on low-voltage windings.
    SpeedOptical or contact tachometer, stroboscopeSlip comes from it: measure it together with the other quantities, not afterwards.
    TemperatureThermometer, thermocouple or thermal imaging cameraFix the measuring point; the frame temperature is not the winding temperature.

    How each instrument works inside and how it is connected is covered in analog instruments, digital instruments and AC measurements.

    Tests are safe only if they are set up properly

    No-load test of a transformer: if it is supplied from the low-voltage side, the high-voltage side is left at a raised voltage and unloaded (440 V in the example). Treat it as live.

    Short circuit: the voltage is low, but rated current flows. Tight terminals and conductors of adequate cross-section: a loose connection heats up and can catch fire.

    Locked rotor: lock the shaft with a firm mechanical device and keep the test to a few seconds; the winding heats quickly.

    Always start from zero voltage with a variable autotransformer, bring it back to zero before touching any connection, and verify the absence of voltage with an instrument. The legal framework in Argentina is Law 19,587 and Decree 351/79; the practical details are in occupational health and safety.

    02Transformer: no-load and short-circuit tests

    Both tests use the same instruments: a variable autotransformer, a voltmeter, an ammeter and a wattmeter. What changes is which winding is supplied and what is done with the other.

    Example · The parameters of a 5 kVA transformer

    Single-phase transformer, 5 kVA, 440/220 V, 50 Hz. No-load test from the low side: 220 V, 1.8 A, 60 W. Short-circuit test from the high side, at rated current: 22 V, 11.36 A, 110 W.

    2 · No load. cosφ0 =60220·1.8 =0.1515 (φ₀ = 81.3°). IFe=1.8·0.1515=0.273 A and Iμ=1.8·0.9885=1.779 A. Then RFe =220260 =806.7Ω and Xμ =2201.779 =123.6Ω. Referred to the high side they are multiplied by a2=22=4: 3227 Ω and 495 Ω.

    3 · Short circuit. Zsc =2211.36 =1.936Ω; Rsc =11011.362 =0.852Ω; Xsc =1.9362−0.8522 =1.739Ω. In percent: εsc =22440 =5.0%, εR=1105000=2.2% and εX =5.02−2.22 =4.49%.

    4 · Efficiency at cosφ=0.8. At full load: P2=5000·0.8=4000 W, losses 60+110=170 W and η=40004170=95.9%. At half load the copper losses are divided by four: 60+110·0.52=87.5 W, and η=20002087.5=95.8%. The maximum occurs when copper equals iron: 60110=0.74, that is, at 74 % of rated load.

    5 · Regulation at full load with cosφ=0.8 lagging: ε≈εRcosφ+εXsinφ =2.2·0.8+4.49·0.6 =4.45%. The secondary voltage drops from 220 V to about 210.2 V.

    6 · Short circuit at the terminals (with an ideal supply): I1nεsc =11.360.05 =227 A, twenty times rated. It is the figure used to choose the protection.

    Two details that change the result

    Resistance depends on temperature. Psc is measured with the machine stabilized and corrected to the reference temperature set by the standard (IEC 60076-1); with a cold winding the copper gives fewer losses than it will in service.

    The voltage must be sinusoidal. If the supply is distorted, the iron loses power differently and P0 is not the one that applies on the mains. Check the waveform with an oscilloscope before accepting the measurement.

    03Induction motor: no-load and locked-rotor tests

    The induction motor is a transformer whose secondary rotates. That is why it is tested the same way: at no load you see the excitation branch, and with the rotor locked (slip s=1, the equivalent of the short circuit) the leakage impedance. The difference is that the motor has mechanical losses that a transformer does not. The rotating field, slip and starting methods are in AC motors.

    No-load test

    Rated voltage, no load coupled, with the machine running for several minutes until the bearings settle. Voltage, current in the three phases and power are measured. The input power covers three things:

    P0 =PFe+Pmech+3I02R1

    iron, friction and windage, and stator copper. To separate iron from mechanical losses, the test is repeated lowering the voltage from 1.1Vn to about half, P0−3I02R1 is plotted against V2 and extrapolated to V=0: what remains does not depend on voltage and is the mechanical loss.

    Locked-rotor test

    Shaft locked and reduced voltage, raised from zero until the current is the rated one; quick reading, in a few seconds. With s=1 almost all the current goes through the series branch and the excitation branch can be neglected. Vlr, Ilr and Plr are measured; the stator resistance, R1, must be measured beforehand with direct current.

    Zlr=Vlr/3IlrRlr=Plr3Ilr2Xlr=Zlr2−Rlr2R2′=Rlr−R1 Per-phase values of the equivalent star, with Vlr the line voltage. Without other data, the leakage reactance is split in halves: X1≈X2′≈Xlr/2.
    Example · A 5.5 kW three-phase motor

    4-pole, 5.5 kW, 380 V, 50 Hz motor, with In=11.5 A and 1440 rpm on the nameplate. Stator resistance measured with DC: R1=1.05Ω per phase (of the equivalent star). No-load test at 380 V: 4.6 A and 340 W. Locked rotor: 65 V, 11.5 A and 650 W.

    1 · No load. S0=3·380·4.6=3028 VA and cosφ0 =3403028 =0.112: the 4.6 A current, 40 % of rated, is almost all magnetizing. Q0=30282−3402=3008 var and the no-load reactance is X0=38023008=48.0Ω (X1+Xm).

    2 · No-load losses. Stator copper: 3·4.62·1.05=66.7 W. That leaves 340−66.7=273.3 W of iron plus mechanical losses. The extrapolation to V=0 gave 110 W of friction and windage, so the iron loss is 273.3−110=163.3 W.

    3 · Locked rotor. Zlr =65/311.5 =3.263Ω; Rlr =6503·11.52 =1.638Ω; Xlr =3.2632−1.6382 =2.822Ω. Then R2′=1.638−1.05=0.588Ω and X1≈X2′≈1.41Ω.

    4 · Starting at full voltage (extrapolating linearly): Ist≈11.5·38065 =67.2 A, that is 5.8In. The torque comes from the power crossing the air gap, 3·11.52·0.588=233.4 W, at synchronous speed ωs =2π·150060 =157.1 rad/s: T=233.4157.1=1.49 N·m at 65 V, which at 380 V is multiplied by (380/65)2=34.2 to give 50.8 N·m. Rated torque is 55002π·1440/60=36.5 N·m, so the starting torque is 1.39Tn.

    What the test estimates and what it does not

    The starting current and torque calculated this way are estimates: at full voltage the leakage flux paths saturate and the real current turns out somewhat lower than the extrapolated one. In double-cage rotors, in addition, R2 and X2 change with the rotor frequency, and with the rotor locked that frequency is the mains frequency. To verify a start, measure it at service voltage with a current recorder.

    04Measurements on AC and DC motors

    Besides the laboratory tests, the technician measures the motor in service and out of service. This is the list of what is checked on an alternating-current machine and what each value says.

    MeasurementHow it is doneWhat it tells
    Nameplate and connectionRead voltage, current, speed, cosφ, insulation class, duty and terminal box.Whether the motor matches the supply (380 V star or 220 V delta) and the load. See the nameplate.
    Line voltageBetween the three phases, at the motor terminals and under load.Voltage drop in the feeder and voltage unbalance. A small voltage unbalance produces a current unbalance several times larger.
    Current in each phaseClamp meter on each conductor, in normal service.Overload (against In) and unbalance.
    Power and cosφTwo wattmeters or analyzer; P=3VIcosφ.Real motor load and power factor. See AC power.
    Winding resistanceBetween terminals, with DC, cold and with the temperature noted.Equal phases (within a few percent), open circuits or shorted turns, and the basis for the temperature-rise test.
    InsulationMegohmmeter between terminals and frame, with the motor disconnected.Moisture or varnish deterioration. The trend matters, not a single value; as a rough minimum, (kV+1) MΩ at 40 °C (IEEE 43).
    SpeedTachometer on the shaft.Slip s=ns−nns; with 1500 rpm synchronous and 1440 rpm, s=601500=4%.
    TemperatureThermal camera or thermometer on frame and bearings.Overload, blocked ventilation or worn bearings.

    Current unbalance is calculated as the largest deviation from the average, divided by the average. With the readings 7.4 – 7.6 – 9.8 A from the electrical maintenance topic, the average is 7.4+7.6+9.83=8.27 A and the maximum deviation 9.8−8.27=1.53 A, that is 1.538.27=18.5%: too much for a motor that must run healthy.

    DC machines

    On a DC machine some things are measured that an AC machine does not have: the armature with its commutator, the field winding and the back electromotive force.

    MeasurementHow it is doneWhat it tells
    Armature resistance RaVoltmeter-ammeter method with the shaft stationary, at four rotor positions.If the four readings differ, there are bad brushes, a dirty commutator or damaged bars.
    Field resistanceBetween the terminals of the excitation winding, with DC.Open circuits or shorted turns in the field.
    Back EMF ECalculated: E=V−IaRa in a motor, E=V+IaRa in a generator.It is proportional to speed and flux: E=kΦn.
    No-load characteristic (generator)At constant speed and no load, measure E while varying the field current If.The curve rises, bends because of saturation and does not return along the same path because of hysteresis.
    Speed regulation (motor)Measure n at no load and at full load with constant voltage.How much the speed drops when loaded.
    Example · Speed regulation of a shunt DC motor

    220 V shunt motor with Ra=0.8Ω. At full load it draws Ia=18 A and runs at 1500 rpm; at no load, Ia=1.5 A. With constant field, speed is proportional to the back EMF.

    At full load: E=220−18·0.8=205.6 V. At no load: E0=220−1.5·0.8=218.8 V. Then n0 =1500·218.8205.6 =1596 rpm and the regulation is 1596−15001500=6.4%. In practice it is somewhat lower, because armature reaction weakens the flux under load and partly compensates the drop.

    A DC motor must never lose its field while the armature is energized

    In a shunt or separately excited motor, if the field circuit is cut with voltage on the armature, the flux falls to almost zero and the speed rises without limit until the rotor destroys itself. Before measuring the field resistance, de-energize everything; and never open the excitation circuit while running.

    05Load curve and temperature-rise test

    The load test is the only one that measures efficiency, speed and power factor directly, and the one that shows the real temperature rise. The motor is loaded with a brake: a Prony brake (band or shoe with a scale), a generator feeding an electrical load, or an electromagnetic brake. Output power comes from torque and speed.

    Pu=Tω=T·2πn60η=PuP1s=ns−nns T in N·m, n in rpm, Pu in W. With a 0.25 m arm and a 100 N force, the torque is 25 N·m and, at 1440 rpm, Pu=25·150.8=3770 W.

    Simplified model built from the tests: constant losses of 273 W (iron plus friction), copper loss of 650 W at rated current proportional to the square of the load, reactive power growing the same way, and useful power equal to the fraction of 5.5 kW. Above 100 % the motor is overloaded; it is shown only to see the trend.

    Why a motor should not be oversized

    Efficiency is maximum when copper losses equal the constant ones: x=273.3650=0.65 of rated load. A motor running at 25 % has cosφ=0.48 and η=81.4%: the installation pays for reactive current and loss energy because a motor was bought «just in case». It is better to choose it to run between 60 % and 100 % of its rating.

    Temperature-rise test

    The machine is run at its rated load until thermal equilibrium —when the temperature stops rising, with a variation under 1 K per hour on the frame— and the temperature rise of the windings is measured. The temperature of a copper winding is obtained from its resistance, without putting sensors inside: copper changes its resistance with temperature in a known way.

    Δθ=R2−R1R1(235+t1)+t1−ta R1 is the cold resistance at temperature t1; R2, the hot resistance, measured as soon as the machine stops; ta, the ambient temperature at the end of the test. The constant 235 is the one for copper (225 for aluminum). The result is the temperature rise Δθ in kelvin.
    Insulation classMaximum temperaturePermissible rise (by resistance, 40 °C ambient)
    A105 °C60 K
    E120 °C75 K
    B130 °C80 K
    F155 °C105 K
    H180 °C125 K
    Example · Temperature rise by resistance

    A class F motor has, cold and at t1=20 °C, R1=2.10Ω between two terminals. After running at full load until equilibrium, R2=2.62Ω is measured (extrapolated to the instant of shutdown) at an ambient of ta=24 °C.

    Δθ =2.62−2.102.10·(235+20)+20−24 =0.2476·255−4 =59.1 K. The winding reached 20+63.1=83.1 °C; at a 40 °C ambient it would reach 40+59.1=99.1 °C. The class F limit is 105 K, so the motor complies, with a margin of almost 46 K.

    The winding cools quickly: measure right away

    When the machine stops the temperature falls, and so does the resistance. Measure it at 30 s and 1 min intervals for a few minutes and extrapolate the curve back to the instant of shutdown. If it is measured a couple of minutes late without extrapolating, the rise is underestimated. For every 10 K above the class limit, the life of the insulation is roughly halved (rule of thumb).

    In a transformer the temperature-rise test is done by the short-circuit method: it is supplied with a power equal to the total losses, P0+Psc, until the oil temperature stabilizes, and then rated current is applied to measure the winding temperature by resistance (IEC 60076-2).

    06Visual inspection and selection criteria

    Before connecting an instrument, look. Visual inspection takes minutes, costs nothing and finds defects that no electrical test detects, or that make the test dangerous (a broken frame, a low oil level). It is done with the machine disconnected and recorded on a checklist.

    How to choose a machine

    CriterionWhat is defined
    Power and dutyThe load power with a reasonable margin, and the duty (continuous S1, intermittent, frequent starts) according to IEC 60034-1.
    Voltage and frequencyThose of the supply. A 220/380 V motor is connected in delta on a 220 V supply and in star on a 380 V one; a 380/660 V motor, in delta at 380 V and in star at 660 V. Always as the nameplate indicates.
    SpeedPoles according to the required speed; with a drive, see variable-speed drives.
    Mechanical protectionIP rating (IEC 60529) according to dust, water and contact: a motor outdoors or in a washdown area needs IP55 or higher.
    Insulation and environmentInsulation class and ambient temperature; above 40 °C or 1000 m altitude the power must be derated.
    EfficiencyIE class (IEC 60034-30-1): a higher-efficiency motor costs more, but the difference is recovered over the running hours.
    MountingFoot or flange, horizontal or vertical shaft (IM codes of IEC 60034-7).

    07In the laboratory

    Practice 1 · The parameters of a single-phase transformer

    Materials: 100 to 500 VA transformer with two windings, variable autotransformer, voltmeter, ammeter, wattmeter (or power analyzer), multimeter for resistance, leads of adequate cross-section.

    Steps: 1) read the nameplate and calculate I1n and I2n; 2) measure the resistance of each winding with DC; 3) no-load test from the low side, raising the voltage slowly to rated, and note V0, I0, P0; 4) short-circuit test from the high side, closing the secondary through the ammeter and raising from zero to I1n; note Vsc and Psc and bring the voltage back to zero; 5) calculate cosφ0, RFe, Xμ, Zsc, Rsc, Xsc, εsc and the efficiency at 100 % and 50 %.

    Accepted if: Rsc matches R1+a2R2 measured with DC to within 15 % (corrected for temperature) and P0+Psc is less than 10 % of the rated power.

    Practice 2 · No-load and locked-rotor tests of a three-phase motor

    Materials: 0.37 to 1.5 kW motor, variable three-phase autotransformer, two wattmeters or analyzer, clamp meter, megohmmeter, mechanical device to lock the shaft.

    Steps: 1) visual inspection and insulation measurement; 2) stator resistance with DC; 3) no-load at rated voltage, running about 15 minutes; 4) no-load at six decreasing voltages, from 1.1Vn to about half; 5) with the shaft locked, raise the voltage from zero up to rated current and read in less than 10 s; 6) calculate parameters, mechanical and iron losses, starting current and torque.

    Accepted if: P0−3I02R1 against V2 gives a straight line, the three no-load currents differ by no more than 10 % from one another and the calculated starting current is 4 to 8 times rated.

    Practice 3 · Load curve with a brake

    Materials: three-phase motor with Prony brake or brake generator, tachometer, two wattmeters, clamp meter, scale.

    Steps: with the motor at rated voltage, load it to 25, 50, 75, 100 and 125 % of rated torque (do not hold 125 % for more than a few minutes). At each point note V, I, P1, the speed and the force on the scale; calculate Pu, η, cosφ and slip; plot against output power.

    Accepted if: efficiency has a maximum between 60 % and 90 % of load, cosφ grows with load and the full-load readings agree with the nameplate within the accuracy of the instruments.

    Practice 4 · Temperature rise by resistance

    Materials: motor with legible nameplate, micro-ohmmeter or bridge, thermometer, stopwatch.

    Steps: note R1 and t1 cold (the machine has been stopped for several hours); run it at full load until the frame varies by less than 1 K per hour; stop it and measure the resistance every 30 s for 5 minutes; extrapolate the curve back to the instant of shutdown to obtain R2; note ta; calculate Δθ.

    Accepted if: Δθ does not exceed the limit of the insulation class on the nameplate (table in section 05).

    Practice 5 · DC motor

    Materials: separately excited or shunt DC motor, adjustable DC supply, ammeters, voltmeters, tachometer.

    Steps: measure Ra with the shaft stationary at four positions; supply rated voltage with the field connected and measure n and Ia at no load; load up to rated current and repeat; calculate E=V−IaRa in each case and the speed regulation.

    Accepted if: the four Ra readings differ by no more than 10 % and the speed calculated from E agrees with the measured one within 5 %.

    08Common mistakes

    MistakeConsequence
    Short-circuit testing the transformer at rated voltageA current tens of times rated flows (in the example, 20) and the winding burns in seconds. Always raise the voltage from zero.
    Using an ordinary wattmeter in the no-load testAt cosφ of 0.1 the pointer barely moves and the relative error is huge. Use a low-power-factor one or an analyzer.
    Taking the motor's P0 as iron lossIt includes friction and windage and the stator copper; they must be subtracted.
    Mixing the transformer sidesValues measured on the low and high sides cannot be added: they must be referred to the same side by multiplying by a2.
    Keeping the rotor locked too longRated current in an unventilated rotor heats the winding very quickly; results change and the motor is damaged.
    Measuring winding resistance without noting the temperatureThe value is useless for comparison or for calculating the temperature rise.
    Measuring hot resistance several minutes after stopping the machineThe winding has already cooled: the rise is underestimated. Extrapolate to the instant of shutdown.
    Testing with an unbalanced or distorted supplyCurrents and losses are no longer representative; a supply problem is mistaken for a motor defect.
    Opening the field of a running DC motorRunaway: speed rises until the rotor is destroyed.

    09Self-assessment

    What is obtained from each of the two transformer tests?

    The no-load test, at rated voltage, gives the iron losses and the parameters of the excitation branch (RFe, Xμ). The short-circuit test, at rated current and reduced voltage, gives the copper losses and the leakage impedance (Rsc, Xsc).

    Why does the short-circuit test not destroy the transformer?

    Because it is done with reduced voltage, a few percent of rated (5 % in the example), just enough for rated current to flow. At rated voltage the current would be about 20 times larger.

    A no-load test reads 220 V, 1.5 A and 45 W. Calculate cosφ0 and RFe.

    cosφ0 =45220·1.5 =0.136. RFe =220245 =1076Ω (referred to the side where it was measured).

    A 380 V three-phase motor at no load draws 4.6 A and 340 W. What is its power factor, and why is it so low?

    cosφ0 =3403·380·4.6 =0.112. Almost all the no-load current is magnetizing (reactive): active power only covers losses, and there is no load to absorb more.

    How is the starting current estimated from the locked-rotor test?

    The current is measured at reduced voltage and scaled linearly with voltage: Ist≈Ilr·VnVlr =11.5·38065 =67 A. It is an estimate somewhat higher than the real one, because of saturation.

    A winding's resistance goes from 1.00 to 1.25 Ω. With t1=ta=20 °C, what is the temperature rise?

    Δθ =1.25−1.001.00·(235+20)+20−20 =0.25·255 =63.75 K: it exceeds the limit of class A (60 K) and complies with those of classes E (75 K), B (80 K), F (105 K) and H (125 K).

    Why does an oversized motor perform poorly?

    Because at low load its power factor and its efficiency fall. The example motor, at 25 % load, has cosφ=0.48 and η=81.4%, against 0.85 and 85.6 % at full load.
