    The Certificate of Suitable Electrical Installation and the technical file

    An installer can do flawless work and still be unable to get a house connected: without the certificate, the utility will not supply it. This page explains what the Certificate of Suitable Electrical Installation is, how to choose the right type, what is verified in each case, and how the technical file that backs it up is put together, with the complete worked example of the ERSeP model house.

    Suitable-installation certificate Law 10.281 ERSeP RG 04/2023 Technical file Category III
    What is taken for granted

    Who may sign the certificate, in which category and with what liability is in the Licensed Electrical Installer. The general framework (law, decree, AEA 90364, regulators) is in regulations for electrical installations; electrification grades and the circuits of a dwelling, in dwelling electrical design; residual-current devices and earthing schemes, in electrical protection. Here the subject is the document: what it says, what backs it up and how what it declares is calculated.

    01What the certificate is and what it includes

    Law 10.281 requires every user electrical installation, public or private, to have a Certificate of Suitable Electrical Installation in order to obtain the service. It is required of new installations and also of those that predate the law when service is reconnected. It is issued by a Licensed Electrical Installer whose scope of practice matches the type, voltage and power of the installation, and the user presents it to the utility when requesting connection or reconnection.

    A public document with the standing of a sworn statement

    Whoever signs declares that the installation meets what it says it meets. That is why the certificate is also the installer's tool for showing what was certified if an accident happens: if the work is well done, the supporting files, complete and truthful, prove it. Liabilities are administrative (before ERSeP), civil and criminal (before the ordinary courts); the details are on the page about the licensed installer.

    Nobody can grant a deadline to comply

    Neither the installer nor the utility may grant a deadline to bring an installation into compliance, or alter the conditions set by the regulations. If the installation is not suitable, it is not certified as suitable until it has been corrected. And on the form only the items actually verified are ticked: if one is missing, the certificate is issued as “not suitable.” There is no middle ground.

    The certificate is not just a form: it is a set of six parts. The implementing decree requires it to contain the description of the installation as executed, modified or declared, its sketch, the photographs and the detail of the materials, and to be issued in triplicate: the original is presented by the applicant to the utility, the duplicate stays with the applicant and the triplicate with the installer. Today the documentation is uploaded digitally on the ERSeP platform, and only .jpg or .pdf files are accepted.

    How the form is completed

    StepWhat is entered

    The power is the installation's, not what the customer asks for

    The declared power is that of the certified installation, complete and calculated, even if the customer asks the utility for something else. It also decides whether the installer has the scope of practice: Category III reaches 10 kW at low voltage.

    “Existing under resolution 54”

    If the installation never had supply but was built, or construction started, before 01/12/2017, it is also existing, but the installer must prove that date in the certificate itself, which is issued as “existing under resolution 54” (ERSeP RG 54/2018). The definition is the same as in RG 04/2023; what changes is the duty to prove it.

    Certification of an existing installation is required at the first reconnection and afterwards each time service is reconnected more than two years after the previous certification.

    Chapters Category III cannot certify

    Chapter IV (larger existing installations or those with special characteristics, street lighting and signalling) and chapter VI (permanent connection without metering) are within the scope of categories I and II only. If the request falls there, the job is not signed, even if the power is small.

    03What is verified: new and existing installations

    New installation (chapter II). It must meet the AEA regulation in force for the type of installation; for a small single-family dwelling that is AEA 90364-7-770. If, because of its characteristics, there is no specific AEA regulation, ERSeP sets the conditions at the installer's request. Materials must comply with the applicable IRAM or IEC standards and be certified under Resolution MP-SC 171/2016 when required. On how a standardized material is recognized (IRAM mark, QR code), see the licensed installer and electrical materials.

    Existing installation (chapter III). It applies to single-family dwellings and small commercial or industrial installations, at low voltage and up to 10 kW. Since the installation already exists and is not going to be redone, the decree provides that the certificate is issued upon the mere verification of the minimum safety conditions. There are ten:

    No.Requirement (RG 04/2023, ch. III)How it is checked
    1Insulated user main board, with double insulation.Enclosure of insulating material, no accessible sheet metal.
    2Ingress protection (IP) suited to the place. Minimums: IP 41 indoors, IP 54 outdoors (IRAM 2444).Enclosure marking and condition of closures and seals.
    3Access to live parts restricted.Covers, faceplates and shutters; no terminal in view.
    4TT protective earthing system, according to the AEA regulation in force and IRAM 2281-2 and 2281-3.Earth rod with inspection box; resistance is measured.
    5Equipotential bonding: every conductive part that is normally not live and could become live in a fault is connected to the protective earth.Continuity of the protective conductor to outlets and exposed parts.
    6In the TPU and in each sub-board, one circuit breaker per line or circuit, breaking phase and neutral.One double-pole MCB per circuit; no loose single-pole breakers.
    7Residual-current protection of 30 mA or less, according to the board structure (see below).RCD present, with legible sensitivity and its test button.

    10Raceways, conductors, sockets and outlets properly installed; sockets in good condition and with three flat pins (IRAM 2071); closed outlets; no exposed cables.Visual inspection of every socket and outlet.

    The RCD according to the board structure. If there is only a main board, all circuits are protected by a residual-current device of 30 mA or less, together with overload and short-circuit protection. If there is a main board and sub-boards, and the main board is outside the user's property, the main board carries the appropriate RCD together with the protection of each sub-feeder, and each sub-board protects all its circuits with an RCD of 30 mA or less. If the supply cannot meet that arrangement, the whole installation is protected with a 30 mA-or-less RCD.

    IP 41, not IP 44

    The ERSeP course material contains examples that say “IP 44 for indoors.” The minimum set by the resolution is IP 41 indoors and IP 54 outdoors. An IP 44 board indoors complies (it exceeds the minimum), but an existing installation is not required to replace an IP 41 board in good condition with an IP 44 one: the checklist is built with the minimum stated in the resolution.

    The power of an existing installation

    An existing installation may have been built under AEA 90364-7-770 or without following it. In the second case, power is calculated with the procedure of Annex IV of ERSeP RG 17/2021:

    1. Total apparent power: 25 VA per lighting outlet, 240 VA per socket outlet and, for any equipment permanently located at an outlet (direct connection or through a socket) whose power is 500 VA or more, its actual power.
    2. Maximum apparent power: a minimum coefficient of 0.8 may be applied to that total.
    3. Maximum power to declare: a power factor of not less than 0.85 is also considered.
    Stot=25·nL+240·nT+Seq,Smax=0.8·Stot,P=0.85·Smax

    Here nL is the number of lighting outlets, nT the number of socket outlets and Seq the sum of the actual power of fixed equipment of 500 VA or more. The result is a minimum reference value: if another method gives more, the other is used. And if the existing installation was built under AEA 90364-7-770, loads are calculated with that regulation: 25 VA per outlet does not apply in that case.

    CircuitAnnex IV (existing, not built under 770)AEA 90364-7-770 (existing, built under 770)
    General lighting (IUG)25 VA per outlet.2/3 of the sum of the outlets at 60 VA each (without derived sockets).
    General-use sockets (TUG)240 VA per socket outlet.2,200 VA per circuit.
    Specific-use sockets (TUE)The actual power of the equipment if 500 VA or more.3,300 VA per circuit.
    Example A · A tenant asks for the “apto” and the installation complies

    A house built in 2019 has just been rented. It has the minimum electrification grade: 6 lighting outlets on a 10 A curve-B double-pole MCB and 15 sockets on another 16 A curve-C double-pole MCB. All sockets are three-flat-pin. The service entrance is double-insulated and there is a single IP 44 board in perfect condition inside the premises (the building line coincides with the property line), with a 25 A double-pole main MCB and a 2×25 A, 30 mA RCD. The conductors are standardized and of adequate cross-section; the green-yellow protective conductor (IRAM NM 247-3) and its earthing are present.

    Verification. 1) insulating (PVC) board: complies; 2) IP 44 ≥ IP 41: complies; 3) access to live parts restricted: complies (the double-insulated service entrance is stricter than required); 4) TT system with rod and inspection box: complies; 5) protective conductor standardized, adequately sized and green-yellow: complies; 6) double-pole MCBs per circuit: complies; 7) 30 mA RCD: complies; 8) no fuses: complies; 9) standardized materials: complies; 10) three-flat-pin IRAM 2071 sockets and raceways in good condition: complies. All ten points are met: the file can be put together and the “existing” certificate issued.

    Power. The installation was built under 770, so it is calculated under 770: a lighting circuit without derived sockets takes 2/3 of the sum of its outlets at 60 VA.

    SIUG=23·6·60VA=240VA

    The 2,200 VA socket circuit is added, and the result is converted to active power with a power factor of 0.85:

    S=240VA+2200VA=2440VA→P=0.85·2440VA=2074W≈2.07kW

    That is 2.07 kW, far below the 10 kW of Category III. (No simultaneity coefficient is applied here; it would reduce the value: this is the worst case.) The tenant also wants to add a socket circuit. With that third circuit of 2,200 VA:

    S=240VA+2200VA+2200VA=4640VA→P=0.85·4640VA=3944W≈3.94kW

    The current would be 4,640 VA / 220 V = 21.1 A, still below the 25 A of the main MCB and the RCD. Note: chapter II of RG 04/2023 includes “modifications or extensions of existing installations”; the new circuit is therefore built to the AEA regulation in force and not to the ten minimums.

    Example B · The same request, and the installation does not comply

    A house from 2010, also rented. The service entrance is galvanized pipe, and behind it there is a sheet-metal board with 80 A screw-in fuses. Inside there is a mixed circuit of 6 lighting outlets and 15 sockets, protected by two 16 A single-pole MCBs (one on the phase, one on the neutral), and at the head a 2×25 A, 30 mA RCD. The breakers are in a sheet-metal board inside the house. The sockets are “biuso” (accepting round or flat pins) and the earth rod is joined to the outlets with bare cable.

    What has to be corrected before certifying:

    1. Sheet-metal board: replace with one of insulating material, with the right IP (at least 41 indoors).
    2. Metal service-entrance pipe: it must have its own service earthing (rod with standardized cable), not connected to the user's protective earthing.
    3. Bare protective conductor: replace with green-yellow IRAM NM 247-3 cable, 2.5 mm².
    4. Check that the user's earth rod has its inspection box and connections according to AEA 90364-7-770 (the TT system, present only in the user's installation, is correct).
    5. Two single-pole MCBs per circuit: replace with one double-pole MCB that breaks phase and neutral.
    6. Head-of-installation fuses: replace with a double-pole MCB that protects the conductors of that stretch.
    7. “Biuso” sockets: replace with three-flat-pin IRAM 2071 sockets.

    The 30 mA RCD is already there and is kept. Once everything is corrected, the existing-type file is put together. Power. This installation does not follow 770, so Annex IV applies, with 6 lighting outlets and 15 sockets:

    Stot=6·25VA+15·240VA=150VA+3600VA=3750VA
    Smax=0.8·3750VA=3000VA→P=0.85·3000VA=2550W=2.55kW

    By another route, multiplying both coefficients at once (0.8 · 0.85 = 0.68):

    P=0.8·0.85·3750VA=0.68·3750VA=2550W

    The declared power is 2.55 kW. The 80 A head fuses say nothing about power: what is declared is the calculated value. And the design current, 3,000 VA / 220 V = 13.6 A, fits a 16 A MCB.

    04The connection and metering point

    Under ERSeP General Resolution 08, the certificate issued by every installer, of any category, includes the connection and metering point of the certified installation: it is attested that its materials, equipment and execution meet the applicable technical specifications, verifiable before the utility grants service. The certification is made within the same form. The utility keeps its own control tasks all the same.

    One number, two years

    The course material cites this resolution as “08/2018”; the ERSeP form names it “08/2017,” and the download link also points to 2017. It is the same general resolution: cite it by its number, and confirm the year on the ERSeP website.

    RuleWhat it establishes
    EPEC ET-21 (ERSeP RG 06/2009)Technical specification for the connection and metering points of low-voltage customers. RG 06/2009 approved its modification; it is the latest version issued.
    ERSeP RG 11/2018For every new connection point, in addition to ET-21: meter boxes and protection-board boxes of insulating, self-extinguishing synthetic material; enclosures and raceways of self-extinguishing synthetic material or insulated with it; steel pipes, where they apply, insulated inside and outside (double insulation); and the earthing of the connection and metering point is dispensed with.
    ERSeP RG 84/2018Points built, or started, before that requirement took effect: the 2009 ET-21 applies and the above is not mandatory, but the installer states it in the certificate. If something is damaged and must be replaced or repaired, it is done to the new criteria.
    ERSeP RG 08 (arts. 5 and 6)The connection point of grouped supplies (collective metering systems of condominium buildings) is certified by an installer of a category matching the maximum power of the whole set.

    What this means in practice. In a new installation or one with temporary supply, the service entrance is made with double insulation and no earth rod. In an existing one with metal components (pipe or meter box), those components are connected to their own service earthing, and under no circumstances is it joined to the user's protective earthing (RG 11/2018, art. 3). When any component is replaced, the replacement already follows the double-insulation criterion. A Category III installer cannot certify connection points of grouped supplies.

    Upstream of the meter, do not touch

    Before the meter the line is always live. Any work on the meter, its seals or the utility's service entrance is done or authorized by its staff. For safety practices in general, see occupational health and safety.

    Temporary supply (chapter V)

    For building sites, fairs, circuses and similar, the certificate is presented as a condition for the utility to grant the supply, and is renewed at each periodic review. It must always include the user installation and the connection and metering point. Besides the conditions of existing installations (double insulation, IP 41 indoors and 54 outdoors, restricted access, TT, equipotential bonding, MCB per circuit, 30 mA RCD, standardized materials), it requires:

    BoardsThe TPU in a different enclosure from the TPA, unless the TPA meets the same requirements.
    Portable installationsSub-boards as close as possible to the loads and in restricted-access areas.
    Link cablesInsulated copper, sheathed, rated 1.1 kV. Stretches on the floor are mechanically protected (PVC tubes, concrete slabs) and do not interfere with circulation.

    05The technical file: description, single-line diagram and plan

    The technical file is the set of documents that accompany the form. ERSeP publishes a model technical file (a new single-family dwelling, executed by the same installer who writes the description) that serves as a guide; its calculations are those of that property and must be redone with the real installation.

    The written description

    It describes what was executed, in technical vocabulary

    The description covers an installation that already exists: it is written in the past tense (“was installed,” “was measured”), not in the future (“a board will be installed…”), which is the tense of a project description. And it uses the technical name: “thermal-magnetic circuit breaker (MCB)” and not “the thermal.” A power-calculation sheet, a circuit sheet or a sheet of outlets per circuit is not a description: they may be attached, but they are not enough. Nor is the plan or the diagram copied; what they cannot say is written.

    Chapter of the model descriptionWhat it contains
    01 · Calculations and sizingLocation; area and electrification grade; number of circuits and maximum simultaneous demand (DPMS); final circuits; raceways; protective devices; summary table.
    02 · Description of the workPreliminary work, boards and their verification, earthing, connection and metering point, feeding of boards, switching and protective devices, protective conductor.
    03 · TestsVisual inspection, checking of materials, earthing with its connections, socket polarity; for boards, mechanical operation and selectivity.
    04 · Technical specificationsCables, protective and switching devices, raceways, boards, earth rod, boxes, switches and sockets, each with its standard.
    05 · Other observationsPolarity, colors, connectors in the boxes, mounting heights, absence of “biuso” sockets and daisy-chain connections, general condition.

    The single-line diagram

    It represents the boards and what they contain: only the protective devices, their associated cables and the earthing, starting at the service entrance. The TPA is located less than 2 m from the service entrance; if the house has its front door on the property line (the building line coincides with it), the same board serves as TPA and TPU. The thermal-magnetic breaker protects the installation against overloads and short circuits, not people; the RCD protects people and animals from earth leakage, not the installation. The data each carries:

    MCB (IEC 60898)

    Number of poles; standardized rated current (2, 4, 6, 10, 16, 20, 25, 32, 40, 50 or 63 A); trip curve (B, C or D); breaking capacity (3,000 A, 4.5 kA, 6 kA or 10 kA).

    RCD (IEC 61008/9)

    Number of poles and type (AC, A or B); standardized rated current (25, 40 or 63 A); sensitivity of 30 mA. Its rated current is chosen equal to or the next value above the sum of the currents of the MCBs it protects.

    Each protective device also states the quantity, cross-section and type of its associated cable (for example, IRAM 247-3 · 2×2.5 mm²), and what it feeds (IUG, TUG…). It is drawn with standardized symbols (IRAM 2010: part I covers electrotechnical symbols and part III those for connection and protection).

    The floor plan

    It is drawn on a standardized sheet format (IRAM 4504) and dimensioned per IRAM 4513. It must show the location on the lot with setbacks and the property line; the connection and metering point; the TPA and TPU boards; all outlets with their height, each linked to its circuit with the cable and raceway of each stretch (for example, 2×2.5 mm² + PE, pR Ø 20); the earthing; and all circuits identified (IUG 1, TUG 2…). If no standardized symbol exists for an element, one is created and described in the legend. Drawing technique is covered in technical drawing and CAD.

    New single-family dwelling of 72.42 m² (living room, dining room, two bedrooms, bathroom, kitchen, service room and patio), at Av. Emilio Olmos 5130, Córdoba.     The model file does not match its own table

    The text of the model file says 7,080 VA, 5,664 VA, 25.75 A and 4.8 kW. They come from adding 2,680 VA of lighting, where circuit 2 is taken at its 480 VA without applying the 0.66. The summary table of the same document gives 6,916.8 VA, 5,533 VA and 4.70 kW: those are the correct values of the calculation and the ones used here. A real file must be consistent from end to end: text, table and single-line diagram. The model file's diagram also labels all four outputs as curve C, while the description asks for curve B on circuits 1 and 2; the description is followed here.

    One board, three IPs in the same file

    The model file sets IP 41 for the TPU and IP 43 for the TPA “according to ET-21,” but its materials list calls for an IP 65 watertight enclosure for the TPA. Whoever assembles a file checks that what the description says is the same as what is listed and photographed.

    The photographs

    • The before of the intervention and the detail of what was done: if a “biuso” socket was replaced with a three-flat-pin one, a photo before, one after and one of the back showing the line, neutral and earth connections.
    • In new service entrances, the whole installation, where it can be seen that the materials are double-insulated and that the meter box is class 2.
    • The TPA and TPU boards: the protection rating marking (IP 54, IP 44), the inside and the board closed.
    • The splices used and the installation in general.

    The measurement protocol

    Verification of the installation ends in a protocol: a table with each test, the instrument, the measured value and the limit.     07In the workshop

    Before surveying

    Surveying an installation means opening boards and touching conductors. Work with the installation de-energized, with lockout, tagging and verification of absence of voltage with an instrument, as explained in occupational health and safety. Tests that require voltage are done by the teacher or under direct supervision.

    Practice 1 · Survey an existing installation

    Material: the classroom installation or an authorized dwelling, the list of the ten minimum points, flashlight, camera and form. Steps: go through the ten points and note “complies / does not comply / could not be verified” with a supporting photo; list what would have to be corrected and decide suitable or not suitable. Acceptance: the ten points with their status and evidence; a consistent decision (a single “does not comply” is enough for “not suitable”).

    Practice 2 · Power by Annex IV and by 770

    Material: the plan of a dwelling with its outlets and a calculator. Steps: count lighting outlets and sockets; calculate the power with Annex IV (25 VA, 240 VA, 0.8 and 0.85) and with AEA 90364-7-770 (2/3 of outlets at 60 VA, 2,200 VA per TUG), and express both in kW. Acceptance: both powers by two routes each, with the comparison and the check that they do not exceed 10 kW.

    Practice 3 · Write the written description

    Material: an installation built in the workshop, its single-line diagram and plan. Steps: write a description of one to two pages, in the past tense and in technical vocabulary, with the standard of each material (IEC 60898, IRAM 2071, IRAM 2309…). Acceptance: no “will be installed”; no colloquial word; an outside reader can reconstruct the installation without seeing the plan.

    Practice 4 · Single-line diagram and plan with IRAM symbols

    Material: the same workshop board and paper or CAD. Steps: draw the single-line diagram with poles, rated current, curve and breaking capacity of each MCB, and the RCD with type and sensitivity; draw the plan with boards, outlets with heights, identified circuits and earthing. Acceptance: all the data in the list of section 05 and the IRAM 2010 symbols, with a legend.

    08Common mistakes

    MistakeConsequence
    Giving a deadline “to fix it” and certifying anywayA false sworn statement is signed. If it does not comply, the certificate is “not suitable” until corrected.
    Choosing the certificate type from memoryThe wrong checklist is verified. A permanent installation without a meter or a larger existing one belongs to chapter VI or IV, which Category III does not certify.
    Declaring the power the customer asks forThe power is the calculated one of the installation; it can take the job outside the scope of practice.
    Calculating a 770-built existing installation with “25 VA per outlet”If it was built under 770, loads are calculated under 770 (2/3 of 60 VA, 2,200 VA per TUG…).
    Leaving screw-in fuses or single-pole MCBs on the neutralFails points 6 and 8: they are replaced with a double-pole MCB.
    Accepting “biuso” socketsFails point 10: they must be three-flat-pin IRAM 2071.
    Joining service earthing with protective earthingIt is prohibited (RG 11/2018, art. 3).
    Writing the description in the future tense or copying the project'sWhat is executed is certified, not what is planned. A calculation sheet is not a description.
    Listing only the materials the installer suppliedThe list is that of the whole installation.
    Text, table and single-line diagram saying different thingsThe file loses value as evidence, exactly when it is needed most.

    09Self-assessment

    Who issues the certificate and to whom is it presented?

    It is issued by a Licensed Electrical Installer whose scope matches the type, voltage and power. The user presents it to the utility when requesting connection or reconnection of service.

    An installation has a single item that does not comply. Can it be certified as suitable with a deadline to fix it?

    No. Neither the installer nor the utility may grant deadlines. It is certified as “not suitable,” or corrected first and certified afterwards.

    A house never had electricity, but its construction was finished in 2016. What type of certificate applies?

    Existing, because it was built before 01/12/2017. Since it never had supply, the installer must prove that date in the certificate, which is issued as “existing under resolution 54.”

    Name five of the ten minimum requirements of an existing installation.

    For example: insulated main board; IP 41 indoors or IP 54 outdoors; TT system; one double-pole MCB per circuit; RCD of 30 mA or less; no fuses; three-flat-pin IRAM 2071 sockets; closed outlets and no exposed cables.

    An existing installation (not built under 770) has 8 lighting outlets and 10 sockets. What power is declared?

    By Annex IV:

    Stot=8·25+10·240=2600VA→P=0.85·0.8·2600VA=1768W≈1.77kW

    By another route: 0.68 · 2,600 = 1,768 W. That is 1.77 kW.

    In a new service entrance, does the connection and metering point have an earth rod? And in an existing one with a metal pipe?

    In the new one, no: it is double-insulated and its earthing is dispensed with (RG 11/2018). In the existing one with metal parts, yes: its own service earthing, never joined to the user's protective earthing.

    In the model house, why is the TPA MCB 32 A and not 25 A?

    Because the design current is 25.15 A: a 25 A MCB would be below it. The next standardized value, 32 A, is taken, and it is checked that the 6 mm² cable (36 A) supports it: 25.15 ≤ 32 ≤ 36.

    What is wrong with this sentence of a description: “A board will be installed and a 16 A thermal will be fitted”?

    It is in the future tense: what was executed is certified (“was installed”). And it uses a colloquial name: it should read “16 A, curve-C double-pole thermal-magnetic circuit breaker (MCB), IEC 60898.”
