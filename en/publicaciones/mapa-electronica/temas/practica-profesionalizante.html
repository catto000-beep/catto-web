    Professional internship (workplace practice)

    This is the stage where the work stops being an exercise: someone is waiting for a result, there is a deadline that does not move and there are real consequences if something goes wrong. At 240 hours, it is the longest course component of the seventh year, and the one that most resembles what comes afterwards.

    Professional profile Agreement Logbook Responsibility

    01What sets it apart

    In the school workshopIn the professional internship
    The problem is scoped by the teacher.The problem comes as it comes, with missing data and conditions nobody explained.
    If it goes wrong, you start over.If it goes wrong, someone loses time, money or service.
    The materials are there.You have to order them, wait for them or replace them with whatever is available.
    The deadline is flexible.The deadline is set by production, not by learning.
    You work with classmates at the same level.You work with people of much more experience and from other specialties.
    The technical result is assessed.Punctuality, safety, manners and record-keeping are assessed as well.
    FormatWhat it consists of
    Work placementThe student joins a company or agency with an agreed activity plan and a tutor on each side.
    Project for a third partyA concrete job commissioned by an institution, carried out at school with follow-up from the client.
    Community serviceRepair of equipment, installations or technical assistance for schools, clubs or institutions in the area.
    Simulated companyThe full operation of an organization is recreated, with roles, budgets, purchasing and deliveries.
    Applied researchA technical problem posed by a third party, approached with an experimental method and a documented result.

    02Before starting

    What must be settled
    • A signed agreement between the school and the organization, setting out the responsibilities of each party.
    • Valid insurance covering the student during the activity and while traveling.
    • An agreed activity plan: what the student will do, what not, and what is expected to be learned.
    • A tutor at the organization and a responsible teacher at the school, both identified and in contact.
    • Personal protective equipment provided and suitable for the task.
    • Prior training in the specific risks of the place.
    What the internship is not
    • It is not free labor. It is a formative stage with educational objectives.
    • It does not replace a worker or fill a vacant position.
    • It does not include unplanned hazardous tasks or hours outside what was agreed.
    • It is not done without supervision: someone is always responsible for what is being done.

    If the internship drifts toward any of this at the workplace, you should raise it with the responsible teacher. Learning to do so is part of the training.

    03During the internship

    AspectWhat is expected
    Punctuality and attendanceArrive on time and give advance notice if you cannot attend. It is the first thing assessed and the easiest to meet.
    SafetyFollow all the procedures, even if everyone else skips them. If something seems unsafe, ask before doing it.
    Asking questionsAsking when you do not know is not a weakness: it is what is expected. What is not accepted is doing something wrong for not asking.
    Initiative within limitsVolunteering for tasks, yes. Touching equipment or changing configurations without authorization, no.
    ConfidentialityWhat you see inside an organization is not discussed outside it or published.
    Care for what belongs to othersTools and instruments that are not your own, and that others will need the next day.
    Record-keepingWrite in the logbook the same day: what was done, what was learned, what is still pending and what questions came up.
    The logbook, which seems like a formality and is not

    It is the only thing that later makes it possible to reconstruct what was done over 240 hours. Without it, the final report is written from memory and ends up generic. With it, you can show the progression: what took a day in the first week takes an hour a month later.

    A useful entry has a date, the task, what was measured or observed, what decision was made and what was left unresolved. Written the same day, it takes five minutes.

    04The capabilities brought into play

    Profile capabilityHow it shows up in the internship
    Interpreting technical documentationReading someone else’s drawing, a datasheet in another language or a service manual, and understanding what it says.
    Assembling and installingCarrying out work to specification, neatly and respecting the logic of the existing installation.
    Measuring and testingChoosing the instrument, measuring properly and recording the result with its unit and its uncertainty.
    DiagnosingStarting from the symptom, forming hypotheses and ruling them out with measurements, instead of swapping parts.
    MaintainingCarrying out preventive routines, detecting deviations and keeping a record.
    Working safelyRecognizing risks, applying procedures and stopping the task when something is not safe.
    CommunicatingClearly reporting the status of a job, in writing and verbally, to someone who was not there.
    Working with othersCoordinating, asking for help, accepting a correction and giving one on time.
    What surprises returning students most

    That the technical part is almost never the problem. What is hard is everything else: understanding what the person asking really wants, warning in time that something is going to be late, writing a report that can be understood, living with someone else’s work rhythm and knowing when to stop and ask.

    Those are, precisely, the capabilities the internship is there to develop. And they are what later sets a technician apart from someone who knows electronics.

    05Wrap-up and what comes next

    The final report
    • The organization: what it does, how it is organized, what place the area where you worked occupies.
    • The tasks carried out, with their method and concrete results.
    • The problems encountered and how they were solved. This is the most valuable part.
    • What was learned, including what is not technical.
    • A well-founded improvement proposal, if one was identified.
    • Photographic record and the complete logbook as an appendix.
    From trade to profession
    • Responsibility: signed work binds whoever did it. A poorly done installation has consequences, and also someone responsible.
    • Licensing and registration: certain jobs require a qualifying degree and registration with the corresponding professional association.
    • Continuing education: technology changes. What was learned at school is the foundation for continuing to learn, not the end point.
    • Work ethics: not charging for what was not done, not promising what cannot be delivered, and saying “I don’t know how to do this” when appropriate.
    A practical recommendation

    Keep everything: photos of the work done, measurements, drawn diagrams, the final report. It is the start of a portfolio, and it is what serves to show what you are capable of when there is no work history yet. It is worth much more than any description written on a resume.

    06Common mistakes

    MistakeConsequence
    Not giving notice that you will not attendIt leaves the worst impression, and it is the easiest to avoid.
    Doing something wrong for not askingTime is lost, equipment can be damaged and trust is lost.
    Touching equipment without authorizationIt can stop production or put someone at risk.
    Skipping a safety procedure “because everyone does”The risk is real even if nobody follows the procedure, and the responsibility is your own.
    Filling in the logbook at the endThe detail is lost and the report ends up generic and useless.
    Discussing outside what you saw insideIt breaks confidentiality and closes the door on those who come after.
    Waiting to be given a taskThe opportunity is wasted. Volunteering and asking what needs to be done changes the whole experience.
    Not asking for feedback at the endYou lose the most valuable thing: how an outsider saw it.

    07Self-assessment

    What distinguishes the professional internship from the school workshop?

    That there is a real client, a deadline that does not move and concrete consequences if something goes wrong. The problem also arrives with missing data, as in real work.

    Name five possible formats of the internship.

    Work placement at an organization, a project commissioned by a third party, community service, a simulated company or applied research.

    What must be settled before starting?

    A signed agreement, valid insurance, an agreed activity plan, a tutor at the organization and a responsible teacher, protective equipment and prior training in the risks of the place.

    Can the internship replace a worker?

    No. It is a formative stage, with educational objectives and supervision. It does not fill a vacant position and it is not free labor.

    What is the expected progression of autonomy?

    Observe, then assist, then work under supervision, and finally work and answer for what was done.

    Why is the logbook written the same day?

    Because the detail is lost afterwards. Written at the time it takes five minutes and it is what makes it possible to reconstruct 240 hours of work and show the progression.

    What do you do about an unsafe procedure that everyone skips?

    Do not do it, ask, and if necessary raise it with the responsible teacher. The risk is real even if nobody follows the procedure, and the responsibility for yourself is your own.

    What is the most valuable part of the final report?

    The problems encountered and how they were solved: it is what shows technical judgment, more than the list of tasks.

    What tends to be harder than the technical part?

    Understanding what the person asking really wants, warning in time about a delay, writing a clear report, adapting to someone else’s rhythm and knowing when to stop and ask.

    Why keep a record of what was done?

    It is the start of a portfolio: photos, measurements, diagrams and reports. It serves to show what you are capable of when there is no work history yet.
